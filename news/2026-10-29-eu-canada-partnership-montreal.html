"Could Canada Become Europe's First Friend Outside Europe?"

By Our World Published at 14:30 IST
Reading options

What Could Canada’s New Friendship With Europe Look Like?

1. A Big Meeting in Montreal

In late October 2026, leaders from the European Union (EU) and Canada met in Montreal for a friendly summit. At the meeting, Ursula von der Leyen — who leads the European Commission — shared an exciting idea: Canada could become the EU’s very first “associate member.” That would make Canada special — the first country outside of Europe to grow this close to the EU family.

Canada is already a good friend of Europe. They already have a huge trade deal called CETA, which helps them buy and sell things to each other without paying big extra fees. But this new idea would go even further.

2. What Does “Associate Member” Mean?

Right now, the EU is made up of 27 European countries that are all full members. An “associate member” would be a non-European country that works very closely with the EU on big issues — but is not quite a full member.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada does not want to move to Europe or become a full EU member. He said Canada wants to be “stronger together” with Europe, not part of a “third bloc” that competes with other countries. Right now, this new idea does not exist in EU treaties, so it would need to be created — and that could take years.

3. Why Do They Want Closer Ties?

Both sides have things to share:

  • AI and Technology: Both Canada and Europe want to work together on smart computer programs and inventions.
  • Critical Minerals: Canada has important rocks and metals that Europe needs for phones, cars, and clean energy.
  • Defense and Security: They want to team up on protecting their countries.
  • Energy Security: Canada can send liquid natural gas (a clean-burning fuel) and hydrogen to help Europe stay powered up.
  • Student Exchange: Canada wants to join Erasmus+, a fun program that lets students study in other countries. The UK will join in 2027, and Canada hopes to follow.

4. Some People Have Questions

Not everyone is completely sure about the idea. Some European countries — like Germany — wonder if calling Canada an “associate member” gives Canada special treatment over other countries that also want to join the EU, like Georgia and Moldova.

Canada would not have to pay the same membership fees as full EU members. But it might have to follow some EU rules, kind of like how Norway and Switzerland work with Europe.

Meanwhile, some people in Canada — like Conservative leader Pierre Poilievre — say they are worried this new partnership takes attention away from everyday problems like high food prices and expensive mortgages.

Fun Fact

Canada and the EU have had their trade deal (CETA) since 2017. As of 2026, only 17 out of 27 EU countries have fully approved it! That shows how complicated international agreements can be.
