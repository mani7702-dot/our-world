How did a tiny kitten survive stuck inside a car engine for hours?

By Our World Published 21 October 2026 at 14:37 IST
Reading options

A tiny black kitten crawled into the warm engine bay of a car in Sandy Springs, Georgia, and got trapped as the couple drove home — only to be rescued by firefighters and police officers just in time for their Halloween anniversary.

How did a tiny kitten survive stuck inside a car engine for hours?

A surprise under the hood

Nicole and Brandon Motahari were driving to pick up birthday cupcakes for Nicole's journalism students in Sandy Springs, Georgia, when another driver started honking at them.

"Something was hanging underneath our car," Nicole remembered.

They pulled over right away. What they found was a tiny black kitten — so small it weighed just 544 grams, less than a pound — wedged deep in the car's suspension and engine bay. The kitten had crawled inside for warmth, probably on a cool October morning, and the car had driven a short distance with him trapped.

Calling the helpers

Nicole and Brandon did not try to move the kitten themselves — a frightened animal in a tight space can be hurt by sudden movement. Instead, they called for emergency help.

Three Sandy Springs firefighters and two police officers arrived. The first job was to make sure the kitten was safe and the car was stable — you never want a spooked animal to run into traffic. The second job was the careful, gentle work of reaching the kitten where he was wedged.

"We couldn't just send him on his way," Nicole said later.

The kitten — they learned he was less than seven weeks old — was checked over right away. He was a little thin, a little dusty, but he was alert and completely unharmed.

A Halloween homecoming

It was Nicole and Brandon's Halloween wedding anniversary — their special day fell right around October 31st, when black cats and unexpected surprises feel like part of the fun.

Adopting a tiny black kitten in October felt fated. They named him Friday Shadow, and he came home to a life of sunny spots by the window, quiet books, and plenty of kitten-sized adventures.

Their story reminds everyone that heroes in our neighborhoods — firefighters and police officers — help in the smallest, gentlest ways too.

Why do kittens crawl into engines?

Tiny kittens are always looking for three things: warmth, safety, and a soft place to nap. A warm car engine looks like a perfect snug den — but it is dangerous for them. Kittens this small can get trapped, and moving parts can hurt them.

If you ever see a kitten or small cat near or under a car:

  • Look carefully before starting the engine
  • Listen for meowing or scrabbling
  • Call for help — local animal services or your fire department can safely free a trapped pet
  • Never reach in yourself — a scared kitten may scratch or bite out of fear

Try this at home

  • Engine-check game: Walk around a parked car (with an adult) and point out all the warm, cozy spots a small animal might like — and all the spots that look safe from danger.
  • Helper role-play: Ask an adult to be the "firefighter" and a stuffed animal to be the "kitten." Practice gentle, calm movements — speaking softly, moving slowly — so the stuffed animal "feels safe."
  • Halloween kindness: If you see community helpers on Halloween (firefighters, crossing guards), draw them a thank-you picture for all the unexpected rescues they do.

Fun Fact

Black cats have been companions to humans for at least 9,000 years — that is older than the pyramids of Egypt! Sailors believed black cats brought good luck at sea, and in Japan, the "Maneki-neko" (beckoning cat) statue is said to bring fortune to shops and homes. A black kitten showing up right before Halloween is a tradition that spans centuries — just ask Friday Shadow.
