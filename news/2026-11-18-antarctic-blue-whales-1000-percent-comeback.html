Is the Biggest Animal That Ever Lived Coming Back?

By Our World Published 18 November 2026 at 21:52 IST
Reading options

Antarctic blue whales were hunted until almost none were left. A 2026 count finds about ten times more than in 1973 - yet still only 2 of every 100 that once lived there.

A giant that almost disappeared

The blue whale is the biggest animal that has ever lived - bigger than any dinosaur we know of. Its heart is the size of a small car, and its tongue can weigh as much as an elephant.

Blue whales live in every ocean, but the ones around Antarctica are their own separate group, called Antarctic blue whales. They eat tiny shrimp-like krill and can live for many decades.

The years the ocean went quiet

For about sixty years, whaling ships hunted these whales for their oil. By the time the hunting finally stopped in 1973, scientists think only about 418 Antarctic blue whales were left.

That is about 1 in 500 of the whales that used to live there. It was one of the saddest chapters in the history of the sea.

Ten times more whales

Now a team of scientists has finished a huge count. They used old whaling records, photos that identify each whale by its markings, and surveys from ships, reaching back more than a hundred years.

Their answer: about 4,018 Antarctic blue whales were alive in 2024. That is roughly ten times more than in 1973 - a rise of about 1,000 percent.

The group is growing slowly, by about 4.5 out of every 100 whales each year. Slow, but steady, and in the right direction.

Still a long way home

Before whaling, there may have been around 205,000 Antarctic blue whales. So today's number is only about 2 out of every 100 of the old one.

That is why the Antarctic blue whale is still listed as Critically Endangered. A mother whale has just one calf every two to three years, so the group can only grow slowly.

What this teaches us

Protecting animals works. But it works slowly, and a comeback can take many whale lifetimes. The ocean is still healing from something people did more than fifty years ago.

Try this at home

Draw 500 tiny dots on a sheet of paper. In 1973 there was about one whale left for every 500, so colour just one dot blue.

Today there are about ten, so colour nine more. The rest of the page is still empty - and that is the room these whales still have to grow into.
