Why Did a Fairy Chimney Fall Down in Cappadocia?

A Special Rock Turned to Dust

In the magical-looking region of Cappadocia, Turkey, tall, skinny rock towers called fairy chimneys have stood for thousands of years. On Thursday, October 29, 2026, one of these stone giants near the town of Göreme suddenly crumbled. Local officials said at least two visitors were hurt and five others were injured in the accident. Rescue teams worked right away to make sure everyone was safe. The collapse happened close to the entrance of the Göreme Open-Air Museum — a place so beautiful it is protected by UNESCO (that is the United Nations helping save special spots around the planet).

How Do Fairy Chimneys Form?

Cappadocia sits on top of layers of volcanic ash and soft rock that harden into tuff over time. Rain and wind slowly carve away at it. Fairy chimneys are special because they have a harder cap on top — like a tiny helmet of stone — that protects the softer rock below. Over many, many years, the wind and water wash away the soft parts and leave only the tall, thin spires you see in photos.

Sometimes, though, the hard cap breaks off. When that happens, the softer rock below is exposed and quickly washes away — and the whole tower can fall down in an instant.

What Happened Here?

Turkish media reported that heavy rain may have soaked into the ground before the collapse. Water is the main sculptor of Cappadocia's landscape, but too much of it at once can weaken the spot underneath a fairy chimney. The governor of Nevşehir province said rescuers found everyone who was trapped, and the area was closed so experts could check the safety of other nearby rocks.

Try This at Home

You can make your own fairy chimney! Build a layer of soft clay or sand, then add a small, flat stone on top as the "hard cap." Now gently sprinkle water over it. Watch how the water soaks in and the layers shift — just like real erosion in Cappadocia. Remember: in real life, scientists check old rocks before letting people climb near them!

Fun Fact: Cappadocia means "the land of beautiful horses" in an old Turkish dialect — but most visitors come to see the fairy chimneys, not the horses!

For Grown-Ups: The incident occurred at approximately 15:15 local time (12:15 GMT) on October 29, 2026, at the Göreme Historical National Park open-air museum, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Per BBC News reporting (Oct 29, 2026), two people were confirmed deceased and five injured; the Turkish Justice Minister initially reported "other potential victims" still being searched for. Heavy rainfall was cited as a possible trigger, consistent with Cappadocia's geological formation process: volcanic tuff softened by water erosion beneath protective harder cap rock, as documented in Wikipedia's "Hoodoo (geology)" and "Cappadocia" articles. Göreme is the central town of Cappadocia province in central Anatolia, Turkey.
