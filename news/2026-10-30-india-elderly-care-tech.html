"How Tech Start-ups Are Helping India Care for Its Growing Elderly Population"

By Our World Published at 16:42 IST
Reading options

How Do Start-ups Help Take Care of Elderly Relatives?

When younger family members live far away, new tech tools are keeping grandparents safe and connected.

1. The Big Picture: More Grandparents, Fewer Helpers Nearby

Did you know? One in five people in India will be 60 or older by the year 2050. That is like having an entire classroom of grandparents for every five kids in your school!

Around the whole world, the number of people aged 60 and above is expected to double from 2020 to 2050 — reaching about 2.1 billion people. This is happening because people are living longer and healthier lives, which is wonderful… but it also means families need new ways to take care of their elders.

In the past, grandparents often lived in the same house as their children and grandchildren. A big "joint family," as it is called, meant help was always nearby. But today, younger people move to cities for school or jobs, making it harder for families to provide daily care close to home.

2. Enter the Care-tech Start-ups

Some smart entrepreneurs saw this challenge and thought, "How can technology help?" They created start-up companies that do things like:

  • Checking health remotely: Apps and devices that send alerts if a loved one falls or misses

taking medicine.

  • Organized senior living: Special apartment communities where older adults have their own space

but can still join group activities and get help when they need it.

  • Emergency support: 24-hour helplines and rapid-response teams that can send help right away.

One startup founder named Anantharam Varayur noticed that older people in India sometimes had fewer options than their peers in other countries. So he helped build senior housing projects that feel like cozy neighborhoods, not hospitals.

3. The Demand Problem: Not Enough Spots for Everyone

Here is a puzzle: Real estate experts say India needs 2 to 2.2 million senior living units. But right now, only about 25,000 are available. That is like having 100 cookies for a school of 800 kids — there are just not enough!

The industry — worth about $7 billion according to India's government planning think-tank NITI Aayog — is growing fast. More and more companies are building new kinds of care services, hoping to fill the gap.

Try This at Home: Stay Connected Across the Miles

If your grandparents live far away:

  1. Set up a simple video call on a tablet or phone (ask a grown-up to help).
  2. Create a shared photo album online so everyone can add pictures.
  3. Teach them how to send voice messages — sometimes hearing your voice is the best part of the day!

Fun Fact

In Japan — a country with one of the world's oldest populations — some hospitals use robot pandas and parrot-shaped companion robots to keep elderly patients company. The robots can remind people to take medication and even lead gentle exercise sessions!

For Grown-ups (but kids can read this too!)

The challenge of caring for an aging global population is a real-world problem that engineers, doctors, and business thinkers are working on every day. Technology isn't just fun — it's helping families stay close even when they live far apart. The key is making sure these tools are designed with older adults, listening to what they need, not just what is trendy.
