"How an Italian farm-waste plant turns straw and peels into gas for 3,200 homes"

By Our World Published 21 October 2026 at 14:30 IST

How an Italian farm-waste plant turns straw and peels into gas for 3,200 homes

The big idea: waste in, gas out

In the "toe" of Italy's boot — a sunny region called Basilicata — a new kind of power plant is running. But unlike a coal or gas plant, it doesn't burn anything at all. Instead, it takes leftover crops, peels, and other farm scraps and quietly turns them into clean-burning gas.

The plant at Grottole opened its pipes in 2026 and sends its first gas into Italy's national energy grid. One year of its output powers about 3,200 homes — enough for a small town.

Why this matters (and why it's clever)

Italy gets more than half of its electricity from natural gas. That means the country needs a lot of pipelines and power, but it also wants to cut pollution. A biomethane plant solves both problems.

Farmers drop off their leftover straw, peels, and other organic scraps. The plant processes that waste into biomethane — a gas so clean it can flow right through the same pipes that already carry natural gas. Nothing goes to landfill. Nothing gets burned the old way.

The circular trick

Here is the clever part. When farmers give the plant their waste, they get something back too: fertilizer. That fertilizer goes right back onto the fields.

So the cycle looks like this:

  1. Farm waste goes into the plant
  2. Clean gas comes out for homes and businesses
  3. Nutrient-rich fertilizer comes out for the soil
  4. Healthier soil grows more crops

No waste. No new pipelines. No forests cut down. And each year, the plant avoids sending roughly 9,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide into the sky — about what you would save by taking 2,000 cars off the road.

Try this at home

  • Compost trick: If you have a garden, try a mini version. Kitchen scraps (peels, coffee grounds) + dry leaves → mix them in a bin → in a few months you get rich soil for plants. Same idea, just smaller!
  • Energy detective: Look around your kitchen for "waste" that could be useful. Those orange peels? They are full of oils. That coffee ground? Great for roses. One person's trash is another's treasure.

Fun Fact

Italy has warmed up fast to biomethane. In 2022, the country produced just 0.25 billion cubic meters of biomethane. By 2026, it was producing 1.6 billion cubic meters — that is a 600% jump in just four years!

The Grottole plant alone makes 45 gigawatt-hours per year. That is enough energy to charge about 1.1 million smartphones.

For grown-ups

The Grottole biomethane plant in Basilicata, southern Italy, was acquired by the Swiss energy group Axpo in 2024 as a ready-to-build project and began feeding renewable biomethane into Italy's national gas grid in 2026. It takes agricultural by-products — leftover straw, peels, and other farm waste from regional farms — and processes them into low-carbon biomethane gas that powers roughly 3,200 Italian households a year (equivalent to about 45 GWh of energy). Compared to using natural gas, this saves roughly 9,000 tonnes of CO2 emissions annually. The gas is sold through a long-term contract to Cartiere del Garda, a paper mill. Full ramp-up takes up to six months; the plant is designed to run for decades. Italy generates 46% of its electricity from natural gas, so projects like this help the country cut emissions while keeping existing gas infrastructure useful.
