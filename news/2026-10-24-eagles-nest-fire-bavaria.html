How Firefighters Saved History's Famous Mountain House

By Our World Published at 14:32 IST
Reading options

How did firefighters race up a mountainside to save a famous old house?

High in the Bavarian Alps sits a tiny house perched on a craggy peak. This stone house — called the Eagle's Nest — is not an ordinary mountain hut. It was built in the 1930s and today it stands as a peaceful memorial, a place where people go to learn about history and enjoy sweeping views.

Last Friday evening, alarm bells rang: a fire had started at the Eagle's Nest! More than 200 brave firefighters and rescue workers raced up the steep mountain roads to the scene. Because the building sits so high up and the terrain is difficult, the teams had to work extra hard to reach the flames and keep everyone safe.

The Eagle's Nest sits above the town of Berchtesgaden, right on the border with Austria. It is one of Germany's most visited historic sites. The building was almost completely destroyed by the blaze, but thanks to the quick action of the emergency crews, the structure still stands and the story of the place can be told for generations to come.

What can young explorers learn from this?

Buildings that are old and special need extra care. Just like how you look after your favorite toy, countries work to protect important places so that children and grown-ups can visit them and learn from them. The firefighters showed teamwork, bravery, and quick thinking — the same skills that help explorers solve problems wherever they are.

Try this at home

Build a mini mountain house using cardboard boxes and toy figurines. Pretend your toys are firefighters racing uphill to help. You can use a small flashlight as a "searchlight" and count how many "emergency helpers" it takes to reach the top!

Fun Fact

The Eagle's Nest was originally built as a guest house, and its elevator was one of the first of its kind — it travels inside the mountainside for over 400 meters before popping out to the house!
