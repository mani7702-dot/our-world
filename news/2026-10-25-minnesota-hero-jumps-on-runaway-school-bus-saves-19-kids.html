"How did a Minnesota man stop a runaway school bus full of kids?"

By Our World Published 25 October 2026 at 11:15 IST
Reading options

"When a school bus rolled down a Minnesota street with its doors wide open and no one controlling it, a quick-thinking bystander named Ebro Ahmed ran toward danger, jumped onto the moving bus, and brought it to a safe stop — saving 19 children and earning a $1,000 reward."

How did a Minnesota man stop a runaway school bus full of kids?

One brave bystander, a moving school bus, and a split-second decision that saved 19 children. When Ebro Ahmed saw a school bus rolling down a Minnesota neighborhood street with its doors wide open and no one controlling it, he didn't look away — he ran straight toward danger.

1. The moment something looked wrong

On a Monday morning in October 2026, Ebro Ahmed, a 27-year-old Eritrean-American man, was standing outside a little convenience store called Ya Bricks Snacks and Tobacco in St. Paul, Minnesota. That is when he noticed a big yellow school bus slowly rolling past him — but something was very, very wrong. The bus's doors were hanging open, and its warning lights were flashing. School buses only flash those red lights when they are stopped to pick up or drop off children, so Ebro immediately knew something was not right. The bus was moving, but no one seemed to be driving it.

2. The leap onto a moving bus

Ebro saw that the bus driver was having a medical emergency — what doctors later described as a seizure. Instead of standing there in shock, he did something most people could only dream of. He sprinted toward the moving bus, jumped through the open door, and landed inside the wobbling vehicle. Cameras from the store caught every second of it: Ebro wrestling with the gear shift, accidentally putting the bus into reverse, then quickly correcting himself and throwing it into neutral before yanking the parking brake to bring the bus to a safe stop. All while 19 schoolchildren sat inside, wide-eyed but unharmed.

3. A hero with no cape (just a TV show for courage)

Ebro had no medical training. He said what kept him calm was remembering his favorite TV show, Grey's Anatomy — where doctors never panic. "I just knew to be calm and think everything through," he told CBS News. After the rescue, a local resident was so grateful that they gave Ebro a $1,000 reward. But Ebro said he would not have taken any recognition at all — his only focus was keeping the children safe.

Try this at home

Next time you see something that does not look right, ask yourself: what could I do to help? You do not have to be a firefighter or a doctor to be brave. Sometimes, like Ebro, just noticing a problem and choosing to act — even when you are scared — is the greatest superpower of all.

Fun Fact

School buses are among the safest ways to travel to school — the big yellow color makes them easy to see, and the flashing lights plus stop sign arm warn other drivers to stay back. But Ebro's quick thinking reminds us that the best safety system is having alert, caring adults ready to help when things go wrong.

"On the morning of Monday, October 19, 2026, Ebro Ahmed, 27, an Eritrean-American man working nearby at Ya Bricks Snacks and Tobacco in St. Paul's Dayton's Bluff neighborhood, noticed a Battle Creek Middle School bus rolling past at about 10 mph with its doors wide open and its flashing lights on. School buses only flash their warning lights when stopped to pick up or drop off children, so Ahmed immediately recognized something was wrong. As confirmed by CBS News and Good News Network, the bus driver was experiencing a medical emergency (a seizure). Ahmed sprinted from the sidewalk and jumped through the bus's open door while it was still moving. Inside, he struggled with the gear shift, briefly putting the bus into reverse before correcting himself and shifting into neutral, then pulling the parking brake to bring the vehicle to a stop. After the incident, a local resident gifted Ahmed $1,000 in appreciation for his bravery. Ahmed, who has no medical training, credited watching the TV show Grey's Anatomy for helping him stay calm during the chaotic moment, saying, 'In Grey's Anatomy, you never see doctors panic — I just knew to be calm and think everything through.' The school bus driver's condition was not released, but Ahmed said the driver was 'coming to' and 'getting a little bit better' by the time paramedics arrived."
