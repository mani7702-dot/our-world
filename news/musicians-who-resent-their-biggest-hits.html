10 famous musicians who grew to regret the songs that made them famous

By Our World Published 21 October 2026 at 08:14 IST
Reading options

Some artists spend their whole lives running from one song. Here are 10 famous musicians who came to resent the very hits that made them stars — and why they just could not sing them anymore.

10 famous musicians who grew to regret the songs that made them famous

Pop stars spend years hoping for a hit. But sometimes the song that shoots you to fame is the very song you can not escape for the rest of your life. Here are 10 famous musicians who came to regret the tracks that made them famous.

1. Kurt Cobain and "Smells Like Teen Spirit"

When Nirvana's Nevermind exploded in 1991, "Smells Like Teen Spirit" became the anthem of a generation. But Kurt Cobain later admitted he struggled to perform it sincerely — the song's massive success felt like an obligation he never signed up for. Every night on tour, audiences treated the opening chords like a call to worship while Cobain increasingly saw the song as something he had to get through.

2. Miley Cyrus and "Party in the U.S.A."

Before she was a pop star, Miley Cyrus was best known as Hannah Montana on TV. "Party in the U.S.A." made her a real-world hit — but she has said the song was "not even my style of music" and worried it would define her whole identity. She worried fans bought into an "all-American" image that did not feel like her real self.

3. Radiohead and "Creep"

Thom Yorke has said he was "not happy with the lyrics" of "Creep" and thought they were "pretty crap." The band grew so weary of the song that they did not perform it live for years — they felt it set narrow expectations of who they were, even as it became their most successful single.

4. Madonna and "Like a Virgin"

"Like a Virgin" made Madonna a global superstar in 1984. Decades later, she has spoken about how uncomfortable she felt with the song's image of her as the innocent bride — a persona she had outgrown. She has said the track became a costume she could not take off, which felt very different from the bold artist she wanted to be.

5. Taylor Swift and "Shake It Off"

"Shake It Off" was a megahit from 2014, but Swift has said the constant requests to perform it left her "so sick" of the song that she could not even listen to it for a year. She has also reflected that some of the lyrics felt like they were teaching the wrong message, even if the song was fun to perform at the time.

6. Lady Gaga and "Poker Face"

Before "Poker Face," Lady Gaga was working in New York clubs. The song made her a worldwide star. But she has said the endless touring on the track left her exhausted, and she struggled with how the public saw her as the "just dance, don't sing" performer — a role that did not match her serious musicianship.

7. Pharrell Williams and "Happy"

"Happy" became one of the most-played songs in history — but Pharrell has said the constant requests almost made him "hate" it for a while. He later forgave the song, but the sheer volume of people asking him to perform it again and again made the experience overwhelming.

8. Carly Rae Jepsen and "Call Me Maybe"

"Call Me Maybe" was a 2012 phenomenon that turned Carly Rae Jepsen into a household name overnight. But she has said the song's success made her feel "typecast" — radio stations did not want to play her other music, so she was stuck in a tiny box even as she tried to grow as an artist.

9. OMI and "Cheerleader"

OMI's 2015 hit "Cheerleader" was streamed billions of times, but he has said the sudden fame and nonstop touring on one song took a toll. He worried he was being reduced to just one moment — even though he had been writing and performing music for years before.

10. Lou Bega and "Mambo No. 5"

"Mambo No. 5" was a 1999 party anthem that Lou Bega performed at countless events for decades. He has said he grew to resent the song because audiences only wanted that one track — and he watched his serious musical work get overlooked because everyone just wanted the "mambo."

Why hits can become prisons

The music business rewards a breakout moment. But that moment can stick to you like glue. Fans love the song that introduced you, and radio stations keep playing it because it still works. For some artists, that means spending decades singing the same hit and wishing they could move on.

The good news? Many of these musicians have come to appreciate their songs again. They remind us that a hit song is not a life sentence — and that regaving your audience one great track does not stop you from making more music that feels like your own.

Try this at home

Make a playlist of your favorite songs — then ask yourself: which ones would I want to sing every single day? Which ones would I need a break from? Talk about it with friends and see if you agree. (You might be surprised how quickly favorites change!)

Fun Fact

The longest-running #1 song on the Billboard Hot 100 is "One Sweet Day" by Mariah Carey and Boyz II Men — but Mariah Carey has said she can barely sing that one anymore, too!
