"How do astronauts earn science's highest honor? Artemis II crew wins the Hawking Medal"

By Our World Published 24 October 2026 at 02:40 IST
Reading options

The four Artemis II astronauts traded places with movie stars and rock legends when they received the Stephen Hawking Medal for science communication at Starmus VIII.

1. A medal fit for a rock star — and a rocket scientist

When NASA's Artemis II crew stepped onto the stage at Starmus VIII in the Canary Islands, they were greeted not just by scientists — but by a rock legend. Queen's Brian May, who is also an astrophysicist, helped present the Stephen Hawking Medal to astronauts Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen.

The medal is one of science's most prestigious honors for communication. It was created in 2016 by the late Professor Stephen Hawking himself, along with Brian May and the Starmus festival. Past winners include legendary naturalist David Attenborough and Apollo 11 moonwalker Buzz Aldrin.

2. The mission that brought the Moon home

In April 2026, the four astronauts climbed aboard NASA's brand-new Orion spacecraft, launched on the mighty Space Launch System (SLS) rocket, and flew around the Moon — the first humans to leave Earth's neighborhood since 1972.

During their 10-day journey, the crew stayed in constant contact with Earth. Through live video calls, breathtaking photos, and interviews, they helped millions of people imagine what it's like to travel to our closest celestial neighbor. That's exactly what the Hawking Medal celebrates.

3. Words from 250,000 miles away

Standing before the Starmus audience, Commander Reid Wiseman shared a simple but powerful message: "If we could take each of you 250,000 miles away from this planet, and you would see Earth the size of your thumb — it's tiny."

When he came home, Wiseman told everyone: "When you come back to this beautiful planet, you realize it's in every single one of us to choose to make tomorrow better."

4. Why we need space storytellers

Brian May — whose PhD in astrophysics from Imperial College London focused on zodiacal light — said it best: "It's a special thrill for us at Starmus to honor the Artemis II crew members in the same way we honored Apollo astronauts 50 years ago."

The medal isn't just about going to space — it's about bringing the wonder back down to Earth, so every child can look up at the night sky and dream.

Try this at home

Look for the Orion constellation in the winter sky — it's easy to spot, with three bright stars forming Orion's belt. The Orion Nebula, visible as a fuzzy star in Orion's sword, is a stellar nursery where new stars are born. Imagine you're orbiting the Moon, gazing back at the blue dot that is Earth — how would you tell your friends back home what it looks like?

Fun Fact

The Stephen Hawking Medal is cast from a meteorite that fell to Earth over 1,500 years ago. Each recipient receives a unique medal made from the Gibeon meteorite — ancient metal from the age of the dinosaurs!

For grown-ups

The four Artemis II astronauts — Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen — received the Stephen Hawking Medal at Starmus VIII on Oct 17, 2026. The medal was established in 2016 by Stephen Hawking and Brian May (Queen guitarist and astrophysicist) in collaboration with the Starmus festival. Artemis II flew April 1–11, 2026, carrying NASA astronauts Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, and Christina Koch, plus Canadian Space Agency astronaut Jeremy Hansen, on a 10-day lunar flyby in the Orion spacecraft — the first crewed flight beyond Earth orbit since Apollo 17 in December 1972. The crew reached a maximum distance of 252,756 miles (406,771 km) from Earth, passing behind the Moon. Starmus VIII took place Oct 17–22, 2026 in Tenerife and La Palma in the Canary Islands. Past Hawking Medal recipients include Buzz Aldrin, David Attenborough, Jane Goodall, and Neil deGrasse Tyson.
