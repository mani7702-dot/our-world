How 99-Year-Old Helga Duffy Became the World's Oldest Wing-Walker

By Our World Published 21 October 2026 at 23:40 IST
Reading options

At age 99, Helga Duffy strapped herself to the wings of a tiny plane in England and earned a Guinness World Record — proving that adventurous spirit doesn't retire.

How 99-Year-Old Helga Duffy Became the World's Oldest Wing-Walker

Most people think 99-year-olds are happy with a cup of tea and an early bedtime. But Helga Duffy had a different idea — she wanted to fly.

Strapped to a wing at 99

In mid-October, Helga climbed aboard a vintage biplane at RFC Rendcomb Airfield in Gloucestershire, England. While the pilot taxied down the runway, she was strapped — fully harnessed — to the top wing of the aircraft. Up in the air, thousands of feet above the countryside, she felt something she described as "peculiar."

"I wasn't frightened," she said. "I was so excited."

The record that keeps moving

Helga didn't invent wing-walking — she just became its oldest practitioner. The title was last held by Betty Bromage, 97, who completed her own wing-walk just three months earlier and joked she'd be back to reclaim it at 100. Wing walking is a real aerial sport: participants run their hands along the wing struts while the plane loops and banks, all while wearing a safety harness that keeps them securely attached.

Guinness World Records presented Helga with an official certificate right there at the airfield. The whole stunt also raised more than £1,300 for the Wiltshire and Bath Air Ambulance — an especially meaningful cause for Helga's family, since their mother had depended on the service.

What's a wing-walker, anyway?

A wing-walker is a person who climbs onto the top wing of a vintage biplane while it is flying through the sky. They are fastened in with strong safety straps so they cannot fall off, and they often wave or run their hands along the wing from front to back. The planes fly slowly in a controlled way, doing gentle rolls and loops so the wing-walker can feel the wind and see the ground far, far below.

Try this at Home

Feel the wind like a pilot. Go to a high hill or tall building with an adult and stick your hand out the car window on a breezy day. Tilt it forward, backward, and side to side — that is the force a wing-walker feels. Can you make your hand "dance" in the air?

Or: learn about safety harnesses. If you visit an aviation museum, ask about pilot harnesses. Notice how the straps crisscross and clip in a pattern that spreads force across the shoulders and hips — that is the same idea that keeps wing-walkers safe.

Fun Fact: The world's oldest wing-walker is now 99 years old, but wing-walking itself has been thrilling crowds since the 1920s, when barnstorming pilots hired people to walk on their wings during air shows!

For grown-ups

Helga Duffy, 99, of Devizes, Wiltshire, became the world's oldest female wing-walker on October 16, 2026, at RFC Rendcomb Airfield in Gloucestershire, replacing 97-year-old Betty Bromage who held the title for just three months. Wing walking is an aerial sport where a person is strapped onto the top wing of a vintage biplane while it flies — participants wear full safety harnesses. Helga raised money for the Wiltshire and Bath Air Ambulance and was presented with a Guinness World Records certificate. She was inspired to try wing-walking after seeing Betty Bromage, then 97, complete the feat on the news. The previous record (Guinness World Records, "Oldest wing-walker") was formally held by Betty Bromage of the UK at age 97.
