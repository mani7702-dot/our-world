"Who Built a Mysterious 4,000-Year-Old Wall in Morocco?"

By Our World Published 31 October 2026 at 19:57 IST
Reading options

Imagine you are hiking through the green hills of north-west Morocco. You stop to rest beside a ridge that looks over a river. Under the grass and soil beneath your feet, something is hidden — something that has been waiting there for about 4,000 years.

In 2023, a team of archaeologists dug into that ridge, at a place called Oued Beht. What they found was extraordinary: a long, wall-like structure that may be the oldest known large defence built in North Africa outside Egypt's Nile Valley.

A wall from a lost chapter of history

The structure is roughly 4,000 to 3,500 years old, which places it in the second millennium BC. Researchers describe it as a ditch, an earthen rampart (a raised bank of earth), and probably a wall as well.

Why would people build something so big, so long ago? Nobody knows for certain — and that is exactly what makes it a mystery. The scientists say the builders were most likely not afraid of "invaders," but rather competing for land, resources, and control of the routes people travelled through the landscape.

The very fact that they could organise such a project tells us something huge: the communities of ancient North Africa were more organised and complex than many history books once suggested.

A place people kept coming back to

Oued Beht is not just a one-time wonder. Earlier digging there revealed that the same ridge held one of the largest early farming settlements in Africa outside the Nile Valley, thriving around 5,400 to 4,900 years ago.

Then, more than a thousand years after the wall was first built, people came back. Between the 11th and 13th centuries AD, they rebuilt the fortification, raised a small farm or hamlet nearby, and buried their dead between the two sites.

One ridge, three chapters of human history — prehistoric, and medieval.

Fun Fact

The ancient builders chose a ridge overlooking a river. That's a classic smart choice: high ground lets you see danger coming from far away, and a river gives you water and a route to travel. People thousands of years later picked the very same spot for the very same reasons!

Try this at home

Become a landscape detective. Stand at the top of a hill or a tall staircase near you and ask: What can I see from up here? Look for water, roads, or paths below. Would this be a good place to spot visitors from far away? Ancient peoples asked those same questions — and sometimes the answers they chose are still hiding under our feet.

For grown-ups

"Archaeologists from the Oued Beht Archaeological Project, publishing in Libyan Studies (September 2026), report the earliest known large-scale construction with a probable defensive purpose in North Africa outside the Nile Valley. Excavated in 2023, the Oued Beht ridge in north-west Morocco carries a ditch, earthen rampart and likely wall dating to roughly 2000–1500 BC (the second millennium BC), placed by 17 radiocarbon dates. The same ridge had earlier revealed Africa's largest known early farming complex (c. 3400–2900 BC), and was reused in medieval times (11th–13th centuries AD) for a rebuilt fortification, a hamlet and a cemetery. The team argues the structures point to competition over land, resources and movement routes rather than to 'invaders'. Source: ScienceDaily / Libyan Studies; corroborated by SciTechDaily and Arkeonews."
