"Can a 240-year-old pond in Cornwall be swimmable again?"

By Our World Published 24 October 2026 at 12:27 IST
Reading options

"A historic bath house pond built in the late 1700s on the Antony Estate in Cornwall is being restored so people can swim in it again — bringing a piece of living history back to life."

Can a 240-year-old pond in Cornwall be swimmable again?

1. A pond built for swimming — 240 years ago

Tucked away on the Antony Estate in Torpoint, Cornwall, there is a pond that has been waiting more than 240 years to be swum in again. It is not just a garden pond — it is a carefully designed swimming pond built in the late 1700s, right next to a little bath house that sits beside the tidal River Lynher.

The pond was planned by architect Thomas Parlby and built by Reginald Pole Carew, who wanted a quiet place to dip into the cool water. Today, the Carew Pole Garden Trust is teaming up with local artists and community groups to bring that pond back to life.

2. Why it stopped being swimmable

Over the centuries, the pond gradually changed. Leaves and twigs from surrounding trees fell in, and tiny plants and mud began to settle on the bottom. What was once clear swimming water turned into a quiet, still pond.

Now, specialists are figuring out how to clear the pond, check the trees around it, and understand how the tidal river naturally feeds water through the site. By studying the flow of the River Lynher, they hope to bring back the movement that keeps a pond healthy.

3. Making it swimmable again

The goal is simple: make the pond swimmable again and turn it into a welcoming place for the whole community. Funded by the National Lottery Community Fund, the project plans to:

  • Clean and restore the 240-year-old pond and bath house
  • Let local people connect with nature — and the water
  • Host fun workshops with artists and nature lovers

The team says education, creativity, and community participation will be at the heart of the project. Open-water swimmers, nature fans, and anyone curious are invited to help shape the pond's future.

Fun Fact: The River Lynher flows right into the River Tamar — which forms the border between Cornwall and Devon! That means the pond's water eventually journeys over 60 miles to meet the sea at Plymouth Sound. And here is a cool twist: the pond is tidal, so the water level rises and falls twice a day with the ocean's rhythm — even though it is more than 20 miles inland!

Try this at home

You don't need a river to explore how water moves! Grab a shallow tray, add some sand or soil, and tilt it slightly. Pour water from a small jug at the top and watch how it flows and carves little channels. You can even add small pebbles to see how they change the water's path. With an adult, visit your local nature reserve or lake and look for water plants, fallen leaves, or tiny creatures that call the water home.

For grown-ups

"The bath house pond at the Antony Estate in Torpoint, Cornwall, was built in the late 18th century, designed by architect Thomas Parlby and constructed by Reginald Pole Carew. It is part of the Grade I-listed Antony House estate, now managed by the National Trust (Wikipedia, Antony House, Cornwall). The pond is fed by the tidal River Lynher, which flows through east Cornwall and joins the River Tamar at the Hamoaze (Wikipedia, River Lynher). Restoration funding comes from the National Lottery Community Fund, with the Carew Pole Garden Trust partnering with Torpoint Artists Collective and Rooted Futures. Natural swimming pools — filter-fed bodies of water that operate without chemicals — are a European tradition (Wikipedia, Natural swimming pool)."
