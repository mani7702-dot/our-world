Why did the world's only floating city hit a fuel wall in Cambodia?

Imagine living on a giant ship that sails around the entire planet — visiting hundreds of countries over three years. That is exactly what passengers on the Villa Vie Odyssey signed up for. But when the price of fuel exploded, their floating dream came to a sudden stop in a Cambodian port.

1. A lifetime at sea, now paused

The Villa Vie Odyssey is not your ordinary cruise ship. It is a floating residential community — some people even bought cabins for life, paying up to $440,000 for the right to live at sea permanently. It left Belfast in October 2024 with a 3.5-year plan to visit nearly 150 countries.

After more than 750 days at sea (that is over two full years!), the ship was cruising near Cambodia when fuel prices made the journey too expensive to continue. The company announced it had to suspend operations — leaving about 200 passengers temporarily stranded in the port city of Sihanoukville.

2. When fuel costs go through the roof

Here is the surprising part: it was not the ship breaking down. It was simply too expensive to keep moving. Fuel costs per cabin had risen from $1,800 to as much as $8,000 in parts of Asia — that is a price jump of over 300 percent!

The company tried everything: they slowed the ship down, spent more time in port waiting, and asked passengers to consider paying extra. But even with all those savings, the fuel bill was impossible to shoulder.

"Passing the full increase on to residents is not a realistic solution," the company explained. They decided to pause the voyage while they restructured the business over the next 60 days.

3. Why did fuel prices jump so much?

Fuel prices around the world have climbed since the start of the year, partly tied to shifts in global energy markets and shipping routes. When a ship is crossing entire oceans, even small changes in fuel costs add up quickly across thousands of miles.

The Villa Vie Odyssey covers vast distances — from the icy waters around Antarctica to the warm tropics of the Pacific. When fuel doubles or triples in price, a journey that cost millions can suddenly cost tens of millions.

4. Not giving up hope

While passengers found themselves unexpectedly in Cambodia (a beach town famous for its own history, not as a cruise stop), they did not just sit quietly. They supported each other, shared hotel rooms, and even kept some of the community spirit alive on land.

After all, they had already been through delays before — the ship had been stuck in Belfast for months during repairs at the start of the trip. The same resilience that carried them through those early hiccups is helping them navigate this latest challenge.

Fun Fact

The Villa Vie Odyssey was designed to carry up to 650 passengers, but at any given time, far fewer people buy into the full voyage — making it feel more like a tight-knit neighborhood than a crowded resort. It is one of the few ships in the world built specifically as a permanent residence at sea, not just for vacation cruises.
