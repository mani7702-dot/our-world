How did a drunk tourist end up guilty of stealing a Las Vegas flamingo named Peachy?

By Our World Published 20 October 2026 at 22:10 IST
Reading options

"A Canadian visitor to Las Vegas thought stealing a flamingo from the Flamingo hotel would be a funny prank — he was wrong. Mitchell Fairbarn was convicted of animal cruelty after the Chilean flamingo Peachy was found injured."

You've probably seen tourists at theme parks sneak a photo with a mascot or a quick feed of the ducks. But what happens when a visitor takes things a little too far?

In Las Vegas, a Canadian man learned the hard way that you absolutely do not steal a flamingo — especially not from a hotel named after one.

The prank that went wrong

Mitchell Fairbarn, 34, was staying at the Flamingo Las Vegas Hotel and Casino in March. The hotel isn't just about slot machines and shows — it's also home to a free wildlife habitat where visitors can walk through and meet real animals, including a flock of pink Chilean flamingos.

Fairbarn later told the court that he had drunk 30 to 40 beers and thought it would be "funny" to take a flamingo back to his room. So he climbed into the habitat, grabbed a bird named Peachy by the neck, and rode the elevator up to his hotel room with the startled flamingo in tow.

Security cameras caught everything.

What happened to Peachy?

Animal experts testified that Peachy suffered real harm. The flamingo had feathers torn out and lost blood from being carried improperly. A veterinarian later said Peachy has mostly recovered — but the bird is now more anxious around people.

For Fairbarn, the joke ended fast. A jury of 12 people deliberated for about five hours before convicting him on both felony and misdemeanor animal abuse charges. He now faces up to five years in prison, and his sentencing hearing is scheduled for December 2026.

Why this matters

The Flamingo's wildlife habitat opened in 1995 and is meant to be a safe home for the animals — not a place where visitors can grab them. Hotels with animal habitats have strict rules about respectful observation, and for good reason. Animals deserve to feel safe, whether they're roaming the African savanna or strolling through a Las Vegas casino.

Fairbarn's lawyer tried to argue that his client meant no real harm and that alcohol led to "bad decisions." But the jury didn't buy it — and hopefully other visitors get the message.

Fun Fact: Flamingos aren't actually born pink! They start life with gray feathers. They only turn that iconic bright pink after eating shrimp and algae full of carotenoid pigments — the same pigments that give carrots their color. So Peachy's pink coat comes from a shrimp-heavy diet.

Try this at home: Wonder what animals live near you? Take a family walk around a local park or nature center and look for signs of wildlife — tracks, feathers, or nests. Remember: look with your eyes, not your hands. Even small wild animals need their space to stay healthy and happy.

Sources

BBC News covered the court proceedings in Las Vegas, including Fairbarn's testimony that he drank 30 to 40 beers, the security camera footage of the flamingo being carried in an elevator, and the jury's five-hour deliberation before convicting him on felony and misdemeanor animal abuse charges. Wikipedia's article on the Flamingo Las Vegas confirms that the hotel's wildlife habitat opened in 1995, is free for visitors to walk through, and features Chilean flamingos, Black-necked swans, pelicans, and other birds. Both sources confirm that the habitat is meant for quiet observation — not touching.
