13 stories this week

New items are added as they happen, written for young readers.

  • 20 October 2026 A simple touch can bring dying corals back to life in 7 weeks

    Scientists found that a bleached, whitening coral can fully recover if it simply touches a healthy coral from the same family. It is the fastest comeback from bleaching ever recorded.

  • 20 October 2026 Why are Mars's moons so lumpy? A new mission will grab a handful to find out

    Japan's MMX spacecraft blasted off to Mars's moon Phobos to scoop up moondust and bring it back to Earth — the first sample ever from the Mars system.

  • 20 October 2026 Mosasaurs: the fast, sneaky hunters of the ancient seas

    While dinosaurs ruled the land, bus-sized mosasaurs hunted the ocean. New physics models show they did it with sudden, sneaky bursts of speed.

  • 19 October 2026 Why did a bear walk into a bakery at a farmers market?

    A hungry black bear strolled straight past the shoppers at an Aspen market and made a beeline for the bread booth — and stayed, they say, for a snack and a nap.

  • 19 October 2026 A 12-foot shark got stuck in a city canal — and the internet made it famous

    A bronze whaler shark chased its lunch into a canal in South Korea and could not find its way out. Then 600,000 people came to wave.

  • 19 October 2026 Why are owls nesting in a Manhattan water tower?

    A family of barn owls moved onto a rooftop water tower in the heart of New York City — the first barn owl nest anyone can remember in Manhattan.

  • 19 October 2026 Why is NASA hunting for caves to build a Moon base?

    Before anyone can live on the Moon, NASA wants to find underground caves, hidden ice, and safe ground. Three new missions are going to scout the way.

  • 19 October 2026 Eight orangutans go home: a forest waited for them

    After wildfires destroyed much of their jungle this summer, eight orangutans were rescued, cared for, and driven back to a safe forest. It was the first release since the fires began — and the news made a lot of people happy.

  • 19 October 2026 Can a crayfish get bigger than "Crawzilla"?

    A friendly rivalry over one giant Kentucky crayfish sent scientists on a nationwide hunt — and the real record-holder turned up hiding in a museum drawer.

  • 19 October 2026 Can a world 20 light-years away have weather?

    NASA's Webb telescope watched a wild world 20 light-years away and found its stormy skies follow surprisingly tidy rules — three repeating weather patterns, not total chaos.

  • 16 October 2026 Look up: October's sky has a meteor shower

    NASA's monthly sky guide says the Orionid meteor shower lights up this month — here is how to watch it with no telescope at all.

  • 15 October 2026 A telescope named after "the mother of Hubble"

    NASA's newest space telescope made TIME's list of the best inventions of 2026 — and it carries the name of a woman who fought to be in astronomy at all.

  • 11 October 2026 Five cheetah cubs, one very busy camera

    Five cheetah cubs were born at the Smithsonian's conservation park — and you can watch all of them, around the clock, on a livestream.
