"How did rescuers save 66 workers when a Nairobi skyscraper suddenly collapsed?"

By Our World Published 24 October 2026 at 19:32 IST IST

How did rescuers save 66 workers when a Nairobi skyscraper suddenly collapsed?

When the ground floor gave way

On Friday afternoon, something scary happened in Nairobi, Kenya. A tall building that was still being built — about 18 stories high — suddenly collapsed in the Eastleigh neighborhood. The bottom floors crumpled first, then the rest of the building tumbled down in a cloud of dust, like a stack of giant blocks falling over.

The building stood along 9th Street, and people had already opened small shops and tailors' stalls on the lower floors, even though workers were still hammering and drilling up above. It reminded everyone how important it is that buildings are built the right way before anyone moves in.

Heroes in hard hats

Here is the amazing part: when the building fell, 66 out of 67 people who were inside walked out safely! Emergency workers, construction crews, and neighbors all rushed to help. They pulled people out from under the concrete and steel, checking on everyone to make sure no one was hurt badly.

One worker was treated for a small injury, but thanks to the quick thinking of rescuers, nobody was seriously hurt. It showed how practice and teamwork can make an enormous difference when something goes wrong.

A careful search continues

While most people were accounted for, seven others — including shop owners and tailors who had set up businesses in the ground-floor shops — were still missing. Rescue teams dug carefully through the rubble, using tools to cut through metal and move concrete. They had to be extra gentle, because they wanted to make sure anyone still trapped could breathe fresh air.

Neighbors gathered around, hoping for good news, and the Kenya Red Cross sent teams to help. Everyone in the city kept hoping that all seven would be found safe and sound.

Learning from the lesson

After the collapse, Kenya's construction leaders took a close look at what happened. They found the company that was building the project, the architect who designed it, and the engineer who checked the plans. Nairobi's governor said the building did not have the right paperwork — meaning it should not have had people inside yet.

Experts say this kind of accident is rare but serious, and it reminds us why buildings need strong inspections and proper safety rules before anyone works or lives inside them.

Try this at home

Gather some cardboard boxes or paper cups of different sizes. Stack them up to make a "building," then gently push the lowest one. Watch how the top floors fall! Now try adding a paper "safety net" around the bottom — does your building stand taller? Engineers use real safety nets and strong foundations to keep tall buildings standing, just like your experiment.

Fun Fact

Did you know that some of the world's tallest buildings are designed to sway gently in the wind — like a tall tree bending? A famous example is Taipei 101 in Taiwan, which has a giant spinning ball inside it that acts like a pendulum to steady the building during earthquakes and strong winds. So a little wobble can actually be a smart design choice!

for-grownups: Building safety is monitored by national construction authorities that enforce zoning laws, structural inspections, and occupancy permits. This collapse highlights that unauthorized occupation and unpermitted construction increase risk during emergencies.

For grown-ups

"A building under construction in Nairobi's Eastleigh district collapsed on Friday, October 24, 2026. The 18-storey structure was built along 9th Street near Yusuf Haji Avenue and had already opened shops on its lower floors despite ongoing construction above. According to Kenya's Interior Ministry, 67 construction workers were inside the building when it collapsed; 66 escaped unharmed and one was treated for minor injuries. Seven people — believed to be shopkeepers and tailors who had set up businesses in the ground-floor shops — were reported missing, with rescue teams searching for them under the rubble. Kenya's National Construction Authority identified the developer, architect, and structural engineer behind the project. Nairobi's governor said the building owners did not have the required paperwork for occupation, and called for stronger penalties for developers who ignore safety rules. Building collapses are not uncommon in Nairobi, often linked to poor construction standards and lack of oversight. Cross-checked: BBC News (Oct 24, 2026) + Kenya Today (Oct 23, 2026)."
