5 Ways Living Underwater Prepares Astronauts for Space

Have you ever wondered how astronauts practice for the challenges of living in space? One surprising answer is: underwater! NASA and other space agencies send "analog astronauts" to live in underwater laboratories so they can train for missions to the Moon, Mars, and beyond.

1. Simulating Weightlessness

On the International Space Station, everything floats. Underwater, astronauts wear special suits filled with weights so they become negatively buoyant — just like floating in space! This lets them practice moving heavy equipment, doing repairs, and even "walking" through water as if they were floating in zero gravity.

2. Living in a Tiny, Isolated Habitat

The Jules' Undersea Lab and Aquarius (NOAA's underwater station) are small metal boxes where teams live for days or weeks. This mimics life on the ISS or a future lunar base — you're sealed in a tiny space with your crewmates, cut off from the outside world, just like astronauts on a long trip to Mars.

3. Scientific Research in Extreme Conditions

Living underwater lets researchers study how the human body reacts to high pressure — something that happens when astronauts' bodies adjust to space. Crews grow microgreens, run science experiments, and even practice neutral buoyancy (floating perfectly) to prepare for spacewalks.

4. Testing Teamwork and Leadership

When something goes wrong 22 feet underwater, you can't just walk outside and call for help. The crew must solve problems together — just like a real space mission. These missions test how well people communicate, make decisions under pressure, and support each other in tight quarters.

5. Practicing Emergency Procedures

Storms, equipment failures, and medical emergencies are real risks for underwater crews. Training for these dangers helps astronauts prepare for the even greater risks of space — where a small mistake can be life-threatening and help is days away.

Fun Fact

NASA's NEEMO (NASA Extreme Environment Mission Operations) program has been sending astronauts to live in the Aquarius laboratory since 2001. The facility sits 62 feet (19 meters) below the surface off Key Largo, Florida — making it the world's only undersea research station used for space training!

for-grownups:

The article "Analog astronauts: How living underwater can prepare humans for space missions" (Space.com, Oct 26, 2026) by astrophysicist Dr. Michaela Musilova describes her experience leading the SEA STAR mission — a simulated space mission at Jules' Undersea Lab, 22 feet underwater in Florida. This is verified against Wikipedia's NEEMO entry, which confirms NASA has used the Aquarius underwater lab since 2001 for analog space missions. Both sources confirm that the high-risk underwater environment (vs. standard land-based analogs) creates stricter procedures that better simulate real space conditions, including emergency scenarios, team isolation, and neutral buoyancy training.
