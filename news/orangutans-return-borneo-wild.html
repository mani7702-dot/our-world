Eight orangutans go home: a forest waited for them

By Our World Published 19 October 2026 at 22:20 IST
Reading options

After wildfires destroyed much of their jungle this summer, eight orangutans were rescued, cared for, and driven back to a safe forest. It was the first release since the fires began — and the news made a lot of people happy.

Earlier this month, eight orangutans went home — and it was one of the happiest animal stories of the season.

The eight great apes — three mothers, three daughters, and two adult males — were released back into a protected forest on the island of Borneo after being taken in when wildfires swept through their homes over the summer. The release was announced by International Animal Rescue Indonesia (known as YIARI) together with Indonesia's forestry ministry.

Why they had to be rescued

This year's dry season brought unusually intense wildfires to Borneo, made worse by a strong El Niño weather pattern. The fires burned a huge share of the forest that orangutans call home, destroying their food and driving them closer to people and plantations.

When that happens, rescuers step in. Altogether, Indonesian authorities rescued 46 orangutans from the fires during August and September. Some, like a mother named Mama Nopi and her babies Nopi, Naufal, and Nopan, had been living in a plantation after the forest around them burned. Others were rescued from patches of trees surrounded by oil-palm fields. One mother and her baby had been watched over by rescue teams since June, when fires boxed them into a small strip of trees.

A ride back to the wild

The rescue teams drove the eight orangutans to a protected reserve in the west of the island that had everything they needed: plenty of food, water, and connections to other forest areas so the apes can roam far and wide.

The release itself was a careful, hopeful moment — each orangutan stepped out of its transport cage and into the trees. YIARI's teams said they wanted the rescued orangutans to have a real chance to survive in their natural habitat, not just be moved from one place to another.

Why it matters

Orangutans are critically endangered — one step away from vanishing in the wild. Their numbers have fallen sharply over the past century, so every family kept safe matters enormously. This was the first release since the fires began, and sadly, many more orangutans still need help. But a mother climbing down from a rescue cage with her babies close behind is a real victory.

For the people who spent months caring for them, watching the big apes melt back into the rainforest was the whole point of their work. One rescued family at a time, the forests of Borneo are getting their gentle giants back.
