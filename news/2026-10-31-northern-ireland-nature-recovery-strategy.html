Can a New Plan Bring Northern Ireland's Nature Back?

1. A Big Promise for a Small Place

Northern Ireland is one of the most "nature-depleted" places in the world — that means many of its wild plants and animals have disappeared over the last few decades. So on 27 October 2026, the region's environment minister, Andrew Muir, announced a brand-new Nature Recovery Strategy: a long-term, science-based plan to protect what is left and help nature bounce back.

"We must put nature back at the heart of everything we do," Muir said, "because when nature thrives, people flourish."

2. Why Does Nature Need Help?

Here is the surprising part. Northern Ireland has hundreds of protected sites — special places for rare plants, animals and habitats — covering about one-tenth of the land and much of its coastal waters. But being "protected" on a map is not the same as being healthy.

  • About 40% of the plants and animals living in those protected sites are not doing well.
  • Only one of 49 important habitat types — such as ancient woodland and blanket bog — is in good shape.
  • The number of sites in healthy condition actually fell from about 62% in 2008 to about 52% in 2024.

As Muir put it: "Our task is not simply to draw lines on a map. Protected areas must be properly managed and genuinely delivering for nature."

3. What's the Plan?

The strategy sets five goals and joins a worldwide effort called "30 by 30" — a promise by many countries to properly protect and manage 30% of land and sea for nature by 2030.

To make it happen, the plan names the six biggest pressures on nature: invasive species (plants and animals brought from elsewhere), climate change, careless development, changes to how land and sea are used, intensive farming, and pollution. It also creates a new Nature Recovery Forum so that scientists, farmers, businesses and communities can all work together.

4. How Can Nature Bounce Back?

"Young people should be able to connect with nature," Muir said, pointing to the health and happiness that time outdoors brings. He also shared a hopeful thought: "There are many inspiring examples of nature bouncing back when given half a chance."

That is the heart of the plan — not just guarding the wild places we have, but helping them heal and grow.

Try this at home

Adopt a "patch of wild." Pick a small corner of a garden, balcony or even a window box and leave it a little messy — let the grass grow, add a native flower, or build a tiny log pile. Write down which insects, birds or plants visit over a month. You are running your very own nature-recovery project!

Fun Fact

A blanket bog is a super-soggy peatland that can store enormous amounts of carbon — more per hectare than a rainforest. Some bogs in Ireland and Scotland have been quietly building up peat for thousands of years, growing only about one millimetre a year.

For grown-ups

"On 27 October 2026, Northern Ireland's Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs (DAERA) Minister Andrew Muir launched the Nature Recovery Strategy, a long-term science-based framework to halt biodiversity loss and restore habitats. It commits NI to the global '30 by 30' ambition (effectively conserving 30% of land, inland water and marine areas by 2030) and sets five strategic objectives plus a target to bring forward statutory nature-restoration targets in the next mandate. Context: roughly 40% of plants and animals in NI's protected sites fall short of 'favourable condition', and only one of 49 priority habitat types has favourable status. Designated sites cover about 10% of land and 38% of coastal waters, and the share in favourable condition fell from 61.7% (2008) to 51.5% (2024). The 2023 State of Nature Report ranks NI among the most nature-depleted regions in the world, with 12% of species threatened with extinction and flowering plants down 14% since 1970. Six named pressures: invasive species, climate change, inappropriate development, land- and sea-use change, intensive farming, and pollution. A Nature Recovery Forum will coordinate delivery, and a biodiversity finance plan must secure public and private funding. Sources: BBC News (27 Oct 2026); DAERA press release ('Muir launches strategy to restore nature')."
