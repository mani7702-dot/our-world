Why Denmark said goodbye to mink farms — and what it means for the animals

A furry fashion that is fading out

For decades, Denmark was the world's biggest supplier of mink fur. Those silky, spotted pelts ended up in coats, hats, and scarves worn by people who wanted something soft and warm. The country's famous Kopenhagen Fur alone supplied about 40% of all the mink fur traded around the planet.

But times are changing — and Denmark is making a big decision. By August 7, 2027, it will be illegal to farm mink for their fur. After that, every remaining farm gets two years to shut down completely.

Why the switch?

The Danish government says it comes down to animal welfare. Nature Minister Christian Rabjerg Madsen said something simple: just because something has been done for a long time does not mean it should keep going on. He said a business should make sense — not just for money, but for society and the animals.

The change did not come out of nowhere. In 2020, the country faced a crisis when a mutated version of COVID-19 spread through mink farms. Denmark's response was to order the mass cull of about 17 million mink — one of the largest animal culls in history. After that, fashion brands worldwide started saying "no thanks" to real fur, and many countries already had bans in place.

What happens to the mink farms?

Denmark is one of the few European countries that still raised mink for fur, but the industry had already become tiny. At its peak, thousands of farms dotted the countryside. Now there are only seven farms left, home to about 15,000 mink.

Closing those farms will cost money — about £1.56 million (14 million Danish kroner), according to early estimates. That cash helps farmers switch to something else, like growing crops or raising different animals.

The fashion world is moving on too. More and more designers now use fake fur — soft materials made from recycled plastic bottles that look and feel just like real fur, without any animals being harmed.

Try this at home

Next time you're at a store or online, spot the fake fur challenge: find one item labeled "faux fur" and one labeled "real fur." Compare the prices, the tags, and the textures. Ask a grown-up which one used recycled materials instead of animals!

Fun Fact

A single mink coat can take the pelts of up to 60 mink to make. That is a lot of animals for just one jacket!
