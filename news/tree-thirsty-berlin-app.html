How a Video-Game App Lets Kids Adopt and Water Thirsty Trees in Berlin

By Our World Published 21 October 2026 at 14:30 IST
Reading options

A smartphone app in Berlin turns tree-watering into a game — kids can adopt a tree, check when it was last watered, and keep the city cool during heatwaves.

The App That Turns Tree-Watering Into a Game

Imagine if your neighborhood had 900,000 trees — and you could adopt any one of them with a tap on your phone.

That is exactly what happened in Berlin, Germany, where a clever smartphone app turned tree-care into a video game. The app is called Gieß den Kiez — which means "Water Your Neighborhood" in German.

How Does It Work?

The app shows a map of the whole city with millions of little tree icons. Each tree is a different color:

  • Green = the tree was watered recently and is happy
  • Orange = the tree could use some water soon
  • Red = the tree is thirsty and needs help right now!

Anyone can sign up to adopt a tree near their home, school, or playground. Once they adopt one, the app sends them reminders: how much water the tree needs, when to water it, and even what time of day is best.

Why Do Trees Need So Much Water?

Berlin saw temperatures over 103 degrees Fahrenheit (39 degrees Celsius) this summer — that is hotter than many places in the southern United States!

Trees are like the city's air-conditioning system. Their leaves release water vapor that cools the air around them. One study showed that a single tree's cooling effect can lower the temperature in a 90-foot radius by more than 1.5 degrees.

Without enough water, trees cannot do their job. They stop cooling the air, and cities get even hotter — which is why Berlin's tree-watering app is so important.

Linda the Linden Tree

Amanda Gläser-Bligh, a mom from Berlin, adopted a linden tree she named Linda. Every week during the hot months, she carries 20 liters (about 5 gallons) of water in a backpack and gives it to her tree.

"It feels really fun, almost like a video game," she said. "I think many people want to water the trees, but they are not sure if it is their job. With this app, you know you are doing the right thing."

A Game That Spreads

Because the app is free and open-source, other cities are copying it. So far, Berlin, Potsdam, Leipzig, Magdeburg, and even one neighborhood in Paris have launched their own versions.

The trees seem to like it too. Berliners have adopted thousands of trees — and the city's youngest residents are some of the most excited new tree-parents.

Try this at home

You do not need an app to help trees in your neighborhood! Here are things you and a grown-up can try:

  1. Adopt a tree at home: Pick one tree in your yard or near your school. Check its soil every few days — if the top inch is dry, give it a deep drink of water.
  2. Measure the cooling: On a hot day, use a thermometer to compare the temperature in the shade of a tree versus the sun. How many degrees cooler is the shade?
  3. Start a tree journal: Draw or photograph your tree every week. What changes do you notice in the leaves, bark, and branches?

Fun Fact

Did you know one mature tree can release enough oxygen for two people to breathe all day long? Trees also filter about 10 pounds of air pollution from the atmosphere each year. Some cities calculate that every dollar spent on planting and caring for trees saves four dollars in future healthcare costs!
