How the World Helps Families Get Food — and Why Money Matters

When a family doesn't have enough to eat, the World Food Programme (WFP) — the United Nations' food-helping arm — sends food. But there's a catch: the more money the WFP raises, the more families it can reach.

Feeding millions with love

Right now, the WFP provides food assistance to about 1.1 million people in Gaza. That is almost as many people as live in Rhode Island! Every day, community kitchens cook large pots of hot meals — about 250,000 plates — and volunteers hand them out to families who need them.

Children get special snacks that help them grow strong, and women receive packages with nutritious foods. Bakers make bread for about a third of the whole area's daily bread needs.

Why the numbers dropped

A year ago, the WFP was helping about 1.6 million people. That is half a million fewer families than before. The reason is simple: donations went down.

Governments and people around the world donate money to WFP — it is like a giant fundraising drive. When the fundraising slows, the food delivery slows too. The WFP says it needs about $450 million to keep all its most important programs running for six months. That is roughly what it costs to buy 90 million Big Mac meals — but food aid is, of course, much more important!

How you can see the helpers

Even when money is tight, the WFP keeps cooking the meals it can. The bakers keep stirring. The volunteers keep handing out plates. And that is the part that matters most — because it shows something beautiful about our world: when we see someone who needs help, we reach across the table and share.

Fun Fact

The World Food Programme won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2020! The Nobel Committee said, "Without a strong focus on peace, there is no sustainable development." The WFP has helped deliver food in disasters from earthquakes to floods to conflict zones, serving more than 5 billion meals since it began in 1961.

Try This at Home

Talk to an adult about local food banks or soup kitchens in your city. You can organize a food drive at school — even a small collection of canned goods makes a difference. The WFP even has a ShareTheMeal app where you can donate as little as $0.50 to feed a child for a day. Every bit counts!

For Grown-ups

The WFP is the UN's food-assistance arm and the 2020 Nobel Peace Prize laureate. In Gaza, it provides hot meals (currently 150,000/day), flour/ration boxes (600,000/month), nutritional snacks for 300,000 children, and bread support for roughly one-third of local bread needs. Funding comes entirely from voluntary government/private contributions — the 2026 shortfall reduced reach from 1.6 million to 1.1 million people. The IPC (July 2026) reported 1.4 million people in Gaza at IPC Phase 3+ (Crisis or worse) acute food insecurity. WFP sought $450m for six months of critical operations.

For grown-ups

"The World Food Programme (WFP) is the UN's food-assistance arm. It operates the world's largest humanitarian logistics network, serving ~100 million people in ~80 countries. In Gaza, WFP provides hot meals via community kitchens, flour and ration boxes to monthly distributions, and specialized nutritious snacks for children. WFP funding comes from voluntary contributions by governments and private donors — when those contributions drop, fewer people can be reached. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) project, co-led by WFP, FAO, UNICEF, and NGOs, reported in July 2026 that 1.4 million people in Gaza faced Crisis or worse acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+). WFP estimated it needed $450 million for critical operations over six months.
