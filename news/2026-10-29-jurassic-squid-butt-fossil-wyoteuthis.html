"What did the fattest Jurassic squid look like? A 'squid butt' fossil surprised scientists"

By Our World Published 29 October 2026 at 17:30 IST
Reading options

"Scientists have found the fattest belemnite ever discovered — a barrel-shaped squid relative from 160 million years ago that was nicknamed a 'squid butt' by local fossil hunters in Wyoming."

The fossil that stuck out like a sore thumb

Bill Wahl is a Wyoming paleontologist who has spent decades combing through rock layers that were once an inland sea. In the late 1990s, he found something that made him stop in his tracks — a belemnite fossil that was unusually wide, like a stubby bullet instead of the long, slim ones he had seen by the thousands.

Belemnites are the fossilized internal shells of ancient squid-like creatures. They look a bit like little bullets and are so common in some rock layers that local fossil hunters call them "squid butts". But this one was different.

The first specimen sat in a museum drawer for years, unstudied. Then in 2019, fossil collector Cliff Linster discovered a second one near Ten Sleep, Wyoming, and donated it to the Wyoming Dinosaur Center. With two specimens to compare, scientists finally had enough evidence to describe an entirely new species: Wyoteuthis linsterorum.

What makes a squid "chunky"?

Most belemnite fossils from Wyoming are long and narrow — about the width of a pencil but a few inches long. The new Wyoteuthis specimen is different: it is 10 centimeters long (about four inches) but six centimeters wide (about 2.4 inches). That makes it the "fattest" belemnite ever found — like a mini cannonball instead of a bullet.

Scientists think the living animal, including its arms, would have been about 60 centimeters long — roughly two feet from tentacle tip to tentacle tip.

Using CT scans, the team peered inside the fossil without breaking it. What they found was surprising: this creature belonged to an ancient family of belemnites that scientists had believed had already disappeared millions of years earlier. Finding it alive during the Jurassic period meant this family had survived far longer than anyone expected — a "ghost lineage" that stayed hidden in the fossil record for decades.

A predator that hunted other squid

Why would a squid be so chunky? The researchers have a theory. The extra width might have given the animal an advantage when hunting.

Modern squid are not picky eaters — they will hunt other squid, sometimes even members of their own species. The team thinks Wyoteuthis may have used its stocky build to chase down and catch smaller belemnites of the genus Pachyteuthis that lived in the same ancient sea. It is like a wolf pack that is just a little bigger and stronger than its prey.

Try this at home

Belemnites are sometimes called "arrow finished at both ends" because of their shape. You can make your own fossil cast at home:

  1. Find a small toy or coin with an interesting shape.
  2. Press it into a chunk of modeling clay or playdough.
  3. Carefully remove the toy and fill the mold with more clay.
  4. When it hardens a bit, peel away the mold. You have just made a "cast fossil" —

just like paleontologists do when an animal leaves an impression in stone!

Ask a grown-up before using the oven or any hardening materials.

Fun Fact

Only two fossils of Wyoteuthis are known to science. That makes it one of the rarest belemnite species ever discovered — and the fact that both were found by hobbyist fossil collectors shows how important amateur paleontologists are to science.

The name Wyoteuthis linsterorum honors the Linster family, who donated their fossil to science. Their earlier discovery, the dinosaur Bambiraptor, is also famous among paleontologists — so the family has a talent for finding remarkable fossils!
