"Where did all this clean energy come from? Tanzania's surprise gas find"

By Our World Published at 15:48 IST
Reading options

Where did all this clean energy come from?

Have you ever wondered how gas lights up homes, powers buses, and heats stoves? Deep in the south of Tanzania, a drill bit found a brand-new pocket of it — and it is a big one.

1. A drill bit struck something special

Near the sunny shores of Mnazi Bay, a little rig called Kasa-1X ST2 started drilling into the ground. Then the instruments began to sing: there was gas down below — a lot of it.

Energy Minister Juma Homera stood at the well site and announced the find held about 223 billion cubic feet of natural gas. For a country that powers almost every home with gas already, that is a big deal.

2. Why is this "a major milestone"?

Tanzania is not new to gas. It pulled gas from the ground and under the sea for decades — its first discovery was on Songo Songo Island way back in 1974, followed by a find at Mnazi Bay itself in 1982. Those pockets helped build the country's existing 57.54 trillion cubic feet of gas reserves.

But nothing this big had been found in over ten years. That is exactly why Minister Homera called it a "major milestone." New gas means more clean energy for lights, factories, and even cars without having to import expensive fuels.

3. What will the gas do?

Nearly 80 out of every 100 light bulbs in Tanzania are already lit by natural gas (government stats from 2024). When the Kasa-1X ST2 field starts production in November, its gas will be bottled and sent straight into the national grid.

That gas will help make electricity, warm homes, and it can even become a cleaner fuel for buses and trucks. In the big city of Dar es Salaam, some people already squeeze a gas cylinder into the back of their cars so they can drive on cheaper, cleaner fuel — and more gas means more of that is possible.

4. The teamwork behind the find

The drilling rig did not work alone. The well at Kasa-1X ST2 is jointly owned by Tanzania's state company, the Tanzania Petroleum Development Corporation (TPDC), and a French oil firm called Maurel & Prom. When the gas showed up, both teams were there to celebrate — and shake hands on the next steps.

Try This at Home

  • Build a "gas" volcano with baking soda and vinegar in a bottle — the fizz is a safe, indoor version of gas bubbling up from under the Earth!
  • Find Tanzania on a world map and point to where the Indian Ocean hugs its southern tip. That is the neighborhood where the gas was found.
  • Ask a grown-up to show you how a gas stove flame burns cleaner than a lump of charcoal — less smoke, more comfort.

Fun Fact

The name Mnazi Bay does not come from gas. It comes from the palm trees (mnazi in Swahili) that grow along the calm, warm water of this corner of the Indian Ocean — and now it is also the name of a promising new energy find.
