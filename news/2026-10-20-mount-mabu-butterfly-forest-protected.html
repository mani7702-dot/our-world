Why Saving One Misty Mountain Lets 200 Kinds of Butterflies Stay Home

By Our World Published 20 October 2026 at 20:15 IST
Reading options

Mozambique protected a misty forest on Mount Mabu so the 200+ butterfly species that live there can keep calling it home.

High up in northern Mozambique, hidden behind a blanket of mist and green, sits a forest so special that scientists call it a "butterfly forest." On Mount Mabu, the butterflies don't just pass through — more than 200 different kinds live there and nowhere else on Earth. Now the whole mountain is officially protected.

A mountain that holds its secrets close

Mount Mabu sits in Mozambique's Lugela district and rises like an island in the sky. Because the slopes are a little cooler and wetter than the land around them, all sorts of plants and animals moved in over thousands of years and slowly grew different from the ones anywhere else. That makes the mountain a "biodiversity hotspot" — a place where life packed in extra variety just by being a little separate.

The butterflies that wear the forest like a costume

Walk into Mount Mabu's forest and you are sharing the air with more than 200 kinds of butterflies. Some have wings the color of sunset, others look like dappled sunlight on leaves. Because so many of them exist only on this mountain, losing the forest would be like closing a library that has no copies anywhere else.

For 20 years, ecologist Julian Bayliss and his team climbed these slopes again and again, counting butterflies and finding new creatures along the way — including a tiny forest viper that was new to science. Each trip added another reason to protect the place before it was too late.

Paperwork that protects a whole mountain

The good news reached a finish line this summer. On August 24, Mozambique's Ministry of Agriculture, Environment and Fisheries signed a decree making 9,341 hectares (about 23,000 acres) of Mount Mabu into the Mount Mabu Community Conservation Area. That is roughly the size of Central Park in New York — but for butterflies, birds, frogs, and trees that live nowhere else.

What makes this choice kind feel important: the local communities who have looked after the forest for generations now get to keep stewarding it. Protection and people, together.

Why kids might care

Kids usually know a few common butterflies (think monarchs or swallowtails). But imagine a place where nearly every flower has its own butterfly that does not exist in any other garden. That is what lives on Mount Mabu, and thanks to the new protection, those butterflies can keep doing their slow, colorful job of carrying pollen from one plant to the next — just like nature planned.

🌱 Try this at home

Plant a single patch of native flowers in a pot or window box and watch who visits. You may not see 200 kinds, but you will be helping the same idea: one small space, many small helpers.

🤓 Fun-fact box

Mount Mabu's forest is one of the largest "medium-altitude" blocks left in southern Africa, which is why so many species evolved there and nowhere else.
