Who Was the Chef Who Brought Curry to America — Then Vanished?

1. A Real-Life Food Mystery

Imagine a chef so famous that crowds gathered just to watch him cook — and then, one day, he simply disappears from history. That is the true story of Ranji Smile, an Indian chef who sailed to New York from London in 1899 and changed what America ate.

Here is the puzzle: we do not even know his real name. Historians think "Ranji" was a nod to a famous Indian cricketer of the day, K.S. Ranjitsinhji, and that "Smile" may have come from the name "Ismail". Like a character in a story, he arrived with a new name and a new life.

2. America's First Indian Restaurant

In 1901, Smile opened a restaurant called Omar Khayyam on Fifth Avenue in New York — almost certainly the first Indian restaurant in the United States. He decorated it with Indian draperies and hanging lanterns, and filled it with waiters in tall, colourful turbans and live musicians.

Dressed in a satin coat and a blue silk turban, Smile would greet guests in the dining room, bow, and ask: "Will you have the curry hot or mild?" Newspapers rushed to write about him, and dishes he served — like Bombay duck (a fish, not a duck!) — amazed New Yorkers who had never tasted Indian food before.

3. Why Did It Not Last?

Sadly, the restaurant closed within about a year. Many diners at the time treated Indian food as a short-lived novelty, and few understood the rich, spicy flavours on the menu. Smile's fame faded, and he began telling bigger and bigger stories about himself — even pretending to be a prince. His last trace in the records comes from a court case in 1937, long after he had vanished from public view.

4. The Legacy That Stayed

Even though Omar Khayyam did not survive, Smile opened the door. Soon other Indian chefs and writers followed — sharing recipes for bhajias, puris, and halwa with curious American readers. Today, there are thousands of Indian restaurants across the United States, and many trace their story back to one bold, mysterious chef who crossed an ocean with a pot of curry.

Try this at home

Ask a grown-up to help you make a simple "curry" with three spices — try turmeric, cumin, and a pinch of mild curry powder stirred into some cooked lentils or vegetables. Taste it before and after adding the spices. How does one little spoonful change everything?

Fun Fact

"Bombay duck" is not a bird at all — it is a small dried fish from the west coast of India! Chefs gave it a funny, catchy name to make it sound more exciting on the menu.

For grown-ups

"Ranji Smile sailed from London to New York in 1899 and opened Omar Khayyam restaurant at 325 Fifth Avenue in 1901 — widely regarded as the first Indian restaurant in the United States. He had previously cooked at London's Cecil and Savoy hotels. His origins are unknown, including his real name; 'Ranji' likely referenced the cricketer KS Ranjitsinhji, and 'Smile' may have been an anglicisation of 'Ismail'. After Omar Khayyam closed within a year, Smile reinvented himself as a supposed 'prince of Baluchistan', was investigated for bringing staff into the US illegally, and later moved between cooking demonstrations across America. The last record of him is a 1937 New York court case filed by one of his wives; he had disappeared. Wikipedia notes he is sometimes called 'America's first celebrity chef'. This story is framed for children as a food-history mystery, focusing on how curry arrived in America and the legacy of the chefs who followed, not on the legal or personal controversies. Sources: BBC News (30 Oct 2026, by historian Dinyar Patel); Wikipedia ('Ranji Smile', 'Dean Mahomed')."
