Why Did Japan Give a Video Game Designer Its Highest Cultural Honor?

The Man Behind Mario, Zelda, and Donkey Kong

In a ceremony at Japan's Imperial Palace on November 3, 2026, Shigeru Miyamoto will step onto a stage to receive one of his country's most prestigious prizes — the Order of Culture. He will be the very first video game creator ever to win it, and it turns out the story of how he got here reads almost like a fairy tale.

A Boy Who Loved to Explore

Miyamoto was born in a small town called Sonobe in Kyoto, Japan, in 1952. As a kid, he didn't have a television or video games — instead, he spent hours exploring the hills, caves, and forests near his home, making wooden toys and telling himself stories about adventures. Those childhood days of imaginative play would one day become the DNA of every great game he created.

When he grew up, Miyamoto studied industrial design (that's a fancy way of saying "making things look cool and work well"). In 1977, he walked into Nintendo's offices with a portfolio of hand-carved wooden toys — and wowed the president so much that he was offered a job designing video games.

The Accidental Hero

At first, Miyamoto was asked to help make a simple arcade game. The result was Donkey Kong — released in 1981. It was an instant hit, and it introduced the world to a new kind of storytelling in games. Donkey Kong told a story: a big ape had kidnapped a girl named Pauline, and a brave carpenter named Jumpman had to climb ladders and jump over barrels to save her.

Jumpman was later renamed Mario after a Nintendo landlord, and Mario became so popular that he got his very own games — Super Mario Bros., Super Mario World, and dozens more. Alongside Mario, Miyamoto created The Legend of Zelda, a series that let players explore vast, mysterious worlds with a fairy-tale sword and shield.

Why This Honor Matters

Japan's Order of Culture was created in 1937, and only a handful of people receive it each year. Past winners include Nobel Prize scientists, world-famous artists, and literary giants. Miyamoto joins their company as the first game designer — proof that the country now sees video games as a serious art form that can rival film, literature, and music.

The badge itself is a golden orange blossom with three crescent-shaped jewels in its center, and it's presented by the Emperor himself each year on Culture Day (November 3) in Tokyo.

The Legacy Lives On

Today, more than 1 billion copies of games featuring Miyamoto's creations have been sold around the world — from tiny handheld Game Boys to massive home consoles. Every time a child grabs a controller and jumps on enemies in a Mario game, or solves puzzles in a Zelda adventure, they're playing a piece of Miyamoto's imagination that started in the forests of rural Japan decades ago.

Miyamoto says he still sketches new ideas by hand every morning — just like the wooden toys he carved as a boy.

Try This at Home

Try drawing your own video game character! You don't need a computer — just paper and a pencil. Think of a hero who must solve a puzzle or overcome an obstacle, then draw them in three poses: standing, jumping, and celebrating. Miyamoto says his best ideas came from simple sketches made during train rides. You could be the next Miyamoto — who knows?

Fun Fact 🌍

Miyamoto isn't just a game designer — he's also directed his own feature films and TV shows. His movie Ponyo (a Studio Ghibli film) was partially directed by him, and he even voiced the character "Nose" in the Nintendo movie The Super Mario Bros. Movie. He's one of the few people whose face appears on a Nintendo game and a movie poster!

For Grown-ups

Shigeru Miyamoto (born November 16, 1952) joined Nintendo in 1977 after impressing president Hiroshi Yamauchi with hand-carved wooden toys. He created Donkey Kong (1981), which introduced Mario (originally Jumpman), and later The Legend of Zelda (1986), Super Mario Bros. (1985), and countless other franchises. His games have sold over 1 billion copies worldwide. The Order of Culture (Bunka-kunshō) is Japan's highest honor in the fields of arts, science, and culture, conferred personally by the Emperor on Culture Day (November 3). It was established in 1937 and has previously been awarded to scientists, artists, writers, and musicians — Miyamoto is the first video game creator to receive it. Wikipedia confirms that Miyamoto created Mario, Donkey Kong, and Zelda, and that he joined Nintendo as an artist in 1977 after impressing the president with his toys. The Order of Culture page confirms it is presented by the Emperor on Culture Day to individuals who have made distinguished contributions to Japanese culture. (Sources: BBC News, October 26, 2026; Wikipedia "Shigeru Miyamoto"; Wikipedia "Order of Culture".)
