What strange cargo did SpaceX deliver to the Space Station this week?

By Our World Published 22 October 2026 at 03:50 IST
Reading options

A SpaceX Dragon capsule docked with the International Space Station carrying more than just food and socks — its cargo bay held 3D heart tissue, brain organoids, materials for an artificial retina, and glass that only forms in zero gravity.

Most rides to the Space Station look the same on the outside: a capsule, a robotic arm, a quiet docking. But what is inside the Dragon cargo capsule this week was anything but ordinary.

SpaceX's 35th cargo run to the International Space Station delivered more than 6,300 pounds of supplies. Tucked among the food packets and new clothes were some of the strangest science experiments ever sent to orbit.

1. Organs that float in zero gravity

The most mind-bending cargo was a set of tiny 3D brain models called organoids — each one a clump of living brain cells about the size of a poppy seed. By studying them in microgravity, scientists hope to learn how brain tissue grows without the pull of Earth's gravity. One experiment, MINDS, is watching how brain cells react to inflammation — a clue that may one day help researchers understand brain health.

The Dragon also carried 3D heart-tissue models — miniature, beating patches of heart muscle grown in a lab. In space, they form blood-vessel networks that are harder to grow on Earth, giving doctors a better model for testing medicines.

2. Building an artificial retina in space

Another experiment is manufacturing artificial retinal tissue layer by layer in microgravity. On Earth, gravity makes the layers uneven. Up in space, the tissue can stack itself into smoother, more stable sheets — potentially better material for future sight-restoring implants.

3. A solar panel upgrade — plus glowing glass

Among the practical cargo were the final two ISS solar arrays (called IROSAs). These roll out like giant wings to catch sunlight and power the station — the last pieces in a years-long upgrade that makes the lab energy independent.

And nestled beside them? Materials for an experiment called ELF-Unconventional Glass 2, which studies how glass made from unusual elements forms when there is no gravity to pull it down. The result could be glass with stronger, more uniform structure than anything made on Earth.

Try this at home

Mix a handful of tiny beads or cereal pieces into a clear jar of water. In the microgravity of your kitchen (well, almost), the beads will settle differently than they do in syrup. Now imagine doing that with melted glass and living heart cells — and you get a sense of why scientists love sending strange cargo to space.

Fun Fact

The name "Dragon" for SpaceX's capsule was chosen because the company joked dragons live in the void between worlds — and the Space Station orbits in exactly that void, 250 miles above us, where anything can happen.

For grown-ups

On 13 October 2026, SpaceX's Falcon 9 launched from Cape Canaveral SLC-40 at 6:33 a.m. EDT on the CRS-35 mission, the 35th commercial resupply flight to the International Space Station. The Dragon capsule carried 6,322 lb (2,868 kg) of cargo and scientific experiments, including the final pair of ISS Roll-Out Solar Arrays (IROSAs) to boost station power, LambdaVision-1 artificial-retina tissue research, Stellar Spheroids 3D human heart-tissue models, MINDS brain-organoid studies of neuroinflammation, and ELF-Unconventional Glass 2 studying how glass forms in microgravity.
