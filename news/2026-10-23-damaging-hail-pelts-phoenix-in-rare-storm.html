"When hailstones the size of marbles fall from the sky — what happened in Phoenix?"

By Our World Published 23 October 2026 at 10:07 IST IST
Reading options

"A rare autumn storm dropped golf ball-sized hail on Phoenix, Arizona, smashing car windshields and house windows. Here is how hail forms and why some storms produce such heavy ice."

When hailstones the size of marbles fall from the sky — what happened in Phoenix?

Most people think of Phoenix as a city of sun and sand — surely it never sees hail? That is what made the storm that rolled through on October 21, 2026 so surprising. Golf ball-sized hail — the kind that can dent your car and shatter your windows — came falling out of the sky.

1. A sky full of ice

Imagine you are walking home and suddenly the sky starts pelting you with hard, cold ice balls. That is exactly what happened to drivers and residents in north Phoenix near the Metrocenter area. The hail was so thick that the streets were covered in white, and the sound was like hundreds of tiny drums beating on rooftops.

The hail ranged in size from a marble to a golf ball — and some reports even mentioned baseball-sized chunks! At the same time, 60 mph winds howled through the neighborhood, knocking over trees and sending loose items flying.

2. Why does hail form?

Hail does not come from cold winter clouds. It comes from thunderstorms — the tall, towering kind that reach high into the sky. Inside these storms, updrafts (powerful upward winds) push raindrops up into the cold parts of the cloud, where the water freezes into tiny ice pellets. Those pellets get pushed up again and again, collecting more layers of ice like a snowball growing bigger and bigger.

When the hailstone gets too heavy for the updraft to hold it, it falls. The stronger the updraft, the longer the hailstone can grow.

3. A rare desert surprise

Phoenix usually stays dry and warm in October. But this storm was different — it pulled in unusually moist air from a low-pressure system over the Southwest. Those extra-strong updrafts had plenty of water to work with, making plenty of ice to share with anyone below.

Meteorologists say storms like this happen a few times each year in Arizona, but golf ball-sized hail is still a rare treat. The National Weather Service issued severe thunderstorm warnings and even tornado watches for parts of the state, just to be safe.

Fun Fact

The world record for the largest hailstone was nearly the size of a bowling ball — 8 inches (20 cm) wide and weighing almost 2 pounds! It fell in South Dakota in 2010. The heaviest hailstone on record weighed more than your pet cat.

Try this at home

Make your own hailstone! Fill a clear balloon with water, add a few drops of blue food coloring, and tie it tightly. Toss it gently in the freezer. Every few hours, open the freezer and squeeze a little more water through a small hole in the neck of the balloon — each layer freezes and adds a new "ring" to your hailstone. After a day, you will have your own layered ice ball just like real hail!

For grown-ups

"On Wednesday, October 21, 2026, a severe thunderstorm swept through Phoenix, Arizona, dropping hail the size of golf balls — and even baseballs — across north Phoenix neighborhoods near 19th Avenue and Peoria. The National Weather Service recorded wind gusts up to 60 mph alongside the hail. Residents reported shattered windows, dented cars, and fallen tree branches. Phoenix rarely sees hail this large in late October because the desert air is usually too dry; the storm tapped unusually moist, unstable air from an upper-level low-pressure system that pushed deep into the Southwest. Hail forms in thunderstorm clouds when updrafts carry raindrops into cold regions of the atmosphere where they freeze into ice pellets. The larger the updraft, the longer the hailstone can grow before it becomes too heavy and falls. Golf ball-sized hail (about 1 inch / 2.5 cm in diameter) is large enough to dent cars and break windows but is not classified as 'giant' hail (which exceeds 2 inches).
