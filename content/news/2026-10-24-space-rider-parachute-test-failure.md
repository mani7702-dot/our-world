# Why did Europe's Space Rider crash during its drop test — and what happens next?

## A test, not a real flight

On October 6, 2026, scientists from the European Space Agency (ESA) deliberately let go of a full-size Space Rider spacecraft model from a helicopter flying about 2 miles (3 kilometers) above the island of Sardinia in Italy. The goal was simple and exciting: watch how the craft would slow itself down and land safely using a big, steerable parachute called a parafoil.

## What went wrong

The craft started its fall just like a skydiver. First, a small *drogue* parachute popped open to slow the descent — that part worked perfectly. But when the larger parafoil was supposed to unfurl and spread its wings to guide the craft toward a gentle runway-style landing, something went wrong. The parafoil detached instead of opening properly, leaving the test model to fall faster than planned and hit the ground harder than hoped.

ESA confirmed the impact was hard enough that the test model was lost. However — and this is the important part — the team recovered all the pieces afterward and is now studying them to find out exactly what happened. An independent investigation is under way.

## Good news: the real missions are still on track

Space Rider is Europe's very first reusable space vehicle. Unlike throwaway rockets that burn up on re-entry, Space Rider is built to fly to orbit, do science experiments for about two months, then come back down and land on a runway — ready to fly again, like a commuter plane but in space!

"ESA's director of space transportation, Géraldine Naja, said during a media briefing on October 22: testing and learning from results — whether good or not — is just part of building and flying spacecraft. The team had already completed many successful tests before this one. The parafoil issue will not push back the timeline for the real Space Rider missions."

The Space Rider is shaped like a lifting body — basically a wingless, flying wing that steers using its parachute. This is different from round splashdown capsules that just fall into the ocean. Space Rider's design lets it touch down gently on solid ground, so it can be ready for its next adventure quickly.

Space Rider is built in partnership with Italy's space agency and is planned to launch on ESA's own **Vega-C** rocket from French Guiana. Future missions will carry experiments from universities and laboratories across Europe.

## Try this at home

**Parachute physics with a plastic bag:** Drop a plastic grocery bag straight down — it floats slowly because air pushes against its wide surface. Now try dropping the same bag with a few coins taped inside so it falls faster. The wider the surface area, the more air resists the fall. Space Rider's parafoil works the same way, but in reverse — it fills with air to create lift and steer the craft down gently.

## Fun Fact

Space Rider fits inside the Vega-C rocket's payload fairing, which means the fully built craft — about the size of **two minivans parked bumper to bumper** — folds up like origami to ride on top of a rocket. When it's time to land, it unfolds its body and parafoil to glide down like a flying saucer.

## for-grownups

The Space Rider programme is led by ESA in collaboration with the Italian Space Agency (ASI). The reusable lifting-body design distinguishes it from traditional capsule return systems. The October 6 test, conducted at the Salto di Padova military range in Sardinia, was the latest in a series of qualification drop tests. While the parafoil deployment anomaly caused the loss of the test article, ESA confirmed all components were recovered and that remaining qualification activities remain on the development critical path.
