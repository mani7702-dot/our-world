# How big can a fungus get?

## The mushroom that looks like a tree stump

Imagine a mushroom so tall you'd have to look up at it — standing nearly two feet high and weighing as much as a bag of sugar. That is no garden-variety toadstool. It is **Dianjunus rex** — nicknamed "D-rex" by the scientists who found it — and it is the biggest fungus anyone has ever seen in its family.

Deep in the forests of Yunnan Province, China, researchers from the Kunming Institute of Botany were walking among the cork oak trees when they noticed something strange poking out of a fallen log. It was not a branch, not a rock — it was a living fungus, thick as a child's arm and standing upright like a tiny, stubborn mushroom soldier.

## A king that appeared after the dinosaurs died

Here is the wild part: this giant has family roots that go back **65 million years** — just one million years after the asteroid that wiped out the dinosaurs struck Earth. By studying the fungus's genes like a clock, scientists can read exactly when each branch split from its cousins. The D-rex family tree says: it grew huge right when the world needed new recyclers.

The asteroid that killed the dinosaurs left forests empty and dead wood everywhere. Something had to move in and break that wood down, returning nutrients to the soil. A tiny ancestor of D-rex likely grabbed that job — and over tens of millions of years, it grew itself into the giant we see today.

## What makes a "fungal giant"?

Most people think of mushrooms as small, umbrella-shaped things that pop up after rain. But the group that D-rex belongs to — the **Ascomycota** — is full of surprises. These fungi do not grow typical mushroom caps. Instead, they make tough, powdery fruiting bodies called stromata that look like something a sculptor carved from wood. They release their spores from tiny sacs inside, like opening thousands of microscopic drawers.

The same family tree that produced gentle **morels** and fragrant **truffles** also gave us life-saving medicines like **penicillin**. D-rex is basically the heavyweight champion of that whole group — proof that the molds and yeasts we usually overlook can, once in a very long while, grow into something almost magical.

## Try this at home

Next time you see mold on forgotten bread, remember: you are looking at a cousin of the giant D-rex. The difference is time. Leave a piece of fallen wood in a shady, damp corner of your garden (with a grown-up's okay) and, if you are very patient, you might spot the first tiny ascomata — the baby version of those lofty stromata — poking out in weeks or months.
