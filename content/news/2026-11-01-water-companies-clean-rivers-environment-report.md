# Can We Make Every River Clean Again? What Britain's Water Watchdogs Found

Every time you flush a toilet, pull a plug or run the tap, dirty water whooshes away through pipes under your street. But where does it go — and what happens if it comes back to our rivers before it's been cleaned?

In late October 2026, the people whose job is to check on Britain's water companies shared some news. Let's look at what they found, and at the clever engineering that is supposed to fix it.

## What the watchdogs found

Four different "watchdog" teams check the water industry in England and Wales. For the first time ever, they shared their notes and added them up **together**.

Their verdict, published on **29 October 2026**, was that **eight of England's nine water companies** still weren't doing well enough for the environment. Only one company, **Severn Trent**, earned the top rating of four stars. Across all the companies, England scored just **16 stars out of a possible 36** — the lowest total anyone has recorded.

The companies' drinking water, though, stayed **"among the best in the world"**, and they had spent **£5 billion** over five years trying to do better.

## Why watchdogs matter

A wide range of living things — otters, kingfishers, fish, insects and water plants — all depend on clean rivers. When too much dirty water flows in by accident, it can harm them. The government's environment chief, **Dame Angela Eagle**, said the results showed the industry keeps **"falling well short"** and that **"people are sick of excuses."**

She asked the bosses of the weakest companies to come to London and explain themselves. Companies given the lowest one-star rating — **Southern Water, Thames Water, South West Water and Yorkshire Water** — now have their bosses' bonuses **automatically blocked**.

## The hidden machine that cleans your water

Here's the part we don't usually see. Long before water reaches a river, it passes through a **sewage treatment works** — a kind of giant water-cleaning factory. It uses the same tricks nature uses, only faster:

- **Screens** catch sticks, wipes and rubbish that shouldn't be flushed.
- **Settling tanks** let heavy sludge sink to the bottom.
- **Friendly bacteria** munch the leftover waste, just like helpful microbes in soil.
- **Filters** and sometimes **sunlight** finish the job before the water is released.

When something breaks down, or a sudden storm overflows the pipes, that cleaning can be skipped — and that's exactly what the watchdogs are counting.

## A plan to put it right

The good news is that plenty of people are working on fixes. Regulators can now fine companies faster under the **Water (Special Measures) Act 2025**, and a new law is being prepared to make repeated failures cost more. The companies have promised their **biggest-ever investment**, and engineers are busy upgrading pipes and treatment works so less dirty water escapes. The aim is simple: cleaner rivers, cleaner lakes, cleaner seas.

### Try this at home

Build a mini water filter! Pour a muddy mixture of water and soil through a paper towel or coffee filter into a jar. What gets caught? Real treatment works layer **screens, grit, bacteria and filters** — try adding gravel and sand to your filter and see how much clearer the water gets.

### Fun Fact

The bacteria in a treatment works do the same job as the microbes in a compost heap — they "eat" waste and turn it into harmless material. A sewage works is basically a compost bin for dirty water!
