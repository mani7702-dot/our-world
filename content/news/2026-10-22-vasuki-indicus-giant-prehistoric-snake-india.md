# How did a 50-foot snake rule ancient India 47 million years ago?

## A giant serpent from the age of mammals

Long before humans walked the Earth — before even the first horses or elephants evolved — a massive snake as long as a school bus slithered through the tropical forests of ancient India. Its name was **Vasuki indicus**, and at up to 50 feet from head to tail, it was one of the longest snakes the planet has ever known. To put that in perspective, that's longer than two full-grown elephants lying end to end!

Scientists discovered Vasuki from 27 fossilized vertebrae — the individual bones that make up a snake's backbone — found in a lignite mine in Gujarat, western India. These bones are from the **Middle Eocene**, about 47 million years ago, when India was an island drifting northward toward Asia. The climate was much warmer then, with temperatures around 82°F (28°C) year-round, which helped support huge reptiles like this ancient giant.

## Named after a mythical serpent

The scientists who found it — **Debajit Datta** and **Sunil Bajpai** from the Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee — gave the snake a special name. **Vasuki** comes from Hindu mythology, where it is the name of a sacred serpent that wraps around the neck of the god Shiva. The second part, **indicus**, means "from India," honoring the place where the fossils were found.

The vertebrae were remarkably well-preserved — some even stayed connected in their original arrangement along the snake's spine! Each individual bone measured between 1.5 and 2.5 inches long and 2.5 to 4.4 inches wide. That's about as big as a large coffee mug — for a single bone in a snake's spine!

## How big was it, really?

Since no complete skeleton was found, scientists had to estimate the snake's total length. They used two different mathematical methods, both comparing the fossil vertebrae to the backbones of living snakes whose lengths are known. One method gave an estimate of 36 to 40 feet, while the other suggested 48 to 50 feet. 

Either way, Vasuki was enormous. Its closest competitor is **Titanoboa**, another giant snake that lived in what is now Colombia about 60 million years ago and reached about 43 feet. Vasuki may have actually been longer than Titanoboa, though scientists note there's still some uncertainty.

## A slow, sneaky ambush hunter

Despite its intimidating size, Vasuki was likely a slow-moving snake rather than a fast, aggressive hunter. Its thick, muscular body and short, wide vertebrae suggest it moved using a rectilinear motion — basically, inching forward like a giant living spring. Rather than chasing prey, it probably ambushed animals that wandered too close.

Scientists think Vasuki hunted like modern anacondas: it would wait motionless in or near swampy areas, then strike suddenly when a deer, primitive horse, or other animal came within range. Its powerful body would coil around the prey and squeeze until it stopped breathing — just like a boa constrictor does today.

Vasuki belonged to an ancient group of snakes called **Madtsoiidae**, which lived for about 100 million years — much longer than any human civilization has existed! Unlike today's pythons and boas, these snakes were part of a completely separate evolutionary family that's now completely extinct.

## Fun Fact

The largest snake alive today is the reticulated python, which can grow up to 30 feet long. That sounds impressive — but it's still 20 feet shorter than Vasuki indicus!
