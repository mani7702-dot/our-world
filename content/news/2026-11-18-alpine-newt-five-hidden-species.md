Imagine finding a small newt in a pond. It looks exactly like the newts in your nature book. Same orange belly, same spots, same gentle way of swimming.
Now imagine a scientist tells you it is not that newt at all. It is another species. And so are four of its cousins, even though your eyes cannot tell them apart.
That is the strange story of the Alpine newt, a small salamander from the ponds of Europe. It turned out to be five species in one.
## One name, five animals

For a long time, scientists had a single name for Alpine newts from Spain to the Balkans. They all look alike, so they were treated as one animal.
Then a team led by Ben Wielstra at Leiden University read their DNA instead of only their bodies. They found five groups that barely mix, even where their ponds sit side by side.
## A newt with no name

Four of those groups already had a scientific name. The fifth did not. It lives only on the Vlasina Plateau, on the border of Serbia and Bulgaria.
In November 2026 the team gave it one: Mesotriton similis. The word similis means similar, because the newt looks just like its cousins.
## Rare and far from home

This is the rarest Alpine newt in Europe. Its home is small, so pollution, damaged ponds and introduced fish can harm it quickly.
There is a bigger lesson here too. Two animals can look the same and still be different. Sometimes you need DNA, not just eyes, to see the difference.
