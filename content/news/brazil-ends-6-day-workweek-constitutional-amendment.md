Imagine working six days a week — every week — with only one day off to rest, visit
friends, or simply breathe. For millions of workers in Brazil, that was reality until
recently. But on October 14, 2026, something big changed: Brazil's Senate voted 75 to 1
to end the six-day work schedule for good.

## One day off is not a weekend

In the 6 x 1 system, workers toiled for six 8-hour days straight, then got just **one**
day off. That single day had to fit everything: groceries, doctor visits, family time,
sleep. For a country of 215 million people, this meant millions never really had a
weekend.

Compare that to the four-day weekend you might enjoy after a long project — two full
days to recharge. Most developed nations settled on a five-day workweek decades ago.
Brazil was one of the richest countries that still clung to the old six-day rhythm.

## The vote that changed everything

The Senate vote on October 14 was overwhelming — 75 senators said "yes," and only 1
said "no." For a body known for deep divisions, that kind of unity on a work issue is
rare. Just two days later, on October 16, the measure was signed into law.

## A slower shift for smoother change

The new rule does not rip off the bandage overnight. Instead, it phases in gently over
ten years:

| Phase | Maximum workweek | What it means |
|-------|-----------------|---------------|
| Now | 44 hours | The old ceiling |
| Over 5 years | 40 hours | A standard five-day week |
| Over 10 years | 36 hours | A four-day workweek for many |

That gradual shift lets businesses adjust, schools reschedule, and families plan for
more together-time.

**Fun Fact:** Brazil is not the only place to try a four-day week. A 2023 trial in
Scotland gave workers the same pay for fewer hours, and most companies said their
teams were just as productive — and a lot happier.

**Try this at home:** Ask a grown-up about their work schedule. Can you figure out how
many hours they work each week? If they worked six days like Brazil used to, how many
hours would they lose — or gain — under the new 36-hour week?
