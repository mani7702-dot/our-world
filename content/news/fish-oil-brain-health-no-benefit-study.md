# Why didn't fish oil pills help people's brains? A two-year study found the answer

## The big question about tiny pills

Every day, millions of Americans swallow fish oil capsules hoping to keep their brains sharp. These supplements are packed with omega-3 fatty acids — nutrients that help build the connections between brain cells that allow us to remember and learn. Americans spend **over $1 billion** each year on these little pills.

But here is the puzzle: if omega-3s are so good for the brain, why do studies keep giving mixed results? A new two-year study set out to answer that question in the clearest way possible.

## What the scientists actually did

The researchers at **Keck Medicine of USC** wanted to know something simple: when people take fish oil supplements, do the omega-3s actually reach the brain — and if they do, do they help?

They recruited **365 adults** between the ages of 55 and 80. All of them rarely ate fish, so their bodies were likely low on omega-3s. Nearly half — 47% — carried a gene called **APOE4**, which is the strongest known genetic risk factor for Alzheimer's disease later in life.

Each participant took either a daily fish oil supplement or a placebo (a fake pill that looks identical). The study was **double-blind**, meaning neither the participants nor the researchers knew who was taking the real supplement. This helps prevent bias.

## The first check: did the pills work?

The supplements contained **2,000 mg of DHA** — a specific omega-3 fat that is especially important for brain cells. After six months, scientists measured DHA levels in the fluid surrounding the brain and spinal cord.

They found DHA levels had risen by an average of **17%**. The omega-3s had reached their destination.

## The second check: did brains get better?

After two full years, the researchers tested everyone's memory and thinking skills. They also took brain scans to see if the supplements protected against shrinkage of the **hippocampus** — the part of the brain deep inside that helps form memories and is one of the first areas damaged by Alzheimer's.

The results were surprising:

- People who took fish oil supplements did **no better** on memory and thinking tests than those who took the placebo.
- Brain scans showed **no difference** — the supplements did not slow down hippocampal shrinkage.

Even though the omega-3s clearly reached the brain, they provided **no measurable protection** against cognitive decline in this group of at-risk older adults.

## What this means for you

This study does not mean omega-3s are bad for you, or that eating fish is unhealthy. Omega-3s are still essential nutrients — our bodies need them to function. But the results suggest that taking high-dose pills alone may not be enough.

The researchers think omega-3s work best when they are part of a **balanced, healthy diet** — like the Mediterranean diet, which includes fish, vegetables, fruits, whole grains, nuts, and olive oil. That combination of nutrients, working together with exercise and good sleep, may support brain health in ways that a single supplement cannot match.

## Try this at home

Fill a clear glass with water. Drop in a few drops of food coloring and watch how they spread — some colors sink, some swirl, some stay in a tight line. That's a little like how nutrients travel through your brain: not as a single stream, but as many tiny currents that work best when they move together.

## Fun Fact

The brain is about **80% fat** — and about 10% of the fat in your brain is DHA. That is why scientists have long thought fish oil supplements might be like giving your brain premium fuel. But as this study shows, even premium fuel alone does not make a car drive itself — the whole engine matters.
