On a stretch of Interstate 25 near Larkspur, Colorado, something wonderful travels above the traffic every day: a giant green bridge made entirely for animals.

It isn't for cars. It's a wildlife overpass — a leafy path that arches over the highway so deer, elk, bears, and even speedy pronghorn antelope can wander safely to the other side.

## A bridge as big as a city block

The overpass is enormous. It's 200 feet wide and 209 feet long — about the size of two basketball courts lying side by side, or a football field shrunk a little. That's 41,800 square feet of grassy path hovering above the roar of trucks and cars.

Around it, five smaller tunnels burrow under the road for shy creatures who'd rather sneak than stroll. And long fences guide the animals like invisible arrows — funnelling them toward the safe spots instead of darting into traffic.

## Collisions drop by 91%

For years, the Larkspur-to-Monument stretch of I-25 had a sad record: about one animal crash per day. That meant hurt or dead wildlife, scary accidents for drivers, and lots of traffic jams while crews cleaned up.

Now, just 10 months after the overpass opened, the numbers have nearly vanished. Collisions are down 91%. One day's worth of crashes? Now it's less than one crash every eleven days.

"The overpass is working beautifully," said Bridget O'Rourke of Colorado Parks and Wildlife. Wildlife overpasses are statistically proven to be successful — and this one is proving it in real life.

## Even the shy runners are crossing

The best moment came from the most unlikely traveler: a pronghorn antelope, caught on camera trotting confidently across the green bridge.

Pronghorn are born to run, and they migrate long distances. But I-25 has blocked their ancient routes for decades. The recorded crossing in March was the first in 30 years — a sign that the bridge is healing a wound the highway carved long ago.

Small neighbors are using it too: northern leopard frogs, rabbits, and all the quiet hoppers and scurriers that share the meadow.

## More bridges on the way

Success invites more success. Colorado is now planning six new wildlife crossings, including North America's first overpass built just for Rocky Mountain bighorn sheep, perched above I-70 near Vail Pass.

Traffic jams have dropped 9% along the original route — not because cars drive faster, but because there are far fewer sad, bloody interruptions.

## The big takeaway

Sometimes the kindest thing we build is something we never drive on. This green bridge says something simple to every deer, elk, and pronghorn that pauses to look at the road below: *you belong on this side, too*.
