Vitamins are the tiny helpers in food that keep your body working. One of them, vitamin B12, has just been handed a very different job: helping a battery hold on to electricity.

## A battery that uses the air

Most batteries in phones and laptops are lithium-ion. Scientists keep looking for other kinds, because lithium is costly to dig up and hard on the places it comes from.

A team in Spain built a different sort of battery. It is called a zinc-air battery. It uses zinc, a common metal, together with oxygen taken straight out of the air around it.

## Why a vitamin is inside it

The battery needs a helper to make its chemical reaction run in both directions. That is where vitamin B12 comes in. Its other name is cobalamin, and at the very middle of each molecule sits a speck of the metal cobalt.

That speck does the work. When the battery is giving out power, the vitamin helps oxygen turn into water. When the battery is charging up again, it helps the water turn back into oxygen.

There was one snag. Vitamin B12 dissolves in water, and this kind of battery is full of water. So the team tied the vitamin molecules onto tiny carbon tubes, thousands of times thinner than a hair, to keep them in place.

## How long did it last?

In the lab, the small prototype went through about 250 rounds of charging and emptying. That counts as a real step, because earlier versions of the same idea could not be charged up again and again.

It is still only a lab prototype. Nobody is putting it inside a torch or a phone yet. The team says the next jobs are making it last longer and building it bigger.

## Try this at home

Zinc-air batteries use oxygen from the air to make electricity. You can watch a slower, simpler version of the same idea, metal meeting oxygen.

Put a piece of steel wool on a saucer, sprinkle it with water, and leave it for a day. It goes rusty. Rust is what happens when iron grabs oxygen out of the air. Inside the battery, that same grabbing is arranged so it pushes electricity along a wire instead of just making rust. Ask a grown-up to help.

## One more thing

This is not the first try for this research group. They have used other body molecules in batteries twice before. Vitamin B12 is the one that finally gave them a battery they could charge over and over.
