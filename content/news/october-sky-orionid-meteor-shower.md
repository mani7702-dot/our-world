Every month, NASA publishes a short guide called "What's Up" that tells everyone what is worth looking at in the night sky. October's edition has good news: a meteor shower.

## The Orionids

The Orionid meteor shower is active through October and peaks around the 21st. Meteors — the streaks people call shooting stars — are tiny specks of dust burning up high in the atmosphere. The Orionids are special because their dust came from a comet you may have heard of: Halley's comet, which passes Earth once every 76 years.

The meteors appear to come from near the constellation Orion, the hunter, which is how the shower got its name.

## How to watch

You do not need a telescope, or even binoculars. Here is the whole method:

1. Find the darkest spot you can reach — a park, a backyard, anywhere away from street lights.
2. Give your eyes 20 to 30 minutes to adjust to the dark. No phone screens during that time.
3. Lie back and look up. You do not need to stare at Orion itself — meteors can appear anywhere.
4. Be patient. A handful an hour is normal, and each one only lasts a second.

Wrap up warm, take a blanket, and bring someone to lie next to — counting together is half the fun.
