# How NASA took to the sky at a football game

Imagine watching a rocket launch, but instead of a launchpad — it soars right over your stadium. That is exactly what happened at a college football game in Alabama, where NASA brought the Space Age down to earth for thousands of cheering fans.

## A supersonic flyover

On Saturday, October 24, NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman climbed into the cockpit of a supersonic F-5E Tiger II jet, joined two other fighter jets, and roared overhead the University of Alabama stadium in Tuscaloosa. The crowd on the ground fell silent for a moment as the three jets screamed across the sky — then erupted in cheers as the sound caught up seconds later.

This was no ordinary flyover. It was part of NASA's Inspiration Tour, a travelling show that takes the wonders of space exploration to towns and cities that never get a front-row seat to rocket science.

## What is the Inspiration Tour?

The Inspiration Tour is NASA's way of saying "come see what we are building." Instead of a museum, NASA brings the museum to you. At this Alabama stop, the NASA Experience Zone set up outside the stadium with:

- A giant **inflatable SLS rocket** — the same kind that will carry astronauts to the Moon
- Displays of **Starship** and **Blue Moon** lunar landers — the vehicles designed to land humans on the Moon again
- Hands-on exhibits where kids could try on astronaut gloves and pilot a virtual spacecraft

NASA hopes these stops inspire the next generation of explorers — the engineers who will build the next generation of spacecraft, and the astronauts who will ride them.

## Why meet fans at a football game?

Football games bring together tens of thousands of people in one place. NASA wanted to reach kids and families who might not visit a space center or watch rocket launches online. By showing up where people already gather, NASA makes space feel closer — like it belongs to everyone, not just scientists in lab coats.

"Space exploration is for everyone," said Isaacman. "And there is no better place to share that excitement than where communities come together."

As the F-5 jets climbed back into the clouds, the crowd gave them one last wave. It was a moment that reminded everyone: the frontier of space is not as far away as it seems.

## Try this at home

You can make your own "flyover" with a balloon and a flashlight! In a dark room, turn off all the lights and shine a flashlight upward at an angle — just like a jet's searchlight. Then let a balloon drift slowly across the beam. To make it more dramatic, ask an adult to help you tape a small LED light to the balloon so it glows like a spacecraft passing overhead.

## Fun fact

The F-5E Tiger II jets used in the Inspiration Tour are the same model that the U.S. Navy used for decades as a supersonic trainer. NASA operates a special version for high-altitude research flights — they can fly faster than **1,300 kilometers per hour** and reach altitudes of nearly **16 kilometers** above the ground.
