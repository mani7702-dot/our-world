# 1. How did 20% of Switzerland's glacier ice vanish in just five years?

## The giants that are silently shrinking

High in the Swiss Alps, some of Earth's most ancient rivers of ice are disappearing before our very eyes. Over the past five years alone, **nearly 20% of Switzerland's total glacier ice has melted away** — an amount greater than all the ice in some small countries combined.

The worst year was 2026, when the country's glaciers lost more than **5% of their total ice volume** in a single summer. The famous **Aletsch Glacier** — the longest glacier in the Alps at 22.6 kilometers (14 miles) long — and other giants like the **Rhône** and **Clariden** glaciers all experienced their most severe melting on record.

## What happens when the snow won't fall

The key culprit is a deadly combination: **far too little snow** and **far too much heat**. The winter of 2025 to 2026 ranked among Switzerland's ten least snowy winters on record, leaving the glaciers with thin blankets of protection. Then came a summer with heat so persistent that the **freezing level climbed above 4,000 meters (13,000 feet) on 76 days** — more than twice the normal number.

By September, even the highest peaks were bare. Snow that normally shields glacier ice at elevations of 3,500 meters simply never arrived. Without that white blanket, the sun's rays struck the blue ice directly, and the melting accelerated.

Two smaller glaciers — **Bella Tola** in canton Valais and **Griessfirn** in canton Glarus — vanished completely, their ices gone for good.

## Why glacier melt matters to everyone

You might wonder why melting mountaintop ice matters if you don't live in Switzerland. The answer is water. Between July and September 2026, Switzerland's glaciers released about **2.2 trillion liters of meltwater** — more than four times the yearly drinking water needs of all Swiss households. But here's the catch: that water comes from a shrinking reservoir.

As the glaciers continue to shrink, they can provide less and less water during dry summer months. The same process is happening across the Alps, where a 2009 survey found that 76 out of 89 monitored Swiss glaciers were retreating — a trend that has only accelerated since.

## Try this at home

Fill a clear cup with ice cubes and a little water. Leave it in a warm place and watch it melt. Now cover a second cup of ice with a thick wool sock — that is your "snow blanket." Compare how fast each cup melts. The sock slows the melt just like fresh snow protects glacier ice from the sun.

## Fun Fact

The **Grosser Aletsch Glacier** has been retreating since the end of the last ice age. It shrank back by **2.8 kilometers (1.7 miles)** between 1880 and 2009 alone — and the rate of retreat has only grown faster in recent decades.

## for-grown-ups

Switzerland's glaciers lost over 5% of their ice volume in 2026, with the GLAMOS observatory network (ETH Zurich, University of Fribourg, University of Zurich) reporting that nearly 20% of total glacier volume has disappeared over the past five years (2021–2026). The 2025–2026 winter was among the ten least snowy on record, and summer 2026 saw 76 days above the freezing level at 4,000+ m — a new record. Smaller glaciers Bella Tola and Griessfirn vanished entirely. The Aletsch Glacier (22.6 km, longest in the Alps) remains retreating per Wikipedia (status: "Retreating" since 1850). These observations confirm accelerating Alpine glacier loss consistent with long-term climate trends documented in the "Retreat of glaciers since 1850" literature (76 of 89 Swiss glaciers retreating as of 2009 surveys).
