# How Does a Forest Come Back to Life After a Wildfire?

## 1. A Huge Fire in the Scottish Highlands

Last summer, a wildfire burned across about **10 square miles (25 square kilometres)** of forest and moorland in the **Cairngorms National Park** in Scotland, near a village called **Aviemore**. At its busiest, more than **500 firefighters** worked together to stop the flames. When the last crew finally went home in August, everyone wondered the same thing: would the forest ever grow back?

## 2. Nature's Comeback Crew

**Forestry and Land Scotland** is the team that looks after the nation's forests — and its rangers have been walking the burned ground. They found good news:

- **Birch, rowan and willow** trees are regrowing from stems and roots that stayed alive underground.
- **Grasses, bracken and blaeberry** are turning the hillsides green again.
- **Deer and capercaillie** (a big, rare woodland bird) have returned, feeding on the fresh new grass.

Rangers say the green is thickest where the soil stayed damp, or where the fire was less fierce.

## 3. The Ancient "Granny Pines"

But not everything bounces back so easily. The park is famous for its **Scots pine** trees — some called "granny pines" because they are more than **100 or even 200 years old**. Fire may have damaged many of these giants. Old pines are precious because they drop the **seeds** that grow the next generation of trees, and they give shelter to wildlife such as the capercaillie. Forester **Eelco de Jong** warned that if the oldest pines are lost, it could take **centuries** for trees of the same size to return. Layers of rich soil were damaged too, which makes recovery harder.

## 4. How Forests Heal

Wildfires are frightening, but some natural forests actually depend on fire to clear away old growth and let sunlight reach the ground. The Cairngorms is showing both sides of the story: quick regrowth from tough roots, and a slow, patient wait for the ancient pines. Rangers will keep watching, replanting where they can, and giving nature a helping hand.

### Try this at home

Grow your own mini-forest! Put a few bean or pea seeds in a pot of soil, water them, and leave them on a sunny windowsill. Keep a diary of how tall they grow each day — then imagine waiting 100 years for a tree to reach its full size!

### Fun Fact

Some pine cones only open and release their seeds when they feel the **heat of a fire**. That way the new seeds land on freshly cleared, sunny ground — perfect for growing.
