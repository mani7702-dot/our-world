## A video message from orbit

Imagine getting a video call from space — but the person on screen is a real astronaut floating in a spaceship!

That is exactly what happened to a group of primary school children in Plymouth, England. Their teacher, Emily Trevarthen, got a special surprise video message from British astronaut Tim Peake.

Peake told the pupils: "I hear you have been studying all about space, a subject very close to my heart, something I love, so I am very pleased that you enjoy it too. Keep looking up at the stars."

## How did it happen?

It all started over the summer. Emily's father was on a cruise ship and happened to strike up a conversation with Tim Peake. During their chat, her father mentioned that Emily teaches young children.

A few months later, the surprise arrived. The video was kept top secret until World Space Week — a celebration of all things space — when the school showed it during a special assembly.

Emily said: "We did not expect to get what we got really."

## A British hero in space

Some of the children had thought all astronauts were American. But meeting Tim Peake — a proud British astronaut — helped them realise that anyone can reach for the stars, no matter where they are from.

Peake first flew to space in 2015 as part of his Principia mission. He spent six months on the International Space Station, where he even ran the London Marathon on a treadmill floating in orbit!

The children were so excited that some of them first wondered if the video was made using artificial intelligence. When they realised it was really him, they were starstruck.

Teacher Joanna Stelmaszczyk said: "I remember it like it was yesterday. I was teaching Year 1 at the time, and the buzz and excitement in the classroom was something I will never forget."

## Try this at home

Ask an adult to help you watch a real astronaut video message online. You can search for "Tim Peake ISS" or "Space Station live" on a kid-safe video site. See if you can spot Earth spinning below — that is the real planet we live on, seen from 400 kilometres above us.

## Fun fact

Tim Peake ran the entire London Marathon — 26.2 miles — while floating inside the International Space Station, strapped to a treadmill. He is the second person in history to run a marathon from space!
