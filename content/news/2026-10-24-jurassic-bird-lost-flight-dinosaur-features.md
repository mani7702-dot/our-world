# How did early birds learn to live on the ground instead of in the sky?

![Fossil bird Confuciusornis sanctus, a close relative of early Jurassic birds. Photo: CC BY 2.0 James St. John via Wikimedia Commons](assets/images/2026-10-24-jurassic-bird-lost-flight-dinosaur-features.jpg)

## A surprising mix of bird and dinosaur

About 150 million years ago, in what is now southeastern China, a crow-sized bird took a very different path from its flying cousins. Scientists have discovered a new species called **Yuezhuornis yangyuanensis** — and this bird had features that look like they belong more on a *Jurassic Park* predator than on a gentle feathered flyer.

Yuezhuornis had a bird-like body built for walking, but its face was full of **sharp, serrated teeth** — just like meat-eating dinosaurs. Its second toe curled backward with a **sickle-shaped claw**, the same weapon that dromaeosaurids (the "raptor" dinosaurs) used to hunt. It is one of the strangest combinations ever found: a bird that still carried its dinosaur ancestors' toolkit.

## Why losing flight can be an advantage

For a long time, scientists thought the story of bird evolution was simple: small feathered dinosaurs gradually became better and better at flying. But Yuezhuornis tells a different tale. This bird appears to have **lost its ability to fly**, choosing instead to live on the ground.

By measuring its leg and wing bones and comparing them to modern birds, researchers found that Yuezhuornis's proportions closely match those of birds that **run or walk on the ground** — like chickens or ostriches — rather than birds built for soaring through the sky.

## What this means for bird history

The discovery shows that early bird evolution was far more diverse than once believed. Some ancient birds didn't just get better at flying — they gave up on flight entirely, adapting to life on the forest floor. This tells us that the age of dinosaurs was also an age of **experimentation**: birds were trying many different ways of living, not just one.

The findings were published in the journal *Nature* by scientists from the Chinese Academy of Sciences and the Fujian Institute of Geological Sciences, who found the fossil in the newly recognized Zhenghe Fauna of Fujian Province.

## Try this at home

Look around your neighborhood for birds that cannot fly. Can you find a **chicken** in a farmyard or a **pigeon** on the ground? Notice how their wings are smaller and their legs stronger compared to a sparrow or a crow. Just like Yuezhuornis, these birds have chosen running over flying!

## Fun Fact

The name *Yuezhuornis* means "Yuezhuo bird" — named after the area in China where the fossil was found. The species name *yangyuanensis* means "from Yangyuan," a nearby county.

## for-grownups

Yuezhuornis yangyuanensis lived during the Late Jurassic (150–148 million years ago). It is placed within Avialae (the lineage leading to modern birds) as a sister taxon to Zhengheornis and just crownward of Archaeopteryx. Phylogenetic analyses used both maximum parsimony and Bayesian tip-dating methods. Its combination of retained dinosaurian features (teeth, sickle claw) with avian features (pelvic structure) indicates that flight loss evolved independently at least once in early avian history, and that ecological diversification — not linear flight improvement — characterized early bird evolution.
