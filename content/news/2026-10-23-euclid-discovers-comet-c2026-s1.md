# How did a dark-energy telescope spot a comet 1.3 billion km away?

## A telescope with a surprise discovery

In July 2023, the European Space Agency launched **Euclid** — a satellite with a lofty mission: photograph **billions of distant galaxies** and uncover the mysteries of **dark energy** and **dark matter**. But in September 2026, while scanning the heavens for ancient light, Euclid noticed something much closer: a **comet** moving across its view.

## A streak in deep space

Mikko Pöntinen, a researcher at the University of Helsinki, had written new software to hunt for anomalies. His program flagged a faint streak across several images from September 16 to 24, 2026 — the signature of something moving fast. Around the streak he saw a fuzzy glow — a **coma** of dust and gas that forms when a comet's ices vaporize. Ground-based telescopes confirmed the find, and the Minor Planet Center named it **C/2026 S1 (Euclid)**.

## Ancient ice from the Solar System's childhood

Comets are icy fossils from when our Solar System was young — leftover chunks of ice, dust, and rock that never became planets. Most live in the **Oort Cloud**, a distant shell of debris trillions of kilometers from the Sun.

C/2026 S1 is currently about **1.3 billion km from Earth** — past Jupiter's orbit toward Saturn. As a long-period comet, it likely takes decades to complete one orbit, having journeyed from the outer Solar System's deep freeze.

## An accidental win for science

Euclid was not built to find comets. Its camera is optimized for seeing incredibly faint galaxies billions of light-years away. Spotting a nearby comet is like finding a firefly in a lighthouse beam. Still, the discovery shows how astronomy thrives on happy accidents.

## Try this at home

Fill a clear glass with cold water and add a drop of milk. Shine a flashlight through the side — the beam will glow, showing milk particles scattering light. The glowing cloud is like your comet's coma, and the flashlight beam is sunlight pushing material away. Tilt the glass and watch the "tail" stretch — just like a real comet!

## Fun Fact

The name "Euclid" is a playful nod to the ancient Greek mathematician who described the geometry of space — fitting, since the telescope measures how dark energy and dark matter bend spacetime.

**for-grownups:** The European Space Agency's Euclid space telescope launched on July 1, 2023 from Cape Canaveral aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9, heading to the Sun-Earth L2 point 1.5 million km away. Its mission studies dark energy and dark matter by observing ~1.5 billion galaxies. Comet C/2026 S1 (Euclid) was discovered by Mikko Pöntinen using software that detected a moving streak in September 2026 images. The Minor Planet Center confirmed and named it; the comet orbits ~8.7 AU from Earth, between Jupiter and Saturn.
