# Who Were the Three Forgotten Kings of Ancient Assyria?

For 100 years, historians thought they knew every king who ever ruled ancient Assyria. They had a long clay tablet — the **Assyrian King List** — that listed ruler after ruler in neat order. It seemed complete.

But what if the list was wrong? What if some kings were erased on purpose — scrubbed from history by the winners?

## A King Vanishes After a Solar Eclipse

One mystery king appears only on a single clay tablet now kept in the British Museum. He took the name **Tiglath-pileser** — the same name as a famous king from earlier centuries — and ruled around **763 BCE**.

That same year, something dramatic happened: a **solar eclipse** darkened the sky. In the ancient world, a sudden eclipse was a powerful omen — a sign from the gods. It could make people doubt their ruler's divine right to govern.

This forgotten Tiglath-pileser tried to seize power from his own nephew, the official king. For one year, there may have been **two rulers** claiming the throne at the same time. Then he was removed — replaced by the nephew he had tried to overthrow.

## The Stone That Was Overwritten

A second missing king is known from a stone monument (called a stela) discovered in 1894. Someone chiseled the name of one Assyrian ruler off the stone and carved a **different name** in its place.

Why? Perhaps the new king wanted to erase his predecessor — to make it look like he had always been in charge. It is like crossing a name out of a photograph and painting a new one over it.

## A Rival in the Shadows

The third forgotten ruler appears in records that mention a royal grant — a document giving land or money to a temple or official. But his name never made it into the official King List.

## The Mystery of the Missing Names

Together, these three discoveries suggest that ancient Assyria was more chaotic than the clean King List admits. Real history includes rival rulers, family feuds, and name-chasing on stone.

The ancient scribes wrote down what they were told. But real power struggles — and the winners who rewrote records — left gaps that modern archaeologists must fill in.

## Try This at Home

Look for "forgotten" names in your own house. Write a list of everyone who has lived in your home — past and present. Now imagine one name was erased. Who might have been "scrubbed" from your home's story? You are a historian now!

## Fun Fact

The Assyrian King List itself is a **real archaeological artifact** — a clay tablet over 2,500 years old. It is written in **cuneiform** (angled wedge-shaped marks pressed into wet clay), which is how we know so much about what happened in that corner of the ancient world.
