# Why is October's Moon called the Hunter's Moon?

*Report filed at 13:25 IST from the Our World news desk.*

## A name from the old calendar

Our ancestors did not have streetlights or alarm clocks. Instead, they told time by the Moon, giving each full Moon a name that matched what was happening in nature. October's full Moon is the **Hunter's Moon** — so named because it arrived at the season when hunters stocked up on food for the long, cold winter ahead. The extra evening light the Moon gives was a welcome gift for anyone working outdoors after sunset.

## Why the Moon looks so big and orange

You may have noticed something strange: the Moon looks huge and reddish when it sits low on the horizon, but smaller and paler when it climbs higher. Does the Moon really change size? **Not really!** This trick is called the **Moon Illusion** — your brain is playing a game with your eyes.

When the Moon is near the horizon, your brain compares it to trees, rooftops, and hills. It thinks, "That must be a giant Moon!" But when the Moon is high up in the dark sky with nothing nearby to compare it to, your brain judges it to be smaller. Astronomers have measured it again and again — the Moon takes up almost exactly the same amount of space on the sky no matter where it is.

The orange colour has a simple explanation, too: sunlight passing through a thick blanket of atmosphere on the horizon scatters away the blue and green light, letting the reddish-orange colours reach your eyes.

## How long does it last?

A full Moon is not just one night — it fills the sky almost every evening for about three days. The Moon is officially "full" for only a single instant (in 2026 that was at 04:11 GMT on Monday, 26 October), but from Earth it still looks round and bright for two full nights. Some cultures call the Hunter's Moon special because it rises unusually late in the evening for several nights running, staying visible well past sunrise — a longer glow than most full Moons give us.

## Try this at home

Stand outside at sunset with a friend and both hold up a thumb, side by side. When the Moon is low, your thumb will cover it easily. When the Moon climbs higher, your thumb still covers it just the same. That little experiment proves the Moon isn't really growing or shrinking — it just looks that way because of your clever brain!

## Fun Fact

The Hunter's Moon is followed by **three more supermoons** in a row. A true supermoon happens when the full Moon is closest to Earth in its egg-shaped orbit, making it look slightly bigger and brighter than usual. So the sky will stay busy with big moons clear into 2027!
