Peggy-Sue is a Bulldog with a name made for a song — and a story that shows just how tough rescue dogs can be.

When she first arrived at the **RSPCA West Hatch Animal Centre** in Somerset, England, in the summer of 2025, something was very wrong. She weighed **32.5 kilograms** — that is about **70 pounds** — which is roughly the same weight as a **baby hippo**. And Peggy-Sue could only walk a few steps before she had to sit down and rest.

## Why she could not walk

A Bulldog is built low and sturdy, but Peggy-Sue was carrying far too much weight for her body. Each step was hard work, and after just a few steps she would **collapse**. The shelter team had to carry her in a crate from the van to her kennel. She also had problems that come with extra weight: an **ingrown claw**, **infected skin folds** (the wrinkles on a Bulldog's face trap moisture and germs), and a body that got **too hot too fast**.

## How the slow-and-steady plan worked

Instead of trying to lose weight quickly (which can hurt a dog's body), Peggy-Sue went to live with a **foster carer** who knew good results come from patience. The plan was simple but slow:

1. **Walk a little farther each day** — no rushing.
2. **Eat smaller, measured meals** — a strict diet plan.
3. **Check her skin and folds** every day so they stayed clean and dry.

Bit by bit, with every extra step and every measured bowl, Peggy-Sue got stronger. Her weight went down, her skin got better, and the summer heat stopped being dangerous for her.

## A happy ending — and a new friend

Last autumn, during the RSPCA's **Adoptober** drive, Peggy-Sue met her new family: **Emmie and Andy** from Taunton. She now weighs a much healthier **23.5 kilograms** — twenty fewer pounds — and she loves the things Bulldogs do best: splashing in **rock pools and streams**, racing through woods, and curling up on the sofa with a best buddy (a Jack Russell whose older brother Mr Woo had passed away).

Peggy-Sue's story is a reminder that rescue dogs can rewrite their whole future when they get the time and care they deserve.

**Try this at home:** Next time you walk a dog, notice how they move. Are they bouncy and steady, or do they lag behind? That tells you their body feels good — and it costs nothing to say hello to a shelter dog who just needs a patient family.

**Fun Fact:** A baby hippo weighs about 33 kg (73 lb) at birth. An adult Bulldog should weigh 18–25 kg (40–55 lb). That means baby hippos and fully-grown Bulldogs can share a bathroom scale!
