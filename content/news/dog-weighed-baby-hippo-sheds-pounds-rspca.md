# How did a dog the size of a baby hippo get healthy enough for a forever home?

Some dogs arrive at animal shelters carrying more than just baggage — they carry extra weight that makes every day a struggle.

Peggy Sue was a bulldog who tipped the scales at **70 pounds** (32.5 kg). To put that in perspective, an adult English Bulldog should weigh about **50 to 55 pounds**. That means Peggy Sue was carrying almost a whole bag of dog food extra — as much weight as a baby hippo calf.

She was so heavy that **she could barely walk**. Every step was hard work. She got infections, overheated easily, and sometimes collapsed just trying to take a few steps. When she first arrived at the RSPCA's West Hatch Animal Centre in Somerset, England, the staff had to carry her in a crate and move her around by hand.

## A slow plan that actually works

Quick fixes don't work for an overweight dog — especially a Bulldog, whose short snout means they can overheat fast. So Peggy Sue's foster family took a patient, slow approach:

1. **Start tiny** — short, gentle walks to build muscles without stressing joints  
2. **Portion control** — a measured diet to burn fat while keeping nutrition right  
3. **Celebrate small wins** — five kilograms (11 pounds) lost before she even found her forever home  

The RSPCA Deputy Centre Manager, Anita Clark, said seeing Peggy Sue struggle broke her heart. But she also said the slow progress — day by day — made all the difference.

## A happy ending in Taunton

After months of steady improvement, Peggy Sue was ready for adoption during the RSPCA's "Adoptober" drive. Her new owners, Emmie and Andy from Taunton, were ready with a warm home — and a surprise: a Jack Russell mix who had just lost his 16-year-old brother, Mr. Woo.

"He also gained a best buddy," Andy laughed. Peggy Sue's new weight sits at a much healthier **23.5 kg** (about 52 pounds) — down twenty pounds from when she started her journey.

When she first came home, she only had "small bursts of energy," Emmie said. But slowly, day by day, she grew stronger. Now she loves her walks, her trips in the car, and yes — even rock pools and streams, because she's a "water-baby" at heart.

"Rehoming a rescue dog is the best thing you could ever do!" her new family says with a smile.

## Try this at home

- **If YOUR dog is carrying extra weight:** Never diet without checking with your vet first. Bulldogs especially need careful weight management.
- **Foster dogs need patience:** It takes time for an abused or neglected dog to learn to trust. Small kindnesses — a soft bed, a quiet walk, a gentle voice — matter more than treats.
- **Adopt, don't shop:** Every shelter has a Peggy Sue waiting for a chance.

## Fun Fact

Adult English Bulldogs typically weigh 40 to 50 pounds. At 70 pounds, Peggy Sue was carrying the equivalent weight of a medium-sized child — every step was like hiking with a heavy backpack!
