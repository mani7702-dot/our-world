## A bridge just for wildlife

Imagine trying to cross a six-lane highway where cars zoom past at 75 miles an hour. Now imagine being a **pronghorn antelope** — a deer — or a **bear** doing exactly that, every single day.

That is what animals near **Larkspur, Colorado** faced when Interstate 25 cut through their forest home. So engineers built something special: not a bridge for people, but a bridge for **wildlife**.

Measuring **200 feet wide** and **209 feet long** — that is bigger than two basketball courts side by side — the overpass stretches across all six lanes of traffic. But it does not stop there. Five hidden **underground tunnels** snake beneath the road for smaller, shyer animals who would rather stay in the shadows. Thousands of yards of sturdy fence guide every creature to the safe crossing points, like an invisible highway system for wildlife.

## The numbers speak

When the overpass opened in **December 2025**, Colorado officials had a bold prediction: collisions between vehicles and animals should drop by **90%**. Ten months later, the real-world result is even better — **91% fewer crashes**.

Before the bridge, the stretch of I-25 between Larkspur and Monument saw an average of **one animal-vehicle collision per day**. That is 365 incidents a year — each one a tragedy for the animal, a terrifying moment for the driver, and an expensive cleanup for the highway patrol.

Now? Thanks to fewer accidents, traffic delays have fallen by **9%**. Paramedics are called less often. And the animals are making it across safely.

## A first in 30 years

Pronghorn antelope are born with an internal GPS that has guided their migrations for thousands of years. But I-25 became an uncrossable wall. For **30 years**, no pronghorn was recorded making it to the other side.

Then came the overpass. A camera caught the first pronghorn trotting across the bridge — the first one in three decades. It was a moment that wildlife biologists had been waiting for their entire careers.

Bears, elk, moose, and even the occasional northern leopard frog have all been caught on camera using the crossing. Bridget O'Rourke from Colorado Parks and Wildlife said the team is "thrilled" — the bridges and tunnels have been "statistically proven" to work.

## A movement grows

The success in Colorado is inspiring other states. Planners are now scouting **six more locations** for wildlife bridges, including the first design built specifically for **Rocky Mountain bighorn sheep**. New overpasses are being planned along Interstate 70 near Vail Pass, near the New Mexico border on I-25, and along US Highway 287 near Wyoming.

It turns out that when concrete and nature work together, everyone wins.

## Fun fact

The word "overpass" usually makes us think of a bridge we drive over. But wildlife overpasses are built so that animals walk *on top* of the bridge while cars drive *underneath* — it is a road that goes underground and a meadow that stays on top.

### Try this at home

Draw a big road on a piece of paper with dashed lines. Now draw little animal figures that need to get to the other side. Build a bridge using a ruler — and add tiny fences (lines) that guide the animals to safe crossing points. You just designed your own wildlife overpass!
