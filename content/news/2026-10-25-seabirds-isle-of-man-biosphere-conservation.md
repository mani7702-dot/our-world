# How did seabirds bounce back on the Isle of Man — and why are sewage and storms still a challenge?

## A whole island tries to be a wildlife haven

Imagine if your entire home — every street, field, and beach — was declared a protected nature zone. That is exactly what happened on the **Isle of Man** in 2016, when **UNESCO** named it the world's first *whole-nation* Biosphere Reserve. Not just a park or a stretch of coast, but the whole island and all the sea around it.

After **ten years**, the island published its very first *"State of the Biosphere"* report — and the news has two sides, like a coin showing both heads and tails.

## A win for birds who nest in the dark

On a tiny island called the **Calf of Man**, off the southern tip, there is a remarkable comeback story. The **Manx shearwater** — a seabird that looks like a soft brown ghost in flight — was nearly wiped out here by rats that stole their eggs and chicks from underground burrows.

But conservationists removed the rats (locally called "longtails"), and suddenly the shearwaters came back. Today, about **1,500 breeding pairs** return each summer to raise their young in the safety of the island's cliffs and grassy burrows.

The Manx shearwater is a superstar traveler: it flies thousands of miles each year from its breeding grounds to winter feeding grounds off South America. And it does all this at night, navigating by starlight, using its incredible night vision to find the burrow entrance in the dark. But even with their local comeback, **12 of the island's 16 breeding seabird species** still appear on the Red List of greatest conservation concern.

## Plants come home, but salmon still struggle

Not everything is about birds. On land, a native shrub called **juniper** — thought lost from the island since 1947 — has been carefully replanted and is now showing "encouraging survival and growth."

In the water, nine new **Marine Nature Reserves** now protect more than half the sea within three nautical miles of the coast. But despite all this protection, young **Atlantic salmon** in local rivers keep declining — scientists say climate change is bringing more floods and droughts, which disrupts their spawning.

And then there is the sewage problem: the report admits that untreated sewage is *still* being discharged into the sea at Peel and Garff, even as new treatment works are being built. Being a Biosphere means facing your problems honestly — the report says its "credibility depends partly on being prepared to admit where it is not working."

## What happens next?

UNESCO will decide in **summer 2027** whether the Isle of Man keeps its Biosphere status. The island is not asking for a free pass — it is asking for a little more time to solve the hard problems, like cleaning up the sea and saving the salmon.

## Try this at home

**Build a mini island in a tray.** Fill a shallow dish with sand, then add small rocks for cliffs and a tiny cave (use a yogurt cup with a hole). Place cotton balls as "seabird burrows" in the grassy area. Sprinkle a few drops of blue food coloring around the edges — that's the sea. Now, place tiny toy animals (a bird, a seal, a fish) in different spots. Ask: which spots are safest from "rats" (use a small toy rat)? Move the rat around and see how the animals react. This shows how one invasive species can threaten many native ones — and why removing just that one thing can help the whole island bounce back.

## Fun Fact

The Manx shearwater is one of the **longest-lived birds on Earth**. Some individuals have been tracked breeding for **over 50 years** — that is longer than most of us have been alive! A single square kilometer of good seabird nesting habitat can host hundreds of these ghost-like birds, all flying the same ancient migration routes that their great-great-grandparents flew centuries ago.
