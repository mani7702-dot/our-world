# What was the fattest prehistoric squid?

## A barrel-shaped beast from an ancient sea

Imagine a sea creature shaped like a plump cigar, swimming through warm shallow waters that are now dry land in Wyoming. That is **Wyoteuthis linsterorum** — a brand-new species of prehistoric squid relative that scientists just named.

It lived about **160 million years ago**, during a time called the Jurassic Period. Back then, what is now Wyoming was covered by a shallow inland sea. Huge dinosaurs walked the land, and the oceans were full of creatures that look nothing like anything alive today.

## Why is it so chunky?

Wyoteuthis belongs to a group called **belemnites** — squid-like animals that had a hard, bullet-shaped internal shell (sort of like a tiny squid backbone made of stone). Most belemnites had thin, sleek shells. But this one is different. Its shell is unusually **thick and barrel-shaped**, making it the **"fattest" belemnite** ever found.

Scientists think its chunky body might have helped it dive deep, swim fast, or even store extra energy. Nobody knows for sure yet — every fossil teaches us something new!

## A predator in the ancient ocean

Based on its sharp hooks and strong beak-like mouth parts, Wyoteuthis was likely a **predator**. It probably hunted smaller squid-like animals that swam in the same ancient seas. That makes it a bit like today's Humboldt squid — fierce and built for hunting.

## Found by people who love fossils

Two extraordinary fossils were discovered — one by a serious fossil collector named Jason K. Stover, and the other by a young enthusiast named Nathaniel S. Lindsey. The research was led by an international team including **Dr. Dean Lomax** from the University of Manchester, who said the creature's nickname among the team is "the big chunky belemnite."

Their findings were published in the scientific journal **Papers in Palaeontology**. The name *Wyoteuthis linsterorum* honors the Linster family, who have supported paleontology research.

## Why do scientists still care?

Belemnites went extinct about 65 million years ago, but every new species we find helps us understand how ocean life changed over time. This chunky squid shows us that belemnites were more diverse than we thought — and some of them kept certain body shapes alive longer than scientists expected.

Next time you visit a museum and see a belemnite "bullet" fossil in a glass case, remember: inside that simple stone was an animal as strange and wonderful as any dragon.

## Fun Fact

The word "belemnite" comes from the Greek *belemma*, meaning "dart" or "arrow" — because the fossilized internal shell often looks like a small stone arrow!

## Try This at Home

- Look for "bullet" fossils at a natural history museum. Belemnites are among the most common fossils found in the UK and the western United States.
- Draw your own prehistoric squid! Give it a silly shape — real fossils come in all sizes and squiggles.
- Learn the Jurassic period by reading about dinosaurs, then imagine the oceans they lived beside.
