# How did two caged brown bears travel 5 years and 2 continents to find a forest home in Ireland?

## 1 — What were the bears' homes like before rescue?

Imagine being too big to stand up in your bedroom. That was daily life for **Tyapa**, a brown bear who lived in a cage only 2.5 metres long, 2.5 metres wide, and 2 metres tall — with no roof to shield him from scorching summers or freezing winters. He had no water dish and drank only from a hose when someone could reach him. The worst part? He was being raised to be killed later for taxidermy.

**Bu** had an equally hard beginning. She was forced to perform in a circus, then spent years locked in a tiny indoor cage with barely enough room to move. Both bears grew thin, hurt, and showed signs of sadness that come from loneliness and fear.

## 2 — Who heard their call for help?

In 2021, **Killian McLaughlin** — founder of Wild Ireland, a sanctuary in County Donegal, Ireland — learned about the two bears. He promised to bring them to safety, starting a rescue that would span five years and two continents.

The plan: drive the bears out of Russia, through Ukraine, and into Ireland. That route became impossible in February 2022 when Russia invaded Ukraine. So Killian found a new path — through the Middle East — but conflict flared again in February 2026, closing those skies.

## 3 — How did 100 people help complete the journey?

With no direct flights between Russia and the EU, the team got creative. They partnered with **Intradco Global** and **Etihad Cargo**, who flew the bears from Moscow to Dublin at a greatly reduced cost. Russian veterinarians **Dima and Yana Kim** of Tiger House sanctuary near St. Petersburg drove 36 hours one way to cut the bears from their rusted cages and nurse them back to health.

On **3 August 2026**, Bu and Tyapa landed in Ireland. After a short rest, they travelled by cattle lorry to Wild Ireland, where they stepped into a world they had never known: forests, streams, and room to roam.

## 4 — How are they doing now?

Since arriving, both bears have gained healthy weight, their fur coats have improved, and blood tests show their kidneys — once damaged by years of dehydration — are healing well. They now wander the woodland sanctuary alongside monkeys, wolves, and lynx, living the life they always deserved.

## Fun Fact

European brown bears can live up to **30 years** in the wild. They are excellent swimmers and can cover more than 6 miles a day when roaming. They also have an incredible sense of smell — about **1,000 times stronger** than a human's — which they use to sniff out berries, fish, and roots.
