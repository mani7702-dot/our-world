# Why should I stockpile and what will I need?

When big storms roll in, when the power goes out for a few days, or when something unexpected happens, grown-ups want to be sure their homes have what they need. That is why governments ask families to keep a small “go-bag” of easy supplies ready. You do not need a bunker — just three days of the basics so life stays calm while help arrives.

## The three-day rule

The idea is simple: keep enough food, water, and everyday helpers to last 72 hours — about three days. In the UK, the “Be Ready” campaign recommends bottled or stored water and non-perishable foods such as tins and dried fruit. Across the Atlantic, America’s “Think 72 Hours” plan suggests about one gallon of water per person per day. Both agree on the same point: if a storm, flood, or power cut stops shops from restocking, you still have what matters.

## What goes in the kit?

A good starter kit fits in a backpack or a sturdy box and includes:

- Water (a few bottles or jugs per person)
- Non-perishable food (tins, crackers, dried fruit, nuts)
- A torch or flashlight with spare batteries
- A battery-powered or hand-crank radio
- First-aid supplies and any daily medicines
- Some cash in small notes
- A whistle to call for help and a wrench to turn off utilities if needed

Everything should stay in one easy-to-carry place so grown-ups can grab it fast.

## Why prepare at all?

Bad weather is becoming more common, and power or internet lines can go down for days. Emergency teams do their best, but if every house has its own tiny stash, helpers can focus on the trickier jobs. Countries such as Sweden, France, and Germany have been doing this for years — and now more places are joining in, because being ready makes the whole neighborhood stronger.

## Try this at home

Ask a grown-up to help you build a mini emergency kit for your toys. Use a shoebox, add a “water bottle” made from a tiny plastic bag, some “food” drawn on paper, and a paper torch. Label each piece. You will see how simple it is to be ready!

## Fun Fact

The longest power outage in modern history happened in India in 2012, when more than 600 million people lost electricity for several days. In homes with water stored and candles ready, families still had light, warmth, and a drink — proof that a little planning goes a long way.
