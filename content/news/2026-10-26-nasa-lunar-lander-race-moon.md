# 1 — When will SpaceX and Blue Origin send humans to the Moon?

NASA is on a tight schedule to land astronauts on the Moon again. At a space conference in early October 2026, the agency revealed exactly what tests each company must finish before their lunar lander can carry people. Here is the race.

## 2 — SpaceX's Starship: refuel in orbit and land before 2028

SpaceX's Starship is a towering, fully reusable rocket and spacecraft. The biggest challenge for Moon missions is that a Starship needs to be *refueled* in Earth orbit first — imagine filling up dozens of tanker trucks to top off one spaceship before it can travel all the way to the Moon.

NASA wants SpaceX to demonstrate its first-ever **orbital propellant transfer** in the coming months, followed by an uncrewed landing attempt on the Moon by **late 2027**. The company's latest test flight on September 28, 2026 already nudged Starship one step closer to orbit.

NASA's timeline for SpaceX's lunar Starship (per the Oct 5 briefing):

- **Late 2026:** Complete the first orbital fuel-transfer demo and build a **crew cabin simulator** (a mock-up astronauts will train in, not the real cabin).
- **Q1 2027:** Deliver the cabin simulator to NASA and finish integrating communications systems with Orion.
- **Q2 2027:** Complete a landing-leg demonstration and begin crew-cabin airlock tests.
- **Q3–Q4 2027:** Launch **one uncrewed lunar landing** mission to prove the lander can touch down and rise again.

When all that succeeds, NASA will pick a date for astronauts to ride Starship to the lunar surface as part of **Artemis IV**.

## 3 — Blue Origin's Blue Moon: New Glenn, BE-7 engines, and a pad rebuild

Blue Origin is betting on two key technologies: its **New Glenn** heavy-lift rocket and the **Blue Moon MK2** lunar lander — a 52-foot-tall (16-meter) spacecraft powered by three **BE-7 engines**. According to Wikipedia, each BE-7 burns liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen for 10,000 pounds of thrust and can throttle down for a gentle lunar landing.

Unlike SpaceX's simpler test version for Artemis III, Blue Origin is flying a **nearly complete** lander with life-support systems and a full crew cabin — so astronauts can practice entering and exiting in space.

NASA's timeline for Blue Origin's lunar lander:

- **Late 2026:** Complete BE-7 engine qualification and restore the damaged **Launch Complex-36** pad at Cape Canaveral. (New Glenn itself is due back to flight by Q4 2026.)
- **Q1 2027:** Launch the smaller **Blue Moon MK1** on NASA's **CLPS** (Commercial Lunar Payload Services) robotic moon mission.
- **Q1 2028:** Attempt the **uncrewed MK2 lunar landing** — the critical demo that must succeed before Blue Origin can fly astronauts.

## 4 — The showdown: who lands first?

Both companies are pushing the clock. SpaceX aims for an uncrewed landing in late 2027; Blue Origin targets Q1 2028. NASA will wait for whichever lander proves itself first before trusting human lives.

If both succeed, the prize is enormous: the first crewed Moon landing since 1972, planned for **Artemis IV in 2028**. Until then, the race to the Red Planet's dusty south pole is wide open.

## Try this at home

Build a mini rocket launcher! Tape a plastic water bottle to a bicycle pump using a needle valve. Fill the bottle one-third with water, pump air in, and watch the water squirt out the bottom, pushing the "rocket" skyward. Measure how high it flies with different water amounts. This simple demo shows how rockets work — just like the real Starship and New Glenn lifting off from Earth.

## Fun Fact

Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket is named after astronaut John Glenn — the first American to orbit Earth in 1962. The rocket's first stage is designed to land back on a floating platform at sea, just like SpaceX's boosters, making it one of the few reusable heavy-lift rockets in the world.

**for-grown-ups:** NASA's Human Landing System program selected both SpaceX (Starship) and Blue Origin (Blue Moon) to develop lunar landers for the Artemis program. Artemis III (late 2027) will test both landers in low-Earth orbit before any crewed lunar landing. Artemis IV (early 2028) is the target for the first Moon landing. SpaceX must demonstrate orbital propellant transfer, complete a crew cabin simulator by early 2027, and launch an uncrewed lunar landing by late 2027. Blue Origin must qualify its BE-7 engines, restore LC-36, launch New Glenn to flight status, fly the Blue Moon MK1 on a CLPS mission in Q1 2027, and attempt an uncrewed MK2 lunar landing in Q1 2028. The timeline was outlined at the Oct 5, 2026 International Astronautical Congress in Antalya, Türkiye.
