# Hairy Feet and Red Eyes: Why Did Two New Sea Spiders Stay Hidden for a Century?

## 1. Tiny spiders with a big secret

Picture a creature with eight long legs — sometimes as many as twelve — that lives its whole life underwater. That's a **sea spider**. They are cousins of the spiders, scorpions and horseshoe crabs you might know from land, but sea spiders never left the ocean. They have been paddling around for about **500 million years**.

Now, scientists from the University of British Columbia have found **two brand-new kinds** of sea spider in the Salish Sea, the cold, green water off Canada's Pacific coast near Vancouver.

## 2. Meet the two newcomers

The first one is called ***Callipallene pilosuspedes*** — that name comes from Latin words meaning "hairy feet." Its lower legs are covered in long, curved spines, and it has striking **red eyes**. It also has a funny little triangular mouth with **three lips**, ringed by wiggly feelers, and a short, clawed snout for grabbing food.

The second is ***Tanystylum kiixin***, pronounced "kee-hin." Its name honours an ancient Indigenous village site near where the animals were found — the word echoes the sound of waves crashing on the shore. This species has smaller, less bendy egg-carrying limbs, so it can't clean itself as well as its cousin. Researchers often found bits of dirt, debris and even tiny hitch-hiking parasites stuck to its body.

## 3. A century of hiding

Here's the surprising part: these are the **first new sea spiders described from the Salish Sea in almost 100 years**, even though only about 20 kinds had ever been recorded there.

Scientists had a hunch that more were tucked away. "People don't know much about what species exist here," said lead author **Cormac Toler-Scott**. "In order to understand how marine invertebrates are being impacted by things like climate change, we have to be able to identify them."

The team caught the animals during dives up to 18 metres deep and used both careful looking and DNA testing to prove the newcomers were truly different from their relatives.

## 4. Why the males do the babysitting

Sea spiders do things their own way. They **breathe straight through their skin** — no lungs needed. And unlike most animals, it is the **fathers** who carry the eggs, gluing clusters of them onto their own bodies until the babies hatch.

## Try this at home

Be an ocean detective without getting wet. Fill a shallow pan with water and scatter in a few small items — a pebble, a leaf, a twig. Look at them from the side at eye level, the way a diver sees the seabed. How many hidden creatures or surprises can you spot once you slow down and really look? That's exactly how the scientists found animals nobody had noticed before.

## Fun Fact

Some sea spiders in Antarctica grow bigger than a dinner plate — over **70 centimetres** across! But the ones living in shallow, warmer water are often smaller than your fingernail.
