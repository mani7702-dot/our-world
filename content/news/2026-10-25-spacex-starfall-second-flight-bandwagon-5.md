# What is SpaceX's mysterious Starfall capsule, and why did it fly again?

## 1 — A tiny capsule that comes back from space

Imagine sending a small package up to space — and having it come *back* to you, landing safely on Earth like a tiny spaceship. That is exactly what **SpaceX's Starfall** capsule does. On **October 20, 2026**, Starfall flew for only the **second time ever**, riding along on SpaceX's **Bandwagon-5** mission like a passenger on a very big rocket.

SpaceX keeps Starfall pretty mysterious — which makes it extra exciting for space fans! But we know this: Starfall is the kind of capsule that **brings things home**. Scientists and companies can put experiments or small cargo inside it, and the capsule carries them back to Earth after their trip through space.

## 2 — How did it get to space?

The Bandwagon-5 mission blasted off at **7:04 a.m. EDT** from **Cape Canaveral Space Force Station** in Florida. The rocket that carried it? A **Falcon 9** — one of SpaceX's most-flown rockets.

But the story does not end there. After the Starfall capsule and 31 other payloads were released, the **Falcon 9's first stage** (the big bottom part) flipped around and flew itself **all the way back to Earth**, landing on a floating drone ship in the middle of the ocean. The ship is named **"A Shortfall of Gravitas"** — yes, that is its real name! This was the **19th time that particular booster flew and landed**, showing how **reusing rockets** has become normal at SpaceX.

## 3 — Who else was on the ride?

There were **32 payloads** in total on Bandwagon-5. One of the most interesting was **Varda Space Industries' W-Series 7** — a small "mini-factory" capsule designed to **make medicines in space**. In the microgravity of space, crystals can grow bigger and purer than on Earth, which could lead to better medicines. Varda's capsule brings those crystals back home, safely landing on solid ground.

Starfall is designed to carry up to **2,200 pounds (1,000 kg)** of cargo back to Earth. It is about **10 feet (3 meters) wide** — the size of a small table — and weighs roughly **4,600 pounds (2,100 kg)**. It is shaped like a flying saucer or a dinner plate, which makes it very stable when it returns through Earth's atmosphere.

## 4 — Why does Starfall matter?

Starfall's job is to make space **affordable for small experiments**. Before capsules like Starfall, only big, expensive missions could bring big payloads back from space. Now, small science teams, schools, and companies can send their experiments up and get them back. That means more people can do real science in space — even if they do not have a billion-dollar budget!

Starfall first flew in **June 2026**, so this second flight is all about proving that the capsule can keep working, coming back successfully again and again — just like the reusable Falcon 9 boosters that carry it.

## Try this at home

Cut a circle out of cardboard or thick paper — about the size of a dinner plate (like Starfall). Tape a small toy or coin in the center as the "cargo." Now drop it straight down onto a cushion or pillow. Did the "capsule" survive the landing? Try adding paper fins or a foldable skirt to slow the fall — just like a real capsule uses its shape to stay stable on the way home!

## Fun Fact

The Falcon 9 booster that carried Starfall has flown **19 times** and landed 19 times. SpaceX's most-flown booster holds the record at **37 flights** as of October 2026 — that is like a passenger jet making 37 round-trip flights across the ocean, and landing perfectly every single time!
