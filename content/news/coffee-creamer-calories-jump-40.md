# Is your morning coffee sneaking in 40% more calories? The creamer culprit scientists found

*Report filed at 03:13 IST from the Our World science desk.*

## Coffee, but make it heavier

Here is a riddle for your taste buds: Americans are still drinking about the same amount of coffee as they were 18 years ago — roughly a 13-ounce cup a day — but every sip now carries nearly **40% more calories** than it used to. If the coffee itself did not change, what did?

The answer is hiding at the bottom of your mug. And no, it is not the sugar.

## The invisible calorie guest

A research team at **Tufts University** combed through the nation's health records — 30,000 American adults, each one reporting exactly what they ate and drank over two separate days. They wanted to know how coffee habits had changed between 2003 and 2018.

The surprising winner? **Non-dairy creamer** — the little cartons and powders that promise to make your coffee creamy without dairy. By 2018, creamer had quietly become the **top source of added calories in coffee**, beating both sugar and milk.

1. In 2003, a typical coffee lover added about 7.7 calories of creamer per day.
2. By 2018, that number had **more than doubled to 18.5 calories** — nearly 2.5 times the calories that sugar was contributing (16 calories) and almost double the 11 calories coming from dairy milk.

## Why creamer is the heavyweight

The secret is in what creamer is made of. Real milk is mostly water, so it is fairly light. But non-dairy creamer is an engineered blend of **vegetable fats, sugars, and milk solids** packed so tightly that just a tablespoon can hold more energy than a sugar cube. That is why researchers found dairy milk was still the most *common* addition by weight — but creamer won the *calorie* race.

Think of it like this: milk is like adding a splash of water, while creamer is like adding a splash of syrup. Same spoonful, very different fuel.

The researchers also found something hopeful: sugar intake stayed flat, and so did the habit of brewing plain coffee. The calorie jump came from one ingredient slipping onto more and more mugs.

## A cup you can feel good about

The good news? You get to decide what lands in your cup. If you are cutting back on hidden calories, try measuring your creamer with a spoon first, or simply sip your coffee black and taste what the beans were meant to do. Every tablespoon swapped out is a small win your future self will notice.

## Try this at home

Make a "creamer rainbow" to see the difference. Fill three identical clear cups with the same amount of coffee (or even colored water). In cup A, add sugar and stir. In cup B, add the same *volume* of water. In cup C, add the same volume of non-dairy creamer. Now weigh each liquid — the creamer cup will feel noticeably heavier. That extra weight is the hidden calories your body will have to process.

## Fun Fact

The most popular non-dairy creamers get their trademarky vanilla or hazelnut flavor not from the real nuts or beans, but from **aromachemicals** — the same kind of molecules food scientists use to scent perfumes. One tiny molecule called vanillin is responsible for most of what we think of as "vanilla" flavor, and it can be brewed up in a lab for a fraction of the cost of real vanilla beans. So your "French vanilla" creamer may be more chemistry lab than French café!
