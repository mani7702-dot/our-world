# How Many Kinds of Apples Used to Grow on Earth — and Why Supermarkets Only Sell 5?

## The Apples That Time Forgot

Walk into almost any supermarket and you will see the same handful of apples: shiny red Gala, bright Fuji, or maybe a crisp Honeycrisp. But did you know that **over 17,000 different apple varieties** were once grown right here in North America alone? Today, just 3 to 5 of those kinds fill most grocery store shelves. The rest — thousands of them — vanished like stories nobody wrote down.

## A Supermarket's Mission to Save Old Apples

Spar, one of Europe's largest supermarket groups, is trying to change that. Working with an Austrian produce trader named Frutura, Spar is testing two very old apple varieties in a special experimental orchard: the **Ilzer Rose**, first grown in 1840 in Austria, and the **Falchs Gulderling**, discovered in the 1870s in South Tyrol. These apples even have fun names that tell stories from the past — Ilzer Rose might sound like a tea, and Gulderling means "little gold coin"!

The orchard has 2,150 trees, and the first fruits were picked just this month. Spar wants to find out: can these 150-year-old apples grow well under modern farming conditions? If they succeed, these rare varieties could appear in markets across Europe.

## Why Did So Many Apples Disappear?

The answer has to do with how farming changed. Modern apple varieties are bred for very specific things: they need to look perfect on store shelves, survive long shipping trips, resist diseases without too many pesticides, and grow in huge quantities. Old varieties were often bred just for taste — and many would not ship well or would bruise easily.

But taste is making a comeback. As shoppers say, "I want to know where my food comes from," old apples are being rediscovered. The Lost Apple Project in the Pacific Northwest has already found 29 lost apple varieties by searching abandoned farms and old orchards.

## Try This at Home

Next time you visit a farmer's market or a specialty grocery store, look for apples with unusual names or shapes. Ask the grower what variety it is and how long it has been growing in the area. You might taste something your great-great-grandparents once ate!

## Fun Fact

The world's largest collection of apple trees — over 7,500 varieties — grows at the **National Plant Germplasm System** in Geneva, New York. Scientists there save apple seeds and grafts so no variety is ever truly lost forever.
