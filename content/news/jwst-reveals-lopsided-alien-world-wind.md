# 1. Is one side of this alien world a blistering hot desert — and the other a cool breeze?

*Report filed at 19:26 IST from the Our World science desk.*

## A puffy planet bigger than our own Jupiter

About **1,300 light-years** away, in the constellation Scorpius, orbits a strange and giant planet called **WASP-17b**. It was discovered back in **2009** — and from the very start, it surprised astronomers. While it weighs only about **half as much as Jupiter**, it is **twice as wide**, making it one of the most "puffed up" planets ever found. Its density is so low that if you could float it in water, it would be lighter than **styrofoam**!

This planet belongs to a family called **hot Jupiters** — gas giants that orbit incredibly close to their star, completing a full year in just a few days. WASP-17b's year lasts only **3.7 days**.

## Why one side is a scorcher and the other is not

WASP-17b is **tidally locked**, which means the same side always faces its star — just like the Moon always shows the same face to Earth. When astronomers looked at the planet's edge with the **James Webb Space Telescope**, they did something new: they used a technique called **"transmission strings"** to map how the atmosphere changes all the way around the planet's silhouette.

Here is what they found: the **morning side** of the planet measures about **858 Kelvin** (that is about **585 °C** or **1,085 °F**) — still incredibly hot. But the **evening side** reaches a blistering **1,522 Kelvin** (about **1,249 °C** or **2,280 °F**). That is a temperature difference of nearly **650 degrees** between the two sides!

Hot gas expands, so on the hotter evening side the atmosphere stretches out to be **1.8 times thicker** than on the cooler morning side. From space, the planet looks less like a ball and more like a squashed **egg** floating in space.

## Weather that could power an airplane... or stop one

Why is there such a huge temperature gap? On a tidally locked planet, **jet streams** blow from the scorching day side toward the cooler night side, carrying heat with them. But the atmosphere is so thick and the temperature difference so extreme that it creates some of the wildest weather in the universe.

Even with all of this chaos, the scientists discovered something surprising: **water vapor and carbon dioxide** are spread evenly all around the planet, from the equator to the poles. Only **methane** and **sulfur dioxide** seem to change, clustering more near the poles away from the jet streams.

## Try this at home

You can see temperature differences with your own eyes! On a sunny day, put one thermometer in the sun and another in the shade. Check them after a few minutes — the one in the sun will read higher, just like how WASP-17b's day side is hotter than its night side. Now imagine if that difference was 650 degrees... that is how astronomers found out this alien world is lopsided!

## Fun Fact

The planet's name was chosen by **Costa Rica** in a global contest run by the International Astronomical Union. They named it **Ditsö** — after a deity from the Bribri people, the indigenous community of southeastern Costa Rica and western Panama. So this puffy, egg-shaped world carries an ancient name from the Americas!
