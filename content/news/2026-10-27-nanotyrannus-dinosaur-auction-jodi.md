# 5 Things to Know About Jodi the Nanotyrannus Dinosaur Going to Auction

## 1. Meet Jodi — a real skeleton, not a toy

Jodi is a nearly complete *Nanotyrannus lancensis* skeleton, about 10 feet (3 meters) tall at the hip. The fossil has 191 bone elements and a beautifully preserved skull. It was discovered in Montana's Hall Creek Formation by rancher Jodi Pierson (hence the nickname), then excavated by commercial paleontologist Clayton Phipps. Jodi was first shown at London's Frieze Masters art fair in October 2023 — back when scientists thought it was just a baby *T. rex*.

## 2. Not a teenage T. rex — it's its own dinosaur

For decades, scientists argued over whether *Nanotyrannus* was a separate dinosaur or simply a juvenile *Tyrannosaurus rex*. In 2025, researchers Lindsay Zanno and James Napoli published an exhaustive study confirming that *Nanotyrannus* is a real, distinct genus. The key clue: the skeleton's bones showed signs of full adult maturity, not adolescence. The name means "dwarf tyrant" — a smaller, sleeker cousin of *T. rex* with blade-like teeth for slicing meat.

## 3. It shares a neighborhood with the famous "Dueling Dinosaurs"

Phipps also discovered the world-famous "Dueling Dinosaurs" fossil eight miles away in 2006. That specimen shows a *Nanotyrannus* locked in combat with a *Triceratops* — and it became the key evidence proving *Nanotyrannus* was real. That same skeleton was nicknamed "Bloody Mary" and helped Zanno and Napoli confirm the genus in their 2025 paper.

## 4. It's hitting the auction block — and scientists are worried

Sotheby's in New York plans to auction Jodi with a $6 million estimate. Just last July, another *T. rex* named "Gus" sold for $50.1 million. Some paleontologists worry that when fossil specimens sell to private collectors, they can disappear into vaults where scientists can't study them. As Professor Steve Brusatte put it, "It's harrowing to think a skeleton could vanish where kids and families can no longer see it."

## 5. Why it matters: one of only three in the world

Jodi is one of only three *Nanotyrannus* skeletons known to science. It lived alongside *T. rex* about 67 million years ago, near the end of the Cretaceous period — making it one of the last dinosaurs before the asteroid impact. Whether it ends up in a museum or a private collection, it helps us understand how these fierce but fascinating creatures lived and evolved.
