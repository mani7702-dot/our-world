# How did sitcoms launch the biggest music careers of the century?

You might think acting and singing are totally different jobs. But for more than 60 years, TV sitcoms have been secret launchpads for some of today's biggest pop stars. Here are 10 stars who started on a television set — and ended up topping the Billboard charts.

## 1. Zendaya: From Shake It Up to "Replay"

Before Zendaya won Emmys for *Euphoria* and starred in Marvel blockbusters, she was Rocky Blue on Disney's *Shake It Up* (2010-2013). The show's dance-focused plot gave her a natural excuse to record music. Her 2013 single "Replay" hit the Top 40, and her self-titled debut album arrived the same year the series ended. Zendaya proved that a Disney sitcom could build a fanbase for both acting and music at the same time.

## 2. Sabrina Carpenter: Girl Meets World to "Espresso"

Sabrina Carpenter played Maya Hart, the wise-cracking best friend, on *Girl Meets World* (2014-2017) — a spin-off of *Boy Meets World*. While the show aired, she released EPs and albums on Disney Records. But her real breakthrough came later: her 2024 album *Short n' Sweet* debuted at number one on the Billboard 200, and "Espresso" became a global smash. The sitcom gave her a start, but she spent years growing into her own sound.

## 3. Miley Cyrus: Hannah Montana's real identity

Miley Cyrus lived a double life on *Hannah Montana* (2006-2011) — playing a normal teenager who was secretly a famous pop star. The show was so popular that the fictional Hannah Montana released real albums and toured in concert. Miley's first solo album without the mask, *Breakout*, came in 2008. She later evolved through pop, rock, and country, with hits like "Party in the U.S.A." and "Flowers."

## 4. Ariana Grande: Victorious to number one

Ariana Grande first appeared on *Barney & Friends*, but her big break came as Cat Valentine on Nickelodeon's *Victorious* (2010-2013). The show's music soundtracks let her showcase her incredible voice. After *Victorious* ended (and its short spin-off *Sam & Cat*), her 2013 debut *Yours Truly* hit number one on the Billboard 200. "The Way" became her first Top 10 single.

## 5. The Partridge Family: Built for TV, built for music

Unlike most entries on this list, *The Partridge Family* (1971-1974) was designed as both a sitcom and a band from day one. David Cassidy (Keith) and Shirley Jones (Shirley) were the real singers; the rest of the family used studio musicians. Their 1970 hit "I Think I Love You" spent three weeks at number one. David Cassidy became one of the first teen idols created entirely by television.

## Why sitcoms work as music launchpads

Television gives performers something record labels can't buy: an audience that already loves them. When a character sings on a sitcom, viewers see personality and relatability alongside the voice. That emotional connection turns casual viewers into fans who buy albums, stream songs, and follow the artist's real music career.

## Fun Fact

The Monkees (1966-1970) were created by TV producers who wanted a Beatles-style band — but the show and the music were planned together from the start. Their TV songs became real hits, and the band eventually fought for creative control over their own music.

## Try this at home

Next time you watch a sitcom, listen for characters who sing or dance. Many of today's biggest stars started with nothing more than a catchy theme tune and a TV studio set. Can you guess which current pop stars had sitcom beginnings?
