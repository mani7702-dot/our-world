High above Earth, a small robot did something no machine had done before. It built a structure in space all by itself, with no astronaut holding the tools.

## A robot with a very long arm

The robot is called ASTRA. Its long name is Autonomous In-Space Tensioned Reflector Assembly. It is a builder on rails.

Think of a crane at a building site, but floating in space. Its arm slides along three straight rails. It lifts a tube from one box, a joint from another, and clicks them together.

Each finished piece is called a bay. Many bays join into a truss, a frame as strong as a ladder that goes on and on. A thin shiny sheet pulls tight across it to make a dish.

## Why build it up there?

A giant dish antenna is a wonderful tool for listening to the universe. But it has a problem: it is far too wide to fit inside a rocket.

So engineers had a clever idea. Send up a small box of parts, plus a robot. Let the robot build the big dish once it is already in orbit.

## A small start, a big idea

The robot launched in March 2026. Engineers on Earth could only talk to it for about fifteen minutes each day.

It did not finish the whole dish. Time ran out. It built only the first part. But that first part proved the idea works.

## Try this at home

You can feel how a truss works with straws and tape.

1. Tape two straws end to end.
2. Bend them. Easy, right?
3. Tape a third straw across to make a triangle.
4. Bend again. Much stiffer!

Triangles make frames strong. That is the same trick ASTRA uses when it joins its pieces into a truss.
