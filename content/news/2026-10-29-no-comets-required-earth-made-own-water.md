# Where Did Earth's Water Come From? Maybe Earth Made It Herself!

## The Great Water Mystery

Imagine looking out at the ocean and wondering: *How did all this water get here?* For a long time, scientists thought comets and space rocks brought water to Earth like cosmic delivery trucks. But new research says Earth might have cooked up its own water right from the start — no delivery needed!

## Rain of Pebbles in a Fiery Sky

About 4.5 billion years ago, Earth was a totally different place. It was still growing bigger by grabbing space dust and pebbles — tiny rocks floating in the solar wind. As these pebbles fell through Earth's early atmosphere, something amazing happened. The atmosphere was full of hydrogen (the lightest element), and the pebbles were made of rock containing iron. When hot pebbles met hydrogen, a chemical reaction occurred that created water vapor!

Think of it like this: if you've ever seen steam rise when cold water hits a hot pan, you've kind of seen a tiny version. But Earth's version was on a HUGE scale — the atmosphere was thousands of degrees hot, and pebbles rained down like fire for hours or even days.

## Hundreds of Oceans, Right at Home

Scientists ran experiments heating space-rock samples to over 2,000°F (about 1,200–1,400°C) in streams of hydrogen gas. They found that nearly 80% of the iron in the rocks reacted, releasing enough hydrogen to form water. When they calculated how much water this process could make during Earth's early growth, they were amazed: hundreds — yes, *hundreds* — of ocean masses could have formed this way!

This means every time you splash in a puddle or watch waves at the beach, you might be seeing water that Earth made herself, right in her own fiery birth cloud.

## What About Comets?

Comets probably still brought *some* extra water — like adding sprinkles to an already-frosted cake. But the big surprise is that Earth didn't need them as the main ingredient. The same process might have happened on Mars too, and possibly on distant worlds orbiting other stars!

## Try This at Home

Mix 1 cup of baking soda in a pot of water. Add a splash of vinegar and watch the bubbles! That's a tiny taste of how chemical reactions create gases. Now imagine doing that with fire-rock pouring through a hydrogen sky for days — that's real water-making power!

## Fun Fact

The water you drink today might include atoms made inside the fiery atmosphere of baby Earth, when the whole planet was one big molten lava ball and raining fire-pebbles from the sky!

For grown-ups: this "pebble accretion" water-formation mechanism was published in Nature Geoscience (October 2026) by Garai & Sharp (University of New Mexico), supported by high-temperature gas-stream reactor experiments.
