# How a nationwide bird count turned every Australian into a citizen scientist?

## 1. What is the Aussie Bird Count?

Every October, something special happens across Australia. Instead of sitting inside, people step outside — onto porches, park benches, school playgrounds, and bush trails — and start counting birds. Not for fun (though it is fun). Not just for fun, either. They are contributing to science.

The **Aussie Bird Count** is a week-long survey run by **BirdLife Australia**. All you need is 20 minutes, a smartphone, and eyes on the sky. Whether you are watching your lunch break unfold from a café window or walking the dog at dawn, if you see birds, you can join in.

## 2. Schools join the count

At **Cedars Christian College** in Wollongong, maths class looks a little different during bird-count week. Teacher **Karen Jooste** has blended birdwatching into her Year 2 curriculum. Students use the count to learn about **data collection**, write **avian-themed stories**, and explore **biodiversity and conservation** — all while sitting under the trees beside the school's tree-lined creek.

The school grounds are a bird hotspot. Students have spotted:
- **Tawny frogmouths** with two fluffy chicks
- **Magpies** and **magpie larks** nesting nearby
- **Wood ducks** and **Pacific black ducks** by the water
- **King parrots** and **rainbow lorikeets** in the canopy
- Even a **bowerbird** that has been known to build an astonishing bright blue bower in spring

"We try to make the most of it," Jooste says. "They actually love it."

## 3. Anyone can be a bird detective

The beauty of the Aussie Bird Count is that it asks nothing special of participants. You do not need expensive binoculars or a field guide. You just need the free app.

The app shows the **30 most commonly recorded birds** within 10 km of your location. If you spot something unusual, you can search by **size, shape, and colour** — no expertise required. Whether you are in a city apartment, a remote outback property, or a quiet suburban street, your 20 minutes of observation helps scientists piece together a continent-wide picture.

In 2025, **64,000 Australians** counted more than **5 million birds**. As of October 23, 2026, more than **3.7 million birds** had already been logged — and the count was on track to beat last year's record.

The most commonly seen birds? The **Australian magpie**, the **rainbow lorikeet**, and the **noisy miner** — three of Australia's most familiar and beloved backyard visitors.

## Fun Fact

Australia is home to **898 recorded bird species** — and more than **45%** of them are found nowhere else on Earth. That means over two in every five of Australia's birds are unique to the continent, like the laughing kookaburra, the majestic emu, and the colourful sulphur-crested cockatoo.

## Try this at home

**Backyard bird count:** Spend 20 minutes in your garden or local park. Count every bird you see or hear. Draw or list them in a notebook. Then try the next day from a different spot — a street corner, a school playground, or a shopping trip. Compare your lists. Do you see the same birds? Are there more near trees and water?

**Bird ID challenge:** Look up pictures of three common birds in your area. Can you tell them apart by size, colour, and shape? The Australian magpie is black and white with a bold yellow bill. The rainbow lorikeet is bright green with orange and blue flashes. The noisy miner is a sleek grey bird with a black face and yellow beak.
