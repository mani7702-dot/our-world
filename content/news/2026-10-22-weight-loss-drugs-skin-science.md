# Why do weight-loss drugs sometimes leave skin looking loose?

Some medicines that help people lose weight quickly are doing more than just shrinking waistlines — they are also changing the way beauty companies think about skin care.

## The drug that tricks your brain

Scientists discovered that certain weight-loss drugs work by copying a natural hormone that your gut already makes. This hormone — called **GLP-1** — sends a message to your brain that says, "I'm full, stop eating." When doctors give this hormone as a medicine (like Ozempic, Wegovy, or Mounjaro), it helps people feel full sooner and eat less.

On average, people taking these drugs lose 5 to 20 percent of their body weight. That is a lot — and it happens over months, not years. But here is the thing: when your body changes shape really fast, not everything gets the memo at the same time.

## Why skin needs time to catch up

Your skin is like a stretchy pair of jeans. It has two main layers: the thin outer layer (epidermis) and a thicker, bouncy layer underneath (dermis). The dermis is held together by tiny cables called **collagen fibers**. These cables act like rubber bands — they snap back when you stretch them, but only if you give them time.

When you gain weight slowly, your skin stretches gradually and the collagen adjusts. But when you lose weight quickly, your skin is stretched out and the collagen fibers are already pulled long. They do not shorten back instantly, so the skin can look looser — like a bungee cord that has been stretched too far for too long.

This is exactly what the beauty giant L'Oreal noticed. Their CEO said the rise of these weight-loss drugs is "a very important phenomenon" affecting how much people buy skin-care products. The company is now making lotions and creams designed to support skin elasticity — helping those collagen fibers stay bouncy while the body catches up.

## What the body is really doing

Each of us is born with a set amount of collagen. After age 20, we make less and less — which is why skin starts to lose its bounce anyway. Adding rapid weight loss on top of that natural decline is like asking an old rubber band to snap back after being stretched for months. Sometimes it just... doesn't.

But here is the hopeful part: your body is always rebuilding. With the right care — gentle massage, hydration, and time — many people see their skin improve. The GLP-1 drugs themselves do not cause the loose skin; they simply help the body move faster than the skin can keep up.

## Try this at home

Fill a balloon with a little water and stretch it slowly for 10 seconds — then let go. The balloon snaps back because it was stretched gently. Now stretch the same balloon as hard as you can for the same time and let go. The neck stays a little wider, right? That is like what happens to your skin's collagen when weight drops quickly. The stretchier the stretch, the slower the snap-back.

## Fun Fact

Your body makes about **1.5 grams of collagen every day** — that is roughly the weight of a paperclip. It takes months for new collagen to rebuild and tighten stretched skin, which is why dermatologists say patience is the best skin-care after any big weight change.
