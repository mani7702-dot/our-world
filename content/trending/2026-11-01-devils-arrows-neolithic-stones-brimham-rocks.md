# Who Hauled 25-Tonne Stones 18 Kilometres? Britain's Tallest Stone Row Finally Has an Answer

## A mystery that stood for 4,000 years

Near the village of Boroughbridge in northern England stand three enormous stone pillars. The tallest is almost **seven metres high** — taller than the stones at Stonehenge. Together they form the **tallest standing stone row in Britain**, and people have wondered about them for centuries.

The stones even have a legend. Locals said the Devil once stood on a nearby hill and **hurled giant stone arrows** at the town — but they fell short and landed where they stand today. That is why they are called the **Devil's Arrows**.

But where did the stones *really* come from? Scientists have finally worked it out.

## Following the stones' fingerprints

A team from **Curtin University** in Australia and the **University of York** used a clever, gentle trick. Instead of drilling into the ancient monument, they pressed **sticky tape** onto the stones to lift tiny mineral grains.

Those grains contain crystals called **zircon** and **apatite**, which carry an age "fingerprint" locked inside them. By matching that fingerprint, the researchers traced the stones to **Brimham Rocks** — a rugged outcrop about **18 kilometres** away.

Each stone weighs more than **25 tonnes** (about the weight of five large elephants). Moving them that far would have taken huge planning, teamwork and engineering skill — and it proves the stones were **chosen on purpose**, not just grabbed from the nearest pile.

## What the discovery tells us

The study also ruled out a simpler explanation: that **ice-age glaciers** had carried the rocks there naturally. They hadn't. People did it.

Even better, the team solved a second puzzle. An old story said a **fourth stone** had once stood at the site but went missing long ago. Researchers now believe part of it was carved into a **medieval cross** that still stands in the nearby village of Aldborough — hiding in plain sight for centuries.

"The location the stones came from likely held meaning for them," said Dr Anthony Clarke, who led the study. The builders weren't picking the easiest stone. They were picking a **special place**.

## Try this at home

Be a "stone detective"! Collect a few small rocks from different spots near your home — a garden, a path, a riverbank. Look closely at their **colour, grain and texture**. Do any match? How might you tell if someone had moved a rock from far away? Real geologists answer exactly that question using the tiniest details.

**Fun Fact:** The Devil's Arrows are so tall that a person standing at the base would need a **ladder taller than two giraffes** stacked up to reach the top of the southern stone!
