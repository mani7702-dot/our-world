# How do black hole jets decide a galaxy's fate?

## 1. The gas that galaxies keep secret

Every galaxy — including our own Milky Way — is like a cosmic iceberg. What you can see with your eyes or a telescope is just the tip. Hidden around each galaxy is a vast, invisible cloud of gas called the **circumgalactic medium**, or **CGM** for short.

This gas is the raw material for making new stars. It stretches out roughly **10 to 20 times farther** than the visible parts of the galaxy. Over time, some of this cooling gas flows inward, gathers into dense clouds, and eventually lights up as brand-new stars.

## 2. The mystery of the missing stars

Here is a puzzle that has puzzled astronomers for decades: if galaxies keep so much potential star-forming material around them, **why do they not make far more stars?** Shouldn't the cosmos be ablaze with starlight?

The answer, scientists now think, lies deep inside every big galaxy — at the very center, where a **supermassive black hole** lurks.

When a black hole actively consumes gas, it can release tremendous bursts of energy. Imagine a tiny whirlpool draining a bathtub — but scaled up so the whirlpool is millions of times heavier than our Sun!

## 3. Jets that shape entire galaxies

The new study — led by **Sanchayeeta Borthakur** of Arizona State University and **Namrata Roy** of India's Raman Research Institute — found that **narrow jets of extremely hot plasma** shoot out from these supermassive black holes. But these jets do not just shoot into empty space.

They reach out **far beyond the visible galaxy**, disturbing the invisible gas cloud and leaving behind a **glowing trail hundreds of thousands of light-years long**. The jets heat the gas and stir it up so it can no longer easily fall inward to form new stars.

Because of this, a galaxy's future is linked to its central black hole. Gas-rich and calm black holes let their host galaxy keep forming stars. Agitated black holes launch powerful jets that **turn the star factory off**.

> "This is a pathbreaking result that solves the long-standing mystery of how black holes influence galaxies, their stars, and life as we know it!" said Borthakur.

## Try this at home

Draw a galaxy on a piece of paper — a big swirly shape with a dot in the middle. Now use a cotton swab dipped in slightly wet paint to flick tiny dots outward from the center. Those dots are like the invisible gas clouds around a galaxy, and your flick shows how black hole jets push that gas far into space.

## Fun Fact

The supermassive black hole at the center of our own Milky Way is called **Sagittarius A* (A-star)**. It is about **four million times heavier than our Sun** — but it is not currently active. However, scientists think it was firing jets like those in this study about **8 billion years ago**. Those ancient jets may have helped shape the Milky Way into the galaxy we live in today!
