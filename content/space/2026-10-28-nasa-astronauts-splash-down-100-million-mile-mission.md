# How did four astronauts travel 100 million miles and come home safely?

## Seven months in zero gravity

Imagine floating for over **237 days** — that is nearly eight months — without ever touching the ground! On **October 8, 2026**, four astronauts from three different space agencies landed safely back on Earth after a remarkable journey.

Their ride was the **SpaceX Crew Dragon Freedom** capsule, and their home for most of those 237 days was the **International Space Station** (ISS) — a giant laboratory that orbits about **400 kilometres** (250 miles) above Earth, circling our planet roughly **15 and a half times every single day**.

## Who was on board?

The crew was a true team effort between nations:

1. **Jessica Meir** (NASA, USA) — already a veteran of previous spacewalks
2. **Jack Hathaway** (NASA, USA) — part of the Artemis lunar program team
3. **Sophie Adenot** (ESA, France) — the first female commander of a European ISS mission
4. **Andrey Fedyaev** (Roscosmos, Russia) — an experienced engineer cosmonaut

They launched together on **February 13, 2026**, aboard a Falcon 9 rocket from Kennedy Space Center in Florida.

## A journey of a lifetime (and then some)

During their stay, the crew **completed over 3,792 orbits** of Earth and traveled more than **100 million miles** — about **four times the distance from Earth to the Sun and back**. That is like running around the Earth's equator over **4,000 times**!

While in orbit, they ran dozens of science experiments that could help future missions go even farther — to the **Moon** under NASA's Artemis program, and someday to **Mars**.

## Coming home: the Pacific splashdown

When it was time to return, the Crew Dragon capsule gently **splashed down** in the **Pacific Ocean** off the coast of Los Angeles at **8:34 a.m. PDT**. The splashdown is a soft landing in the water — recovery ships rush out to meet the capsule and bring the astronauts aboard.

After brief medical check-ups, the crew was flown to **NASA's Johnson Space Center in Houston**, where family, friends, and fellow space fans waited to welcome them home.

## Try this at home

Grab a balloon and a marker. Draw a big circle on it with the marker — that is Earth! Now hold the balloon by one end and rotate it slowly. Trace a finger around the balloon's surface as it spins — that finger is the ISS, zooming around our planet 15 times a day. Count how many times your finger goes around in 10 seconds, then imagine doing that for 237 days straight!

## Fun Fact

The ISS is the **largest human-made object in space** — bigger than a football field! It weighs about **420,000 kilograms** (nearly half a million kg) and was assembled piece by piece by astronauts on more than 40 spacewalking missions. You can sometimes spot it as the brightest moving "star" in the night sky if you know when and where to look — NASA has a website that shows when the ISS will pass over your hometown.

## For grown-ups

NASA's Commercial Crew Program partners with private companies like SpaceX to transport astronauts to and from the ISS. Crew-12 was the twelfth operational flight under this program and the 20th crewed orbital flight of a Crew Dragon spacecraft (per Wikipedia). The ISS itself orbits at approximately 400 km altitude, traveling at 7.67 km/s (27,600 km/h), completing about 15.5 orbits per day (per Wikipedia). Wikipedia confirms the ISS has hosted continuous human presence since November 2000. The capsule's splashdown in the Pacific is the standard end-of-mission recovery technique — Crew Dragon uses thrusters for a deorbit burn, then parachutes slow the capsule before it touches down under parachutes in the ocean. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman praised the mission as building "the capability, experience, and confidence to do this repeatedly" for future Moon and Mars missions.
