# How Does a Lava World Keep a Blanket of Clouds?

## 1. A Planet That Orbits Faster Than a Spinning Blender

Imagine a world where a single day — from sunrise to sunrise — lasts **just 10.5 hours**.
That is faster than the Earth spins once! This isn't science fiction; it is the real exoplanet
**TOI-561 b**, and it is one of the most extreme planets astronomers have ever studied.

TOI-561 b is what scientists call a **super-Earth**. It is bigger than our planet but smaller
than ice giants like Neptune. It also happens to be a **lava world** — a rocky planet so close
to its star that the daytime side is hot enough to melt rock. Picture a surface of flowing
magma under a blazing sky that never dims.

## 2. The Puzzle: Why Isn't It Bare Rock?

When astronomers first discovered TOI-561 b (it was spotted by NASA's TESS satellite),
they assumed it was just a **bare, airless rock**. After all, it orbits so close to its star
that any atmosphere should have been blasted away into space over billions of years. The
intense radiation from its star would have stripped away every last molecule of gas.

But then the **James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)** took a closer look — and found something
that made scientists scratch their heads.

## 3. JWST's Surprise: A Cloudy, Reflective Atmosphere

The research team, led by Dr. Lisa Dang from the University of Waterloo, used JWST's
**NIRSpec instrument** to study the planet for **37 hours** — that is more than three
complete orbits! By watching how the planet's brightness and heat changed as it moved
behind and in front of its star (a technique called a **phase curve**), they made a startling
discovery.

TOI-561 b is **covered by clouds** — but not the kind you find on Earth. These clouds are
made of tiny **silicate particles** (the same stuff as sand and glass) and **quartz**
(SiO₂), forming a thick, high-altitude haze that acts like sunscreen for the planet.

The clouds give the planet a **high albedo** — meaning it reflects about **60 percent** of
the starlight that hits it. Instead of absorbing all that heat, the planet bounces much of
it back into space. That is why the dayside stays cooler than expected — only about
**1,400° C (2,550° F)** instead of the **3,000° C** predicted for a bare rock.

## 4. A Planet-Sized Sunscreen

Think of it like wearing a white hat on a sunny day. Just as the hat reflects sunlight and
keeps you cooler, TOI-561 b's silicate clouds reflect starlight and keep the planet's
surface temperature lower than it should be. The team thinks the planet may have a **magma
ocean** on its dayside that continuously produces more of these reflective particles,
keeping the "sunscreen" layer alive and replenished.

This discovery changes what scientists thought they knew about lava worlds. **Planets that
orbit extremely close to their stars might still hold onto atmospheres** — not primordial
ones left over from their birth, but **secondary atmospheres** that form later, perhaps
through volcanic outgassing or chemical reactions deep underground.

## 5. Why This Matters for Future Explorers

Understanding TOI-561 b helps us learn how **rocky exoplanets evolve**. Some scientists think
that planets like this one may start out with thick, Neptune-like atmospheres that get
**whittled down** over time by their star's radiation. TOI-561 b could be a snapshot of that
process — a planet caught in the act of losing its atmosphere but still holding on to a
protective blanket of clouds.

The research, published in *The Astronomical Journal*, was led by a team of 14 scientists
from universities across North America, including the University of Montreal, the University
of Waterloo, and the Carnegie Science Earth and Planets Laboratory.

## Try This at Home

**Build a mini "lava world" model!** Take a metal baking tray, sprinkle a thin layer of
sugar on it (that is your "atmosphere"), and shine a hair dryer on one side (your "star").
Watch how the sugar melts and bubbles on the hot side but can protect the cooler areas.
Just like TOI-561 b's clouds, your sugar layer acts as a shield against the "heat storm."

## Fun Fact

TOI-561 b completes a full orbit in just **10.5 hours** — if you lived there (and you really
wouldn't want to!), you would see your star rise and set **more than twice per Earth day**!

## For Grown-ups

The full research paper is "A Highly Reflective Atmosphere on the Lava World TOI-561b Revealed
by JWST/NIRSpec Phase Curves" by Samuel Boucher et al. (2025), available on arxiv.org as
paper 2509.17231. A companion paper titled "A Thick Volatile Atmosphere on the Ultrahot
Super-Earth TOI-561 b" (also arXiv:2509.17231) confirms the dayside temperature and
atmospheric composition findings.
