# Why are Switzerland's glaciers disappearing so fast — and what does it mean for the whole world?

## A shrinking blanket of ice

High in the Swiss Alps, ancient rivers of ice — called glaciers — are melting faster than ever. In 2026 alone, Switzerland's glaciers lost more than **5% of their total ice volume**. That means a layer roughly the thickness of a two-story building was stripped away from these slow-moving giants. And it is not just one year: in the past five years, the country has lost nearly **20%** of all its glacier ice.

## What makes a glacier disappear?

Glaciers live in a delicate balance. They grow when winter snow piles up faster than summer heat melts it. But the winter of 2025 to 2026 was one of the snowiest — no, actually one of the **least snowy** winters on record for Switzerland. With barely any new snow to protect them, the glaciers were exposed to the sun. Then came a summer of record-breaking heat. For **76 days** in a row, the freezing point climbed above 4,000 metres — higher than any mountain peak in Switzerland. By September, even the highest snowfields had melted away, leaving bare, blue ice glowing in the sun.

## When the ice is gone forever

Some of Switzerland's smaller glaciers have now **vanished completely and permanently**, including Bella Tola and Griessfirn. The famous Rhône, Aletsch, and Clariden glaciers all experienced their worst melting on record. But here is an interesting twist: as the glaciers melt, they spill out huge amounts of water. Between July and September 2026, Swiss glaciers released about **2,200 billion litres** of water — more than four times what Swiss households drink in an entire year. That meltwater helps keep rivers flowing during dry summer months. But the price is steep: as the glaciers keep shrinking, that helpful water source slowly disappears too.

## Why we should all care

Switzerland's glaciers act like nature's reservoirs, storing water in winter and releasing it slowly in summer. They feed the Rhine, Rhône, and Danube — rivers that millions of people downstream depend on for drinking water, farming, and electricity. When glaciers melt too fast, the short-term water boost eventually turns into long-term drought. Scientists at ETH Zurich and the GLAMOS monitoring network are tracking every centimetre of loss, because these icy sentinels tell us how our whole planet is changing.

## Try this at home

Fill a shallow baking tray with flour, then sprinkle cocoa powder on top. Drop a small marble from about a metre high — watch the perfect circle form with a central peak and a spray of flour around the edges. That mini-crater mirrors the shape of a glacier valley. Now tilt the tray so one side is higher — see how the "glacier" flows downhill? That is how real ice creeps and carves valleys over thousands of years.

## Fun Fact

Glacier ice can look blue — and it is not a trick of the light. When snow piles up for many years and squeezes into ice, the air bubbles get squeezed out. Without those bubbles, the ice absorbs the red and green parts of sunlight, reflecting only blue back at you. That is why deep glacier ice glows an eerie, beautiful blue.

**for-grownups:** Swiss glaciers lost >5% of their ice volume in 2026 (average thinning 2.5–4 m; up to 10 m at glacier tongues), following the snow-poor 2025–26 winter and a summer with 76 days above the 4,000 m freezing-line elevation (a Swiss record). Bella Tola and Griessfirn vanished permanently; Rhône, Aletsch, and Clariden experienced worst-on-record melt. Annual melt was 5.08 m (2026). GLAMOS (Glacier Monitoring Switzerland, headquartered at ETH Zurich with universities of Fribourg and Zurich) compiles these data in its annual report. Glaciers released ~2,200 billion litres of meltwater (July–Sept 2026), >4× annual Swiss household consumption.
