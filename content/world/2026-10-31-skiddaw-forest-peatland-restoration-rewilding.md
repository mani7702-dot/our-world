# How Do You Bring a "Sponge Mountain" Back to Life?

## 1. A Very Special Kind of Ground

High in the **Lake District** in England rises a mountain called **Skiddaw**. On its slopes lies something you might not expect: a huge, squishy, living carpet of **peat** — a kind of soil built up over thousands of years from moss and plants that never fully rotted away.

Peat acts like a giant **sponge**. It soaks up rainwater and stores **carbon**, the same stuff that traps heat in our atmosphere. In fact, healthy peatlands are among the **biggest carbon stores on land**. Now a team called the **Cumbria Wildlife Trust** has started a big project to bring the mountain's damaged peat back to health.

## 2. When a Sponge Gets Squeezed Dry

Over many decades, ditches were dug across the peat to drain it, and bare patches were worn away. When peat dries out, something surprising happens: instead of **storing** carbon, it releases it back into the air as **carbon dioxide**. "Peatlands are a huge carbon store, so they're essential in our fight against climate change," explains **Susie Webb**, the trust's peatland team manager.

The repair job is one of the biggest the trust has ever done: **435 hectares** of peatland — about the size of 600 football pitches — plus **6 km of boundary fence** to keep wandering sheep off the recovering plants.

## 3. The Tools of the Trade

How do you fix a dried-out sponge the size of a hill? Workers use **heavy machines to block up the old drainage ditches** so water stays on the mountain. They also **re-seed bare patches** with special mosses, especially **sphagnum moss**.

Sphagnum is a superstar plant: its spongy cells hold many times their own weight in water, and over centuries dead sphagnum slowly turns into brand-new peat. As the ground gets wetter, wildlife returns — the team expects **sundews** (a plant that catches insects!), **dragonflies**, and wading birds called **snipe**.

## 4. A Hundred-Year Plan

The peatland work should take **three to four months**, but the trust is thinking much further ahead. Their plan is a **100-year vision** for Skiddaw Forest: restoring over 1,000 acres of peat bog and planting **620 acres of temperate rainforest**. Wetter peat also **slows rainwater running off the fells**, which helps protect villages downstream from flooding.

### Try this at home

Make a mini "peat sponge" test! Soak a dry sponge in water, then squeeze it — that's like rain flowing off damaged peat. Now put the sponge back and pour water over it slowly. Which one holds on to more water? Healthy peat does the slow, spongy job best.

### Fun Fact

Sphagnum moss can hold up to **20 times its own weight in water** — like a tiny towel that's 20 times heavier soaked than dry!
