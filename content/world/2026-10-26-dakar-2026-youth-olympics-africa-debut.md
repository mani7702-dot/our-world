The Olympic rings will be raised on African soil for the very first time when **Dakar, Senegal** hosts the 2026 Summer Youth Olympic Games starting Saturday, 31 October. Here are five things young explorers should know about this exciting worldwide celebration of sport and friendship!

### 1. The Olympic Rings Come to Africa

The Youth Olympics bring together about **2,700 young athletes** from over 200 countries. The games run for 15 days across three cities — **Dakar**, Diamniadio, and Saly — and feature **153 medal events** in 25 different sports.

The Youth Olympics are designed especially for athletes aged **15 to 17**. They encourage young people to see sports as a way to build peace and friendship around the world.

### 2. New Sports You Have Probably Never Heard Of

While familiar sports like swimming and athletics are there, three brand-new sports make their debut in Dakar:

- **Baseball5** — an urban version of baseball that needs no fancy equipment, just a rubber ball and a makeshift field.
- **Wushu Taolu** — a martial art from China where competitors perform graceful, choreographed sequences.
- **Coastal rowing** — racing boats on the open sea, which is very different from traditional lake or river rowing!

Skateboarding, breaking, and surfing — all popular with young people today — are also featured.

### 3. Renovated Stadiums, Not New Ones

Rather than building expensive new venues, organizers are **renovating and repurposing existing facilities**. This means the stadiums and pools will continue to be used by local communities long after the games end.

Athletics, rugby sevens, and futsal happen at the historic Iba Mar Diop complex in Dakar. The new city of Diamniadio, southeast of Dakar, serves as the operational heart and hosts the opening ceremony at the Abdoulaye Wade Stadium.

### 4. A Mascot Named Ayo

The official mascot is **Ayo** — a cheerful lion cub who wears a traditional red-and-yellow Senegalese cap and a T-shirt with the Youth Olympics logo. Ayo holds up the green, yellow, and red flag of Senegal with its central star.

The mascot helps share Senegalese culture with the world and reminds everyone that the games celebrate both sport and heritage.

### 5. Training Young Leaders

Even before the games begin, more than **400 young Africans** have already been trained in event operations, logistics, technology, and venue management. This helps create lasting skills and jobs, not just a one-time event.

#### ❓ Fun Fact

The first Summer Youth Olympics were held in **Singapore in 2010**, and the second in **Nanjing, China in 2014**. Dakar was originally set to host in 2022, but the games were postponed because of the COVID-19 pandemic.
