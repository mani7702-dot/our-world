# Why Would Someone Dress Up Like a Ghost to Give Blood?

On **31 October 2026**, something funny and kind happened in **Bangkok**, the capital of **Thailand**. To celebrate **Halloween**, staff at the **Thai Red Cross Society** put on ghost and monster costumes — not to give people a fright, but to encourage them to do something wonderful: **give blood**.

## A spooky way to help

The Thai Red Cross called the event a **"Blood Party."** Workers dressed up, and donors who came along were handed **free Halloween-themed T-shirts** with the joke name **"BOOtiful Mind Halloween,"** plus small spooky souvenirs. There were even Halloween doughnuts called the **"HAUNTED Ghost Donut Gang,"** given out by a partner company. A cheerful **"Frightful Ghost Gang"** of costumed helpers wandered the building "haunting" the visitors from **10:00 a.m.** onwards.

You could join in at the **National Blood Centre**, at **12 regional blood donation centres**, and at the blood-donation room at **Thammasat Hospital**. The charity hoped the fun would make **more than twice as many people** turn up than on a normal Saturday.

## Why blood donations matter

Blood cannot be made in a factory — it can only come from kind people who give a little of their own. Doctors use donated blood to help people who have been in accidents, people having certain operations, and people whose bodies need help making healthy blood. One single donation can help **more than one patient**, because blood is separated into parts that each do a different job.

That is why blood centres around the world are always looking for new donors — and why dressing up as a friendly ghost is a clever trick to get people through the door.

## What happens when you donate

Giving blood is quick and simple. A grown-up sits comfortably while a nurse checks their health, then a small tube takes about a **cupful of blood**. After a short rest and a snack, most people are ready to go about their day. Donors are asked to **sleep well, drink water, and skip fatty food, smoking and alcohol** beforehand, and to tell the nurse about any medicine they take.

## Try this at home

You do not need to give blood to be a helper. Make your own **"kindness checklist"**: think of three small ways to help someone this week — carrying a bag, drawing a get-well card, or saying thank you to a nurse or doctor. Helping others is a habit, like any skill.

## Fun Fact

Your body makes **new blood all the time**. A healthy grown-up has about **5 litres (roughly 10 cups)** of blood, and after donating, the body replaces the fluid within a day and the red blood cells within a few weeks.
