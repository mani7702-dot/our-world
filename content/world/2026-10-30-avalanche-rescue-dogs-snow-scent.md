# How Can a Dog's Nose Find Someone Buried Under Snow?

## 1. A Special Kind of Helper

In the snowy Rocky Mountains of Canada, rescue teams once worked alongside some remarkable partners: **trained search-and-rescue dogs**. For 55 years, a programme in **Banff National Park** used these dogs to find hikers, skiers, and even lost children in the wilderness. This month, the programme came to an end — and thousands of people have asked for it to come back.

So what made these dogs so good at their job? The answer starts with a superpower that lives right on the end of their snout.

## 2. The Superpower in a Dog's Nose

Every person has their own **scent** — a unique mix of tiny skin flakes and natural oils that floats off our bodies and drifts into the air. To a trained rescue dog, that scent is like a name tag that never comes off.

Dogs can smell far better than humans. They have about **40 times more scent receptors** in their noses — as many as 300 million in some breeds, like bloodhounds. While our noses rest between breaths, a dog's nose keeps pulling in new smells even as it breathes out. That means a dog can follow a scent trail across a windy mountain and even smell a person **buried under a thick pile of snow**.

## 3. How the Search Works

When someone goes missing after an avalanche, rescue teams move fast. They may:
- **Split the snowfield into zones** so searchers cover every patch.
- **Send in the dogs**, who sniff the wind and trace the strongest scent toward the buried person.
- **Use probes and shovels** once a dog signals exactly where to dig.

A dog's nose can save rescuers *hours* of poking through snow — and hours matter, because a person buried in snow can only survive a short time.

## 4. Lessons From the Mountain

The Banff programme taught the world a lot about working with animals in tough places. Trainers learned that:
- **Trust the nose.** Dogs often detect a scent before any human sees a clue.
- **Start young.** Good scent dogs train for years, like athletes.
- **Respect the mountain.** Avalanche dogs themselves wear safety gear and never search alone.

Even though this programme has ended, rescue dogs still work in many mountain regions around the world, including **Italy** and the Alps.

### Try this at home

Grab a grown-up and a favourite smelly snack — like a piece of orange or a cookie. Ask them to hide it somewhere in one room while you wait outside. Then come in and use only your nose to track it down. How close did you get? Now imagine doing that across a whole snowy mountain!

### Fun Fact

An avalanche dog can sniff out a person buried under more than a metre of snow. To the dog, a human scent under all that snow is a bit like the smell of popcorn drifting out of a closed kitchen door — faint, but impossible to miss!
