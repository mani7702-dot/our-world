# How Did a Fox Cub Recover from Paralysis After Being Hit by a Car?

## The Rescue

A young fox cub named Chance was left for dead after being hit by a car in Nottinghamshire, England. A passerby found him days later, unable to move his back legs. A veterinarian feared the injury might be so severe that euthanasia would be kinder.

But wildlife rescuers at Mansfield Canine Centre would not give up. They took Chance into their care and began a careful rehabilitation program that is now showing remarkable results.

## Swimming Lessons for Recovery

Instead of giving up, the team turned to hydrotherapy — swimming sessions in a special pool normally used to help recovering dogs. The water's natural resistance and buoyancy provide a low-impact way for injured animals to regain strength and movement without putting stress on sore joints.

Chance's designated caretaker, Chelsea, guides him through the pool during each session. Slowly but steadily, Chance has started using his back legs again. He is regaining sensation, and he can now kick his legs while in the water.

## Why So Many Foxes Need Help

Mansfield Wildlife Rescue founder Cheryl Martins says they have seen a worrying rise in foxes being struck by vehicles this year. The main driver? Super-hot weather.

"A lot of the rodents that they would normally find and hunt are keeping out of the way because of the weather," Martins explained. "They're moving more into towns and cities looking for food, so they're getting into the busier roads while they're out searching."

This year alone, the rescue center has nursed five foxes hit by vehicles back to health.

## What Happens Next

To give Chance the best possible chance of returning to the wild, contact with humans has been kept to a minimum. If his recovery continues on its current path, rescuers hope he will one day be released back into the English countryside — wild and free.

> **Fun Fact:** Foxes can squeeze through openings as small as a quarter! Their skulls are narrow and their bodies are flexible, which helps them hunt in tight underground spaces.

> **Try This at Home:** You can help local wildlife by keeping cats indoors during dawn and dusk (when foxes are most active), securing garbage cans so foxes do not rummage through trash, and never feeding wild foxes — they stay healthier when they find their own food.

## What Is Hydrotherapy?

Hydrotherapy uses water's physical properties — buoyancy, resistance, and temperature — to support healing. The buoyancy helps reduce the weight on injured limbs, making it easier to move and exercise. The water resistance provides gentle strength training. This same technique is used by veterinary physiotherapists to help dogs, cats, and even horses recover from surgery or injury.

## Why Are Urban Foxes More Common?

As natural prey animals like rodents stay hidden during extreme heat, foxes follow them into human neighborhoods. Urban environments offer more shelter, food sources (like unsecured garbage), and warmer spots to rest. This brings foxes closer to busy roads, increasing the risk of vehicle collisions. Conservationists recommend securing trash, removing food sources, and creating wildlife corridors to help foxes move safely through cities.
