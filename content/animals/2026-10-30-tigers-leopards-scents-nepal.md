# Can Artificial Scents Keep Tigers and Leopards Away From Villages?

## 1. A curious idea from the forest

In the forests of Nepal, tigers and leopards sometimes wander close to villages, looking for food. That can lead to trouble for both people and wild cats. So scientists are trying something unusual: can a made-in-the-lab smell act like an invisible fence that gently turns big cats around?

## 2. How scent marks work

Big cats live by smell. Tigers and leopards rub scent marks on trees and rocks to "talk" to other cats — telling them where they live and what belongs to them. When another cat smells a fresh mark, it often understands: this spot is taken.

Researchers wondered — what if you could copy that smell and place it where you *don't* want cats to go? They call these invisible scent borders "BioBoundaries."

## 3. From Botswana to Nepal

The idea began in Botswana in the 1990s, when biologist Tico McNutt suggested that lasting scent marks could keep African wild dogs inside protected areas. Later, chemical engineer Peter Apps studied leopard urine and found a compound — called 3M3MB — that greatly reduced leopard attacks on farm animals there.

That gave researchers hope the trick might work for other cats, too. In Nepal, a team from Cornell University, WWF Nepal, and local forest authorities set up 30 test stations along the Khata Corridor, a 13-kilometre strip of land linking Bardiya National Park to a wildlife sanctuary in India.

## 4. What the cameras saw

Each station has an iron stake, a scent dispenser, and a camera trap. The cameras filmed tigers and leopards walking up, sniffing the scent, and then turning back the way they came.

But the results were mixed. Some scents made leopards keep away, while others made tigers *more* curious. Scientists learned an important lesson: different big cats respond differently — so the same trick won't work everywhere. The team is now fine-tuning which scents to use and where to place them.

## 5. Try this at home

You can explore animal "scent language" in your own neighbourhood:
- Look for trails or paths worn into grass and dirt
- Watch for pets sniffing the same spots again and again
- Notice how animals seem to follow invisible rules about where they go

Even a park or garden is full of animals sending messages we can't see.

## Fun Fact

Big cats often make a funny face called the "flehmen response" — they curl back their upper lip and hold it for a few seconds to sniff a smell more deeply. It looks like a grimace, but it actually helps them taste the air!
