# 4 Spooky-Cool Things to See in October's Night Sky

# The cosmos's Halloween special is already playing

October is like the universe's own Halloween party — and the best seats are right outside your door. While ghosts and goblins get all the attention, space is putting on a show that makes pumpkin carved faces look tame by comparison. Four amazing things are happening in our night sky this month, and you don't need a telescope or a spacesuit to see them.

## 1. Jupiter's disappearing act (October 6)

Picture this: you're looking out before sunrise and the brightest "star" in your sky slowly... vanishes. That's what skywatchers in eastern and central North America saw when **Jupiter**, the largest planet in our solar system, slipped behind the Moon's thin crescent like a cosmic magic trick.

Astronomers call this a **lunar occultation** — just a fancy word for one space object hiding another from our view. It happens when the Moon drifts directly between Earth and something farther away. Don't worry, Jupiter wasn't really gone. It just took about an hour for the Moon to move out of the way.

## 2. The Draconid meteor shower (October 8-9)

If you looked up on the evening of October 8, you might have seen a handful of shooting stars streaking across the sky — and the best part is, they showed up early! Unlike most meteor showers that put on their best show after midnight, the **Draconids** put on a cozy pre-midnight performance.

These meteors get their name from **Draco**, the constellation that looks like a dragon (yes, like the one from the vampire stories!). But this dragon isn't scary — it's made of the same stardust that lights up every shooting star you've ever seen.

## 3. Halley's Comet's gift: The Orionids (October 21)

Remember Halley's Comet, the spectacular visitor that last swung by in 1986? Well, it left behind something extra special — a trail of tiny space pebbles and dust that Earth is plowing through right now. When these bits slam into our atmosphere at tens of thousands of miles per hour, they light up as **Orionid meteors**.

You can see them radiating from **Orion the Hunter** constellation (another Halloween connection!). From a dark spot away from city lights, you might catch 3 to 6 meteors per hour — each one a tiny piece of a comet that won't visit again until 2061!

## 4. The Hunter's Moon glow (October 26)

Rounding out October's spooky spectacular is the legendary **Hunter's Moon** — a fat, orange full moon that rises in time for Halloween. It got its name because hunters used its bright light to gather food for winter.

This year, the full moon glowed in Universal Time on October 26 (and on October 25 for most of North America). It's like the universe put up the biggest, brightest nightlight just in time for trick-or-treaters.

**Fun Fact:** The Hunter's Moon isn't actually special by cosmic standards — it's just the first full moon after the Harvest Moon, but it happens to fall right before Halloween, making it feel extra spooky!

**Try this at home:** You can build your own mini planetarium! Take a dark room, poke tiny holes in a piece of cardboard (representing different stars), and shine a flashlight through it onto a white wall. You'll see your very own star pattern. Try changing the holes to make different constellations — see if you can make the Big Dipper or Orion!
