# 10 True Tales from the Tower of London's 1,000-Year History

**for-grownups:** This article is based on historical records and archaeological findings. While the Tower of London is a real place with a rich, documented past, some stories have grown taller through the centuries—history here is fascinating, not frightening.

## 1. The Conqueror's Castle (1066)

Right after winning the Battle of Hastings in 1066, William the Conqueror built the Tower of London to make sure everyone knew who was boss. The first White Tower took about 20 years to build, with stonemasons from Normandy and stone shipped from Caen, France. People didn't love it at first—it was a giant reminder that the Normans were now in charge!

## 2. The Last Execution (1941)

You might think the spooky old Tower stopped being a prison long ago, but it didn't! The last person executed inside the Tower was Josef Jakobs, a German spy during World War II. He parachuted into England in 1941, broke his leg on landing, and was caught soon after. He was shot on August 15, 1941—the very last execution at the Tower.

## 3. The Only Woman Tortured

Out of hundreds of prisoners, only one woman was ever tortured at the Tower: Anne Askew in 1546. She was a Protestant writer who was put on the rack for her beliefs. She couldn't walk afterward and was later burned at the stake in 1546.

## 4. The Great Jewel Heist (1671)

Irish adventurer Thomas Blood had the boldest plan: steal the Crown Jewels right from the Tower! In 1671, he dressed up as a priest and befriended the Jewel House keeper, then attacked him and grabbed the crown, scepter, and orb. He even tried to flatten the crown with a hammer to make it easier to sell. But guess what—King Charles II was so impressed by Blood's nerve that he pardoned him!

## 5. The Escape Artist Bishop

Ranulf Flambard, a bishop from Durham, became the Tower's first prisoner—and also its first escape artist. After falling out of favor with King Henry I around 1100, Flambard got his guards drunk by sharing a hidden gallon of wine, then climbed out through a window using a rope. History's first prison break!

## 6. The Disguised Earl

In 1715, after a failed rebellion, Earl of Nithsdale was sentenced to death. His wife Winifred wasn't having it! She smuggled women's clothes into the Tower, helped him dress up as a lady, painted on a fake beard, and even answered his own door in disguise. While the guards thought Nithsdale was still inside, he walked right out. They lived in France after that.

## 7. The Nine-Day Queen

Lady Jane Grey was only 16 when she became queen for nine days in 1553. She was put on the throne to keep Catholics from taking power, but it didn't work. Mary Tudor soon took over, and Jane was sent to the Tower. She was executed at age 17, but her ghost is said to still walk the Tower's halls.

## 8. The Missing Princes

In 1483, 12-year-old Edward V and his 9-year-old brother Richard were locked in the Tower by their uncle, who became King Richard III. The boys were supposed to be crowned, but instead, they just... vanished. In 1674, workers found two skeletons at the Tower. Were they the boys? We'll never know for sure—their fate remains one of history's greatest mysteries.

## 9. The Royal Menagerie

For over 600 years, the Tower had its own zoo! In 1252, Norway's king sent a polar bear that was allowed to swim in the Thames. Louis IX of France gave Henry III an elephant (who didn't last long in London). By the 1700s, the collection had lions, tigers, eagles, and even jackals. The animals even inspired one of the first April Fools' jokes—in 1686, visitors were told to come see the "washing of the lions," which didn't exist!

## 10. The Peasant Rebellion

During the Great Revolt of 1381, thousands of angry peasants marched on London. They stormed the Tower, dragged out the Archbishop of Canterbury and the Lord High Treasurer, and beheaded them right on Tower Hill. One of the rebels, Joanna Ferrour, is thought to have personally carried out the executions!

**Fun Fact:** The Tower has been used as a royal palace, an armory, a treasury, and even a home for ravens! Legend says if the ravens ever leave, the kingdom will fall.
