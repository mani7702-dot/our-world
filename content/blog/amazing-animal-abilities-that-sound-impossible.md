# 7 amazing things animals can do that sound impossible

Nature is full of surprises. Scientists keep finding that animals can do things that sound like superhero powers — and they are all real. Here are seven of the strangest discoveries.

## 1. A seal named Hoover who said hello

Most people think only humans can talk. But **Hoover**, a harbor seal at the New England Aquarium in the 1970s and 1980s, learned to copy human words. He would say things like "hello there" and "hurry, hurry" in a deep, rough voice. Modern audio analysis confirmed he was not just making seal sounds — he was shaping his vocal tract to sound like speech. Hoover the seal even had his own Wikipedia article.

## 2. Bees that make silk stronger than spider webs

Silk comes from silkworms and spiders, right? Wrong! **Blue orchard bees** also spin silk — but their silk is tougher because it does not break easily. Scientists discovered that a bee larva anchors silk to the wall of its nest, stretches it across the space, and weaves a cocoon that parasitoid wasps cannot pierce with their stingers.

## 3. Birds that use fire to hunt

In northern Australia, a type of bird called the **black kite** (also known as the "firehawk") does something no other animal is known for: it picks up burning sticks and drops them in dry grass to **start new fires**. The flames flush out insects and small animals, making them easy prey. Researchers published this in the Journal of Ethnobiology in 2017, and Aboriginal rangers have known about it for centuries.

## 4. Elephants that call each other by name

Elephants live in tight family groups and communicate with deep rumbles that travel for miles. A 2026 study found that elephants **recognize the specific calls of their family members** and answer more enthusiastically when called by their own name-like rumble. They even know which direction to move toward the sound.

## 5. Bumblebee queens that survive a week underwater

A bumblebee queen hibernates underground all winter, but sometimes her nest floods. Researchers discovered that when condensation filled their containers, **90% of submerged bumblebee queens survived for seven days underwater**. Their fuzzy bodies trap a thin layer of air that acts like a snorkel, letting them breathe until the flood drains away.

## 6. A tiny mite that has not needed sex for 20 million years

Most living things reproduce with two parents. But the **oribatid mite** (*Platynothrus peltifer*) has been doing it alone for over 20 million years through **parthenogenesis** — babies with no dad needed. Its chromosomes have evolved a workaround so harmful mutations do not pile up, proving that sex is not the only path to success.

## 7. Whales that blow underwater bubble rings

Humpback whales are famous for their songs and bubble-net feeding, but some whales do something stranger: they blow **perfect underwater smoke rings** from their blowholes. Scientists watched 11 whales create 39 bubble rings, sometimes directing them toward boats and swimmers. The whales may be playing, communicating, or just curious.

### Try this at home

Try "talking" like Hoover the seal. Put your hands over your teeth and hum — the vibration changes the sound. You are not really copying speech, but you are feeling how vocal tract shape affects what comes out, just like a talking seal.

### Fun Fact

The black kite is also called a "firehawk" — and it is the only bird besides humans known to spread fire on purpose. When it spots a wildfire, it grabs a burning twig and drops it in fresh grass nearby, starting a new fire so prey flees into the open.
