Imagine a universe where stars still shine, planets still form, and water still exists — but the water is as thick as honey. Even with all the right ingredients for life, every living cell would be stuck. Nutrients could not flow, signals could not travel, and life as we know it would never get off the ground.

That is the puzzle physicist Kostya Trachenko at Queen Mary University of London has been studying. His discovery is a surprising one: the very same numbers that control how the universe works from the tiniest scale up — like the mass of an electron and Planck's constant — might also be the reason water flows just right for life to exist.

## The numbers that set the rules

Every universe has its own rulebook. Our constants include the charge of an electron (how strongly it attracts or repels other particles), the Planck constant (the grain size of quantum reality), and a handful of others. Together, these numbers decide how strongly atoms bond, how hot stars burn, and how freely liquids pour.

For decades, scientists have wondered: why do these constants have the exact values they do? Change them even a little, and stars would burn out, atoms would fall apart, or chemistry would become impossible. The fact that they seem fine-tuned for complexity is one of the biggest unsolved questions in physics.

## Water that is too thick

Trachenko's work zooms in on one everyday detail: how thick liquids are. That property is called **viscosity** — think of water (runs fast) versus honey (runs slow).

His team showed that fundamental physical constants set a lower limit on how runny any liquid can be. Water flows easily because our constants allow it. But if even one of those numbers were off by just a few percent, that same water could thicken to the consistency of tar. Blood would sludge through your veins. Nutrients would crawl instead of flow. Life depends on liquids that move at just the right speed.

As Trachenko explains it: a glass of water and the grandest question about the cosmos are more connected than they first appear.

## When the stars and the cells agree

Here is the remarkable part. The constants that let stars forge the elements we need — carbon, oxygen, iron — are the very same constants that keep our blood and cells flowing. One set of numbers does double duty: it makes both starlight and life possible.

That double-match is what makes the universe look so suspiciously life-friendly. In a hypothetical universe where stars still cooked up the right ingredients but liquids were too thick, the recipe for life would still fail. So the tolerance window for life may be far narrower than scientists once thought.

## Try this at home

Fill two clear cups. Put honey in one and water in the other. Drop a raisin into each. Watch the raisin in water zoom down, then slowly rise again as bubbles form — that is buoyancy and diffusion at work. Now watch the same raisin in honey barely move. The thickness of the liquid (its viscosity) sets how fast everything moves. Cells in your body rely on that same principle — only their liquids are tuned to perfect flow by the universe's deepest constants.

## Fun fact

The viscosity of liquid helium — the coldest, weirdest liquid we know — gets so low it can climb the walls of its container and shoot out the top. At near-absolute-zero temperatures, the physics breaks the usual rules entirely, proving just how special our everyday room-temperature world really is.
