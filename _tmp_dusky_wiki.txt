The **dusky shark** (_**Carcharhinus obscurus**_) is a [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species") of [requiem shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requiem_shark "Requiem shark"), in the family Carcharhinidae, occurring in tropical and warm-temperate continental seas worldwide. A [generalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalist_and_specialist_species "Generalist and specialist species")[apex predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apex_predator "Apex predator"), the dusky shark can be found from the coast to the outer [continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf "Continental shelf") and adjacent [pelagic waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelagic_zone "Pelagic zone"), and has been recorded from a depth of 400 m (1,300 ft). Populations [migrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_migration "Fish migration") seasonally towards the poles in the summer and towards the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator "Equator") in the winter, traveling hundreds to thousands of kilometers. One of the largest members of its genus, the dusky shark reaches more than 4 m (13 ft) in length and 350 kg (770 lb) in weight. It has a slender, streamlined body and can be identified by its short round snout, long sickle-shaped [pectoral fins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectoral_fin "Pectoral fin"), ridge between the first and second [dorsal fins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsal_fin "Dorsal fin"), and faintly marked fins.

Adult dusky sharks have a broad and varied diet, consisting mostly of [bony fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bony_fish "Bony fish"), [sharks and rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasmobranch "Elasmobranch"), and [cephalopods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalopod "Cephalopod"), but also occasionally [crustaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustacean "Crustacean"), [sea stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_star "Sea star"), [bryozoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryozoan "Bryozoan"), [sea turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_turtle "Sea turtle"), [marine mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_mammal "Marine mammal"), and [carrion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrion "Carrion"). This species is [viviparous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viviparous "Viviparous") with a three-year reproductive cycle; females bear litters of 3–14 young after a [gestation period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestation_period "Gestation period") of 22–24 months, after which there is a year of rest before they become pregnant again. This shark, tied with the [Spiny dogfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiny_dogfish "Spiny dogfish") as a result is the animal with the longest gestation period. Females are capable of storing [sperm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sperm "Sperm") for long periods, as their encounters with suitable mates may be few and far between due to their nomadic lifestyle and low overall abundance. Dusky sharks are one of the slowest-growing and latest-maturing sharks, not reaching adulthood until around 20 years of age.

Because of its slow reproductive rate, the dusky shark is very vulnerable to human-caused population depletion. This species is highly valued by [commercial fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_fishing "Commercial fishing") for its fins, used in [shark fin soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_fin_soup "Shark fin soup"), and for its meat, skin, and [liver oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_liver_oil "Shark liver oil"). It is also esteemed by [recreational fishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_fishing "Recreational fishing"). The [International Union for Conservation of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_for_Conservation_of_Nature "International Union for Conservation of Nature") (IUCN) has assessed this species as [Endangered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered_species_(IUCN_status) "Endangered species (IUCN status)") worldwide and [Vulnerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerable_species "Vulnerable species") off the eastern United States, where populations have dropped to 15–20% of 1970s levels. The dusky shark is regarded as potentially dangerous to humans due to its large size, but there are few [attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_attack "Shark attack") attributable to it.

French [naturalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalist "Naturalist")[Charles Alexandre Lesueur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Alexandre_Lesueur "Charles Alexandre Lesueur") published the first [scientific description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_description "Scientific description") of the dusky shark in an 1818 issue of _Journal of the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia_. He placed it in the genus _Squalus_ and gave it the [specific epithet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_name_(zoology) "Specific name (zoology)")_obscurus_ ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") for "dark" or "dim"), referring to its coloration.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-lesueur-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-ebert-5) Subsequent authors have recognized this species as belonging to the genus _Carcharhinus_. Lesueur did not designate a [type specimen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_specimen "Type specimen"), though he was presumably working from a shark caught in North American waters.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6)

Many early sources gave the scientific name of the dusky shark as _Carcharias_ (later _Carcharhinus_) _lamiella_, which originated from an 1882 account by [David Starr Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Starr_Jordan "David Starr Jordan") and [Charles Henry Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Henry_Gilbert "Charles Henry Gilbert"). Although Jordan and Gilbert referred to a set of jaws that came from a dusky shark, the type specimen they designated was later discovered to be a [copper shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_shark "Copper shark") (_C. brachyurus_). Therefore, _C.lamiella_ is not considered a [synonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synonym_(taxonomy) "Synonym (taxonomy)") of _C.obscurus_ but rather of _C.brachyurus_.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-ebert-5)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-jordan_and_gilbert-7) Other [common names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_name "Common name") for this species include bay shark, black whaler, brown common gray shark, brown dusky shark, brown shark, common whaler, dusky ground shark, dusky whaler, river whaler, shovelnose, and slender whaler shark.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-fishbase-8)

## Phylogeny and evolution

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dusky_shark&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Phylogeny and evolution")]

Phylogenetic relationships of the dusky shark, based on allozyme sequences[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-naylor-9)

Teeth belonging to the dusky shark are fairly well represented in the [fossil record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_record "Fossil record"), though assigning _Carcharhinus_ teeth to species can be problematic.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-heim_and_bourdon-10) Dusky shark teeth dating to the [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene "Miocene") (23–5.3 [Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mya_(unit) "Mya (unit)")) have been recovered from the Kendeace and Grand Bay formations in [Carriacou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carriacou "Carriacou"), the Grenadines,[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-portell_et_al-11) the Moghra Formation in Egypt,[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-cook_et_al-12)[Polk County, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polk_County,_Florida "Polk County, Florida"),[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-brown-13) and possibly Cerro La Cruz in northern Venezuela.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-sanchez-villagra_et_al-14)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-15) Teeth dating to the [Late Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Miocene "Late Miocene") or [Early Pliocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Pliocene "Early Pliocene") (11.6–3.6 Ma) are abundant in the Yorktown Formation and the [Pungo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pungo_River "Pungo River"), North Carolina, and from the [Chesapeake Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_Bay "Chesapeake Bay") region; these teeth differ slightly from the modern dusky shark, and have often been misidentified as belonging to the [oceanic whitetip shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_whitetip_shark "Oceanic whitetip shark") (_C. longimanus_).[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-heim_and_bourdon-10) Dusky shark teeth have also been recovered from the vicinity of two [baleen whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baleen_whale "Baleen whale") in North Carolina, one preserved in Goose Creek Limestone dating to the [Late Pliocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pliocene "Late Pliocene") (c. 3.5 Ma), and the other in mud dating to the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene "Pleistocene")-[Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene "Holocene") (c. 12,000 years ago).[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-cicimurri_and_knight-16)

In 1982, [Jack Garrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Garrick "Jack Garrick") published a [phylogenetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetic "Phylogenetic") analysis of _Carcharhinus_ based on [morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_(biology) "Morphology (biology)"), in which he placed the dusky shark and the [Galapagos shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galapagos_shark "Galapagos shark") (_C. galapagensis_) at the center of the "obscurus group". The group consisted of large, triangular-toothed sharks with a ridge between the [dorsal fins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsal_fin "Dorsal fin"), and also included the [bignose shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bignose_shark "Bignose shark") (_C. altimus_), the [Caribbean reef shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_reef_shark "Caribbean reef shark") (_C. perezi_), the [sandbar shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandbar_shark "Sandbar shark") (_C. plumbeus_), and the oceanic whitetip shark.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-garrick-17) This interpretation was largely upheld by [Leonard Compagno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Compagno "Leonard Compagno") in his 1988 [phenetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenetic "Phenetic") study,[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno2-18) and by Gavin Naylor in his 1992 [allozyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allozyme "Allozyme") sequence study. Naylor was able to further resolve the interrelationships of the "ridge-backed" branch of _Carcharhinus_, finding that the dusky shark, Galapagos shark, oceanic whitetip shark, and [blue shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_shark "Blue shark") (_Prionace glauca_) comprise its most [derived](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synapomorphy "Synapomorphy")[clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade "Clade").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-naylor-9)

## Distribution and habitat

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dusky_shark&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Distribution and habitat")]

[![Image 6: A grey shark swimming in shallow, sun-dappled waters, with a large school of smaller fish in the background](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Dusky_shark_seaworld.jpg/330px-Dusky_shark_seaworld.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dusky_shark_seaworld.jpg)

A female dusky shark on display at Sea World, Queensland; this species is found throughout Australian waters.

The range of the dusky shark extends worldwide, albeit discontinuously, in tropical and warm-temperate waters. In the western Atlantic Ocean, it is found from Massachusetts and the [Georges Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Bank "Georges Bank") to southern Brazil, including the Bahamas and Cuba. In the eastern Atlantic Ocean, it has been reported from the western and central Mediterranean Sea, the Canary Islands, Cape Verde, Senegal, Sierra Leone, and possibly elsewhere including Portugal, Spain, Morocco, and Madeira. In the Indian Ocean, it is found off South Africa, Mozambique, and Madagascar, with sporadic records in the Arabian Sea, the Bay of Bengal, and perhaps the Red Sea. In the Pacific Ocean, it occurs off Japan, mainland China and Taiwan, Vietnam, Australia, and New Caledonia in the west, and from southern California to the Gulf of California, around Revillagigedo, and possibly off northern Chile in the east. Records of dusky sharks from the northeastern and eastern central Atlantic, and around tropical islands, may in fact be of Galapagos sharks.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-iucn_status_19_November_2021-1)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno_et_al-3)[Mitochondrial DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_DNA "Mitochondrial DNA") and [microsatellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsatellite_(genetics) "Microsatellite (genetics)") evidence suggest that Indonesian and Australian sharks represent distinct populations.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-ovenden_et_al-19)

Residing off continental coastlines from the [surf zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surf_zone "Surf zone") to the outer [continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf "Continental shelf") and adjacent oceanic waters, the dusky shark occupies an intermediate [habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat "Habitat") that overlaps with its more specialized relatives, such as the inshore [sandbar shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandbar_shark "Sandbar shark"), the pelagic [silky shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silky_shark "Silky shark") (_C. falciformis_) and [oceanic whitetip shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_whitetip_shark "Oceanic whitetip shark"), the deepwater [bignose shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bignose_shark "Bignose shark"), and the islandic [Galapagos shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galapagos_shark "Galapagos shark") and [silvertip shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvertip_shark "Silvertip shark") (_C. albimarginatus_).[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6) One tracking study in the northern Gulf of Mexico found that it spends most of its time at depths of 10–80 m (33–262 ft), while making occasional forays below 200 m (660 ft); this species has been known to dive as deep as 400 m (1,300 ft). It prefers water temperatures of 19–28°C (66–82°F), and avoids areas of low [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity "Salinity") such as [estuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuaries "Estuaries").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-ebert-5)[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-hoffmayer_et_al-20)

The dusky shark is nomadic and strongly [migratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_migration "Fish migration"), undertaking recorded movements of up to 3,800 km (2,400 mi); adults generally move longer distances than juveniles. Sharks along both coasts of North America shift northward with warmer summer temperatures, and retreat back towards the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator "Equator") in winter.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-iucn_status_19_November_2021-1) Off South Africa, young males and females over 0.9 m (3.0 ft) long disperse southward and northward respectively (with some overlap) from the nursery area off [KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu-Natal "KwaZulu-Natal"); they join the adults several years later by a yet-unidentified route. In addition, juveniles spend spring and summer in the surf zone and fall and winter in offshore waters, and as they approach 2.2 m (7.2 ft) in length begin to conduct a north–south migration between KwaZulu-Natal in the winter and the [Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Cape "Western Cape") in summer. Still-larger sharks, over 2.8 m (9.2 ft) long, migrate as far as southern Mozambique.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-iucn_status_19_November_2021-1)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-van_der_elst_and_borchert-21) Off [Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australia "Western Australia"), adult and juvenile dusky sharks migrate towards the coast in summer and fall, though not to the inshore nurseries occupied by newborns.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-iucn_status_19_November_2021-1)

Alongside the bull shark, the dusky shark is the largest members of its genus, commonly reaching a length of 3.2 m (10 ft) and a weight of 160–180 kg (350–400 lb); the maximum recorded length and weight are 4.2 m (14 ft) and 372 kg (820 lb) respectively.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-fishbase-8)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-knickle-22)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-23) However, the maximum reported size of the species is 4.5 m (15 ft), while the maximum weight is reported to reach up to 500 kg (1,100 lb).[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-24) Females grow larger than males.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-natanson_et_al-25) This shark has a slender, streamlined body with a broadly rounded snout no longer than the width of the mouth. The [nostrils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nostril "Nostril") are preceded by barely developed flaps of skin. The medium-sized, circular eyes are equipped with [nictitating membranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nictitating_membrane "Nictitating membrane") (protective third eyelids). The mouth has very short, subtle furrows at the corners and contains 13–15 (typically 14) tooth rows on either side of both jaws. The upper teeth are distinctively broad, triangular, and slightly oblique with strong, coarse serrations, while the lower teeth are narrower and upright, with finer serrations. The five pairs of [gill slits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gill_slit "Gill slit") are fairly long.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-knickle-22)

The large [pectoral fins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectoral_fin "Pectoral fin") measure around one-fifth as long as the body, and have a falcate (sickle-like) shape tapering to a point. The first [dorsal fin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsal_fin "Dorsal fin") is of moderate size and somewhat falcate, with a pointed apex and a strongly concave rear margin; its origin lies over the pectoral fin free rear tips. The second dorsal fin is much smaller and is positioned about opposite the [anal fin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anal_fin "Anal fin"). A low dorsal ridge is present between the dorsal fins. The [caudal fin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudal_fin "Caudal fin") is large and high, with a well-developed lower lobe and a ventral notch near the tip of the upper lobe.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-mceachran_and_fechhelm-26) The [dermal denticles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dermal_denticle "Dermal denticle") are diamond-shaped and closely set, each bearing five horizontal ridges leading to teeth on the posterior margin.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-knickle-22) This species is bronzy to bluish gray above and white below, which extends onto the flanks as a faint lighter stripe. The fins, particularly the underside of the pectoral fins and the lower caudal fin lobe) darken towards the tips; this is more obvious in juveniles.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-last_and_stevens-27) Dusky sharks can be found at Redondo Beach, southern California to the Gulf of California, and to Ecuador. But sometimes rarely off southern California; common in tropics. Dusky sharks have a total length of at least 3.6 m (11.8 ft) or possibly to 4.2 m (13.8 ft). At birth, dusky sharks are about a length of 70–100 cm (27.6-39.3 in). In the surf zone, dusky sharks swim to a depth of 573 m (1,879 ft). Dusky sharks have a color of Gray or beige.

## Biology and ecology

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dusky_shark&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Biology and ecology")]

As an [apex predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apex_predator "Apex predator") positioned at the highest level of the [trophic web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trophic_web "Trophic web"), the dusky shark is generally less abundant than other sharks that share its range.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6) However, high concentrations of individuals, especially juveniles, can be found at particular locations.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-ebert-5) Adults are often found following ships far from land, such as in the [Agulhas Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agulhas_Current "Agulhas Current").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-van_der_elst_and_borchert-21) A tracking study off the mouth of the [Cape Fear River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fear_River "Cape Fear River") in North Carolina reported an average swimming speed of 0.8 km/h (0.50 mph).[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-huish_and_benedict-28) The dusky shark is one of the hosts of the [sharksucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echeneis_naucrates "Echeneis naucrates") (_Echeneis naucrates_).[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-schwartz-29) Known [parasites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasite "Parasite") of this species include the [tapeworms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapeworm "Tapeworm")_Anthobothrium triloculatum,_[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-caira_and_jensen-35) the [monogeneans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogenea "Monogenea")_Dermophthirius carcharhini_[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-bullard_et_al-36) and _Loimos salpinggoides_,[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-maccullum-37) the marinus_).[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-jensen_and_schwartz-40)

Full-grown dusky sharks have no significant natural predators.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-knickle-22) Major predators of young sharks include the [ragged tooth shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragged_tooth_shark "Ragged tooth shark") (_Carcharias taurus_), the [great white shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_white_shark "Great white shark") (_Carcharodon carcharias_), the [bull shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_shark "Bull shark") (_C. leucas_), and the [tiger shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_shark "Tiger shark") (_Galeocerdo cuvier_). Off KwaZulu-Natal, the use of [shark nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_net "Shark net") to protect beaches has reduced the populations of these large predators, leading to a dramatic increase in the number of juvenile dusky sharks (a phenomenon called "[predator release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predator_release?action=edit&redlink=1 "Predator release (page does not exist)")"). In turn, the juvenile sharks have decimated populations of small [bony fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bony_fish "Bony fish"), with negative consequences for the [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity "Biodiversity") of the local [ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem "Ecosystem").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6)[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-martin-41)

[![Image 11: A silvery, spindle-shaped ray-finned fish with a forked tail](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Bluefish_noaa.jpg/330px-Bluefish_noaa.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bluefish_noaa.jpg)

The bluefish is a major prey species of dusky sharks in the northwestern Atlantic.

The dusky shark is a [generalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalist_and_specialist_species "Generalist and specialist species") that takes a wide variety of prey from all levels of the [water column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_column "Water column"), though it favors hunting near the bottom.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-last_and_stevens-27)[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-gelsleichter_et_al-42) A large individual can consume over a tenth of its body weight at a single sitting.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-hussey_et_al-43) The bite force exerted by a 2 m (6.6 ft) long dusky shark has been measured at 60 kg (130 lb) over the 2 mm 2 (0.0031 in 2) area at the tip of a tooth. This is the highest figure thus far measured from any shark, though it also reflects the concentration of force at the tooth tip.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-martin2-44) Dense aggregations of young sharks, forming in response to feeding opportunities, have been documented in the Indian Ocean.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-iucn_status_19_November_2021-1)

The known diet of the dusky shark encompasses [pelagic fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelagic_fish "Pelagic fish"), including [herring and anchovies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clupeiformes "Clupeiformes"), [tuna and mackerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scombridae "Scombridae"), [billfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billfish "Billfish"), [jacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carangidae "Carangidae"), [needlefish and flyingfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beloniformes "Beloniformes"), [threadfins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threadfin "Threadfin"), [hairtails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hairtail "Hairtail"), [lancetfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancetfish "Lancetfish"), and [lanternfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanternfish "Lanternfish"); [demersal fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demersal_fish "Demersal fish"), including [mullets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugilidae "Mugilidae"), [porgies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparidae "Sparidae"), [grunts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciaenidae "Sciaenidae"), and [flatheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platycephalidae "Platycephalidae"), [eels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eel "Eel"), [lizardfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizardfish "Lizardfish"), [cusk eels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cusk_eel "Cusk eel"), [gurnards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurnard_(disambiguation) "Gurnard (disambiguation)"), and [flatfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatfish "Flatfish"); [reef fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reef_fish "Reef fish"), including [barracudas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barracuda "Barracuda"), [goatfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goatfish "Goatfish"), [spadefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spadefish "Spadefish"), [groupers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouper "Grouper"), [scorpionfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpionfish "Scorpionfish"), and [porcupinefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcupinefish "Porcupinefish"); [cartilaginous fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartilaginous_fish "Cartilaginous fish"), including [dogfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squaliformes "Squaliformes"), [sawsharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawshark "Sawshark"), [angel sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_shark "Angel shark"), [catsharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scyliorhinidae "Scyliorhinidae"), [thresher sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thresher_shark "Thresher shark"), [smoothhounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustelus "Mustelus"), smaller requiem sharks, [sawfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawfish "Sawfish"), [guitarfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitarfish "Guitarfish"), [skates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajidae "Rajidae"), [stingrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stingray "Stingray"), and [butterfly rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterfly_ray "Butterfly ray"); and [invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate "Invertebrate"), including [gastropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastropod "Gastropod"), [cephalopods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalopod "Cephalopod"), [decapod crustaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decapoda "Decapoda"), [barnacles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnacle "Barnacle"), and [sea stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_star "Sea star").

Very rarely, the largest dusky sharks may also consume [sea turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_turtle "Sea turtle"), [marine mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_mammal "Marine mammal") (mainly as [carrion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrion "Carrion")), and human refuse.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-ebert-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6)[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-gelsleichter_et_al-42)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-gubanov-45)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-46) In waters of [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts"), dusky sharks have been observed multiple times actively prey on [grey seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_seal "Grey seal") (_Halichoerus grypus_).[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-47) In 2014, a group of 10-20 dusky sharks were observed hunting and presumbaly killing a [humpback whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humpback_whale "Humpback whale") calf.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-48)

In the northwestern Atlantic, around 60% of the dusky shark's diet consists of bony fishes, from over ten families with [bluefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluefish "Bluefish") (_Pomatomus saltatrix_) and [summer flounder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_flounder "Summer flounder") (_Paralichthys dentatus_) being especially important. Cartilaginous fishes, mainly skates and their [egg cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_case_(Chondrichthyes) "Egg case (Chondrichthyes)"), are the second-most important dietary component, while the [lady crab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_crab "Lady crab") (_Ovalipes ocellatus_) is also a relatively significant food source.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-gelsleichter_et_al-42) In South African and Australian waters, bony fishes are again the most important prey type. Newborn and juvenile sharks subsist mainly on small pelagic prey such as [sardines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardine "Sardine") and [squid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squid "Squid"); older sharks over 2 m (6.6 ft) long broaden their diets to include larger bony and cartilaginous fishes.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-simpfendorfer_et_al-49)[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-smale-50) The [run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardine_run "Sardine run") of the [southern African pilchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinops_sagax "Sardinops sagax") (_Sardinops sagax_), occurring off the eastern coast of South Africa every winter, is attended by medium and large-sized dusky sharks. Pregnant and post-partum females do not join, possibly because the energy cost of gestation leaves them unable to pursue such swift prey.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-hussey_et_al-43) One South African study reported that 0.2% of the sharks examined had preyed upon [bottlenose dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottlenose_dolphin "Bottlenose dolphin") (_Tursiops truncatus_).[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-cockcroft_et_al-51)

Like other requiem sharks, the dusky shark is [viviparous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viviparous "Viviparous"): the developing [embryos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryo "Embryo") are initially nourished by a [yolk sac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yolk_sac "Yolk sac"), which is converted into a [placental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta "Placenta") connection to the mother once the [yolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yolk "Yolk") supply is exhausted. [Mating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mating "Mating") occurs during spring in the northwestern Atlantic, while there appears to be no reproductive seasonality in other regions such as off South Africa.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-ebert-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6) Females are capable of storing masses of [sperm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sperm "Sperm"), possibly from multiple males, for months to years within their [nidamental glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidamental_gland "Nidamental gland") (an organ that secretes egg cases). This would be advantageous given the sharks' itinerant natures and low natural abundance, which would make encounters with suitable mates infrequent and unpredictable.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-pratt-52)

With a [gestation period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestation_period "Gestation period") estimated at up to 22–24 months and a one-year resting period between pregnancies, female dusky sharks bear at most one litter of young every three years.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-iucn_status_19_November_2021-1) Litter size ranges from 3 to 16, with 6 to 12 being typical, and does not correlate with female size.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-ebert-5)[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-hussey_et_al-43) Sharks in the western Atlantic tend to produce slightly smaller litters than those from the southeastern Atlantic (averaging 8 versus 10 pups per litter).[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-knickle-22) Depending on region, birthing may occur throughout the year or over a span of several months: newborn sharks have been reported from late winter to summer in the northwestern Atlantic, in summer and fall off Western Australia, and throughout the year with a peak in fall off southern Africa.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-last_and_stevens-27) Females move into shallow inshore habitats such as [lagoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoon "Lagoon") to give birth, as such areas offer their pups rich food supplies and shelter from predation (including from their own species), and leave immediately afterward.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-ebert-5)[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-smale-50) These nursery areas are known along the coasts of KwaZulu-Natal, southwestern Australia, western Baja California, and the eastern United States from New Jersey to North Carolina.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-iucn_status_19_November_2021-1)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-ebert-5)

Newborn dusky sharks measure 0.7–1.0 m (2.3–3.3 ft) long;[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-iucn_status_19_November_2021-1) pup size increases with female size, and decreases with litter size. There is evidence that females can determine the size at which their pups are born, so as to improve their chances of survival across better or worse environmental conditions. Females also provision their young with energy reserves, stored in a liver that comprises one-fifth of the pup's weight, which sustains the newborn until it learns to hunt for itself.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-hussey_et_al-43) The dusky shark is one of the slowest-growing shark species, reaching [sexual maturity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_maturity "Sexual maturity") only at a substantial size and age (see table).[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-fowler_et_al-58) Various studies have found growth rates to be largely similar across geographical regions and between sexes.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-natanson_and_kohler-53)[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-white-55)[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-simpfendorfer_et_al2-56) The annual growth rate is 8–11 cm (3.1–4.3 in) over the first five years of life.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-simpfendorfer-59) The [maximum lifespan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_lifespan "Maximum lifespan") is believed to be 40–50 years or more.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-fowler_et_al-58)

The dusky shark is considered to be potentially dangerous to humans because of its large size, though little is known of how it behaves towards people underwater.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6) As of 2009, the [International Shark Attack File](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Shark_Attack_File "International Shark Attack File") lists it as responsible for six attacks on people and boats, three of them unprovoked and one fatal.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-isaf-60) However, attacks attributed to this species off Bermuda and other islands were probably in reality caused by Galapagos sharks.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6) It is also likely that this species of shark (along with potentially other species) was responsible for a very rare fatal shark attack off the [Haderan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadera "Hadera") coastline in April 2025, in which a snorkeler was killed and eaten.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-61)[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-62) A study determined that one shark, attracted by the faint electromagnetic signature of the 40-year old man's [GoPro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GoPro "GoPro"), committed a "[reflex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflex "Reflex")/clumsiness bite" which drew blood. The diffusion of blood in the water, along with the sound of the initial bite, then led other sharks in the school to bite the man predatorily, which lead to a [feeding frenzy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeding_frenzy "Feeding frenzy") resulting in the destruction of his body; "very small quantities" of human remains were recovered from the area the day after.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-63)

[Shark nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_net "Shark net") used to protect beaches in South Africa and Australia entangle adult and larger juvenile dusky sharks in some numbers. From 1978 to 1999, an average of 256 individuals were caught annually in nets off KwaZulu-Natal; species-specific data is not available for nets off Australia.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-fowler_et_al-58)

Young dusky sharks adapt well to display in [public aquariums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_aquarium "Public aquarium").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-compagno-6)

[![Image 13: A fishery worker standing on a ship, holding a small shark in his gloved hands](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7a/Carcharhinus_obscurus_noaa.jpg/330px-Carcharhinus_obscurus_noaa.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Carcharhinus_obscurus_noaa.jpg)

Dusky sharks are highly valued by commercial fisheries.

The dusky shark is one of the most sought-after species for shark fin trade, as its fins are large and contain a high number of internal rays (ceratotrichia).[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-fowler_et_al-58) In addition, the meat is sold fresh, frozen, dried and salted, or smoked, the skin is made into leather, and the liver oil is processed for [vitamins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin "Vitamin").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-fishbase-8) Dusky sharks are taken by targeted commercial fisheries operating off eastern North America, southwestern Australia, and eastern South Africa using multi-species longlines and gillnets. The southwestern Australian fishery began in the 1940s and expanded in the 1970s to yield 500–600 tons per year. The fishery utilizes selective demersal gillnets that take almost exclusively young sharks under three years old, with 18–28% of all newborns captured in their first year. Demographic models suggest that the fishery is unsustainable, provided that the mortality rate of sharks under 2 m (6.6 ft) long is over 4%.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-fowler_et_al-58)

In addition to commercial shark fisheries, dusky sharks are also caught as [bycatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bycatch "Bycatch") on longlines meant for [tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna "Tuna") and [swordfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swordfish "Swordfish") (and usually kept for its valuable fins), and by recreational fishers. Large numbers of dusky sharks, mostly juveniles, are caught by sport fishers off South Africa and eastern Australia. This shark was once one of the most important species in the Florida trophy shark tournaments, before the population collapsed.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-fowler_et_al-58)

The [International Union for Conservation of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_for_Conservation_of_Nature "International Union for Conservation of Nature") (IUCN) has assessed this species as Endangered worldwide. The American Fisheries Society has assessed North American dusky shark populations as Vulnerable.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-knickle-22) Its very low reproductive rate renders the dusky shark extremely susceptible to [overfishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfishing "Overfishing").

Stocks off the eastern United States are severely overfished; a 2006 stock assessment survey by the U.S. National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS) showed that its population had dropped to 15–20% of 1970s levels. In 1997, the dusky shark was identified as a [Species of Concern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_of_Concern "Species of Concern") by the NMFS, meaning that it warranted conservation concern but there was insufficient information for listing on the U.S. [Endangered Species Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered_Species_Act "Endangered Species Act") (ESA).[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-64) Commercial and recreational retention of dusky sharks was prohibited in 1998, but this has been of limited effectiveness due to high bycatch mortality on multi-species gear. In addition, some 2,000 dusky sharks were caught by recreational fishers in 2003 despite the ban. In 2005, North Carolina implemented a time/area closure to reduce the impact of recreational fishing.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-soc-65) To aid conservation efforts, molecular techniques using [polymerase chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerase_chain_reaction "Polymerase chain reaction") (PCR) have been developed that can identify whether marketed shark parts (e.g. fins) are from prohibited species like the dusky shark, versus similar allowed species such as the sandbar shark.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-66)

The New Zealand [Department of Conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Conservation_(New_Zealand) "Department of Conservation (New Zealand)") has classified the dusky shark as "Migrant" with the qualifier "Secure Overseas" under the [New Zealand Threat Classification System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Threat_Classification_System "New Zealand Threat Classification System").[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_shark#cite_note-67)
