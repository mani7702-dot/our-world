Why the sky looks blue

18 October 2026 4 min read
Reading options

Sunlight is white — so why is the daytime sky blue and the sunset red? A simple answer kids can repeat at dinner.

Sunlight looks white, but it is actually every colour at once. You can see this for yourself after rain: raindrops split white sunlight into a rainbow.

The air plays a trick on the light

When sunlight travels through the sky it bumps into tiny bits of air. Every colour of light is a wave, and the waves are not the same size.

Blue light has short, choppy waves. When those waves hit the air, they bounce off in every direction — so blue light gets scattered all over the whole sky. Red light has long, lazy waves that pass through almost straight.

So why does the sky look blue?

Because blue light is bouncing around up there, and some of it comes down into your eyes from every part of the sky. The sky is not blue because it is painted blue — it is blue because blue light is being thrown around in it.

Why sunsets turn red

In the evening, sunlight has to travel through much more air to reach your eyes. All that extra air scatters the blue light away long before it arrives, so what is left by the time the light reaches you is the red end of the rainbow. That is why a sunset looks like a slow-motion version of the sky's trick.

Try this at home: shine a torch through a glass of water with a few drops of milk in it. Look from the side — the water looks faintly blue. Look from the far end — it looks orange, just like a sunset.

For grown-ups

The short version for curious grown-ups: this is Rayleigh scattering. Air molecules scatter light with an intensity that falls off as the fourth power of the wavelength, so blue light around 450 nm scatters roughly five times more strongly than red light around 650 nm. That scattered blue reaches our eyes from every direction, which is why the whole dome of the sky looks blue. At sunset the light travels through much more atmosphere, the blue is scattered out along the way, and the reds and oranges survive the trip.
