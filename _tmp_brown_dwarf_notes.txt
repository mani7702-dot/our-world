Astronomy Stars 'They're kind of goth': NASA spacecraft gives mysterious brown dwarfs a close-up News By Robert Lea Published 19 October 2026

Key facts from Space.com article:
- NASA's SPHEREx spacecraft studied "failed stars" (brown dwarfs)
- Brown dwarfs: mass between 13 and 80 times Jupiter (0.013 to 0.08 solar masses)
- They form like stars from collapsing gas/dust but fail to trigger hydrogen-helium fusion
- They are "cosmic loners" - free-floating, no parent star
- Quote: "They're kind of goth" - Zafar Rustamkulov, Caltech
- SPHEREx = Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization, and Ices Explorer
- From Earth orbit, SPHEREx sees wavelengths blocked by Earth's atmospheric water vapor
- Studied 37 nearby brown dwarfs across 102 wavelengths (red to infrared)
- Temperature range: 4,000°F to -10°F (2,200°C to -29°C). Sun is ~10,000°F (5,500°C)
- Atmospheres chemically rich in: water, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, methane
- Discovered in the 1990s
- Related: "astronomers discover the 1st ever green star, a cosmic zombie just in time for Halloween"
